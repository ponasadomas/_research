  • Miscarriage options
    01/16/2025
    Kellogg34572

    During my first pregnancy, I really enjoyed this app. It made the pregnancy super exciting getting to see how baby was changing. Unfortunately, that pregnancy ended in a miscarriage. I will say this app did provide articles and support for people going through this. Four months later we found out we were pregnant again! When I got on the app to track this pregnancy there wasn’t a way to “start over” from the beginning. There were still the articles about pregnancy loss but no option to begin a new pregnancy. I tried deselecting the pregnancy loss option but then it just took me to where my previous pregnancy would be if I was still pregnant- which was hard and emotional to see. I wish there was a way, while in “miscarriage mode” that you could just select an “I’m pregnant” option and it starts like a new pregnancy again instead of having to see all the reminders of where I would have been in my lost pregnancy. I ended up deleting my whole account and just starting a new one- because I really do enjoy it. But just a suggestion for possible grieving mothers.

    Developer Response

    Thank you for your feedback. We understand that adding a new pregnancy after experiencing a miscarriage can be sensitive. As our app doesn't support tracking of multiple pregnancies, in this case you can either delete the data and use the same account, or create a new account, as you have done. If you have any questions or need further assistance, please feel free to contact us. You can write us from your Profile (top right) > Contact us. Wishing you all the best.

  • Needs updated verbiage around breastfeeding articles
    11/28/2020
    Genshinimpactyaya

    I love this app for its general informational purposes and tracking purposes, and I wish I could give it five stars. However, there’s a very evident push to persuade mothers to breastfeed, with one article even explicitly stating: “Breastfeeding is still the best.” This type of explicit pressuring is incredibly insensitive to the mothers for whom breastfeeding isn’t an option, and disregards very recent research that suggests that, while there may be some genuine medical and emotional benefits to breast feeding, a generous portion of the difference in health and behavioral metrics for breastfed versus formula fed babies are actually due to socioeconomic conditions that would better account for the purported benefits of breastfeeding- socioeconomic factors that were never controlled for in most of the most widely cited articles by breastfeeding advocates. Even as a breastfeeding mother, I find, as do many medical professionals in the field, that taking a hardline stance is irresponsible at best. Mothers deserve a full breadth of education on both choices in order to make their own decisions.With that aside, I’m not saying that the apps authors shouldn’t present the benefits of breastfeeding to upcoming mothers. I’m just encouraging the authors to reconsider verbiage that may be biased, insensitive and potentially misleading, as in the example above, and rather that they would take an impartial stance in presenting the pros and cons of both choices. The one thing evidence is clear on for the well-being of the mother and the baby: either choice may be a healthier fit for one mother, and not the other. Some articles within the app do better at achieving this balance. Others fall short.

    Developer Response

    We appreciate your detailed feedback about the breastfeeding content in the App. We will share it with our content development team as they plan for future update. Thanks to comments from customers like you we can continue to improve the App.
