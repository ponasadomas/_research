# Reader notes — PR01 (keep_purchase 001–008)

165 units read, 1 excluded, 246 quotes (pb01-0001 → pb01-0246), verify 0 failed. Counts are of quotes I chose, not of people. Interpretation below; check against the quotes.

## Patterns

1. **The doula is the purchase that goes wrong most loudly.** A $1,500 birth doula who texted suggestions during active labour and never came (pb01-0133–0141); a Medicaid-programme doula who stopped answering (0092–0094); an insurance-paid doula who missed the birth and went silent for two weeks "to give us space to bond" (0148, 0149); three doulas interviewed, each shaming a medically advised c-section, formula or nappy brand (0052–0054); an overnight postpartum doula at $400+/night regretted, contract paid in full up front (0087–0091); a night doula not up to date on vaccines with a cancellation clause that costs thousands (0217–0220); a postpartum contract asking 100% before birth (0007). Only one unqualified praise post (0100). The thing bought is *someone who will speak for me when I can't* (0004, 0005, 0135, 0140) and, for women without family, *a mother figure* (0011).
2. **US cost of birth is a live fear and a source of practical tricks.** Shock bills: $70k birth + $90k NICU week (0032, 0033), $5k for a movement-check visit through the ER door at 39 weeks (0234, 0235), $341/$432 first-trimester scans billed as diagnostic on a "100% covered" plan (0144, 0145), a $1,300 scan estimate (0163), OB offices demanding monthly prepayment of the delivery fee (0046–0048, 0236, 0237), overpayment found only by reading EOBs (0123, 0124), a husband's layoff two weeks before birth (0034–0036). What worked: asking for an itemised bill and financial assistance (0035), a 60-second hardship call cutting a NIPT bill from $759 to $99 (0180), feeding the mailed benefits list to an AI to find covered classes and doula sessions (0118). "Why does the OB office charge for labor and delivery?" (0048) — people don't know who bills.
3. **UK: the complaint is no conversation, not no care.** "they never talk about labour" (0077–0079), cancelled and refused appointments costing £80 Ubers (0221–0224), PALS ignored. Private routes weighed: Kensington Wing / Lindo / Portland for elective sections (0069, 0117), private physiological birth with continuity (0185–0187, a partner writing), private NIPT £240 (0231). NCT "preachy" about vaginal birth (0003). A John Lewis "expert" vs the antenatal teacher on sterilisers (0171–0173).
4. **Overload asks for one trustworthy list.** "50 tabs" (0037), "drowning in purchasing research" (0096), "a never ending list" (0182), a prenatal-vitamin "new personality" (0150, 0151), baby books that tell you about the baby "not about surviving after and what to buy" (0061), "I wish someone made a simple page" (0081), a registry list wanted by someone too overwhelmed to open her app (0239, 0240), mixed messages on *when* to buy (0204, 0205, 0115). Direct evidence for a guide that decides *what and when*, by week.
5. **Ad suspicion is explicit.** Babybub praise looks scripted (0043); Yana "bamboo" is 30% polyester (0063, 0064); Emme Mama "AGGRESIVELY advertised" then 0/10 (0157); Garbha Sanskar ads since the positive test (0183); "my Instagram has been hijacked by all these ads about courses" yet "nothing solid" (0206); What to Expect spam vs Ovia "don't try to sell you things" (0108); "how much garbage women are sold in preparation for 'big days'" (0142). Any Mamivio ad meets this guard.
6. **Second/third pregnancy has its own buying logic.** Previous birth sets the brief: the Lila gown after a hard first labour, ceiling £53 not £90 (0013–0017), a VBAC choice between a birth course + private OB or a doula, "I can't afford both", with a toddler (0071, 0072); hand-me-downs vs something new (0044); "bigger at 13w than 20w last time" (0066); the toddler logistics — buggy board ≤£80 (0084, 0085), double stroller (0161), car (0164), third stroller (0167); "fell for all the things being marketed" after an 8-year gap (0192–0194); registry fatigue (0246); an older sibling who sings to the bump each Saturday when the app flips week (0155).
7. **Partners write as buyers and as gap.** Husbands paying or querying (0001, 0007, 0051, 0063, 0185–0187, 0245); "he feels like it's his day to lead me through birth" but may get no leave (0010); a home birth paid from savings, then he can take only the delivery night off (0120–0122); a husband who "feels like he let me down" when the doula didn't come (0138); watching newborn videos together at 35 weeks (0176).
8. **First two weeks.** Food plan and meal train (0021), adult diapers and compression socks (0018), add baby to insurance early (0020), visitors who did chores helped — contrary to "no visitors" (0023), a mum's toilet basket (0210), "my first 2 weeks were a blur" (0211), the post-section grabber (0190), first nights "just us" before night help (0127), a $3K/month postpartum doula vs in-laws (0024–0026).
9. **Scepticism about preparation.** Perineal massage with an expensive tool, "about to give up and just let it tear" (0212–0214); breastfeeding reading "flipped on its head" (0022); a newborn book binned (0200, 0201); doula "unnecessary expense?" (0075, 0076); NHS checks "useless" (0222); "the baby doesn't know what it doesn't know" (0194).

## Contrary evidence

Visitors helped (0023). A doula "100% worth the money" (0100). One woman plans an epidural *and* wants hypnobirthing (0229) — preparation is not only for unmedicated birth. "Pricing is not really a topic for me" for a fitness app (0208). Ovia praised for not selling (0108). Course purchases themselves are scarce in these 165 purchase posts: classes are shopped for (0003, 0050, 0206, 0229) but rarely reviewed after.
