# Reader notes — PR50 (wave 3, posts 10 April – 20 May 2026)

Chunks read-w3-keep_otherside-004 … -017: 153 units, all read in full; 20 excluded; 236 quotes (pb50-0001 … 0238; 0227 and 0228 withdrawn when their unit was excluded), 15 restricted. Ledger: `sources/reddit-reading-pr50.md`. Interpretation is mine; every claim points at quote ids. Counts are counts of quotes I chose, never of people.

**What this slice is.** These are "other side" units: family, partners, visitors, in-laws. They are not the birth or symptom threads. 109 quotes come from expecting mothers, 55 from partners, 34 from new mothers, 27 from experienced mothers, 9 from relatives or friends and 2 from doulas. Themes are therefore heavy on `partner_role` (76), `visitors` (63), `mother_in_law` (46), `own_mother` (37) and `family_pressure` (37). Work and leave appear mainly through the partner's leave. Birth fear, induction, epidural and NHS caesarean pushback hardly appear (see "Asked to test").

## 1. Patterns, with quote ids

**1. The visit plan is set in pregnancy and keyed to the due date and to his leave. This confirms 16.1 "For 7" and the "one dated plan" claim, with new forms.**
- Grandparents book flights against the due date: "Both sets are planning on coming the weekend of July 5. If she’s born after her due date, she might not even be 4 weeks by then." [pb50-0007]; in-laws "decided anyways to come 2 weeks after your due date and we booked tickets" [pb50-0152], after agreeing to about a month [pb50-0153]; a mother-in-law who "selected the dates" for an 8–9 day stay [pb50-0202]; family told to "save their pto for September" [pb50-0222]; a mother-in-law driving over unannounced at 40+4 [pb50-0225] when the staggered plan had been agreed months earlier [pb50-0224].
- The plan hangs on his return-to-work date: "I suggested MIL come when I return to work. My logic is that this "staggers" the help" [pb50-0036], with his "5 weeks off (paternity+vacation)" and no money for a hotel [pb50-0037]; a mother-in-law who will "come over to help most days" "once my husband goes back to work" [pb50-0196]; "My husband is taking a whole 6 weeks off … we are going to be just fine" [pb50-0187]; a mother-in-law who will retire "once my husband parental leave finish" to take over childcare [pb50-0180].
- New tactic: "next pregnancy I’ll say the due date is 2-3 weeks after the actual one to them" [pb50-0154]; keeping labour secret [pb50-0236]; "Also not telling anyone about my labour" (PregnancyUK-1tb1rt7, not quoted).
- Lodging and travel are part of the plan, and they cost money: "We cannot afford a hotel or Airbnb" [pb50-0037]; a mother's Airbnb booked months ahead [pb50-0226]; a father's plan to rent an Airbnb for a month as "personal chef", followed by the mother for another month [pb50-0186]; a $400-a-person flight to a shower against PTO "for the baby" [pb50-0217].
- Interpretation: in these units the visit plan, his leave, lodging and the due date really are one dated plan. **Work and leave from her side hardly come into it** (see Contrary).

**2. Helpers versus guests is the strongest thread in this slice. It confirms 16.1 and sharpens it.** "She was meant to help us during the newborn phase, but has been mainly critical of me" [pb50-0016]; "Since she got here she has taken over all the support he was giving me" [pb50-0017]; "acted like a guest and expected to be entertained" [pb50-0015]; "keep asking “so what are we doing next?”" at 16 days [pb50-0091]; "I’m a guest in my own house" [pb50-0219]; she offers "to take baby upstairs instead of offer to finish vacuuming" [pb50-0221]; "we'll come over and help." "And the they come over and do *less* than nothing" [pb50-0142, pb50-0143]; a trial visit before the birth that "does not go well" [pb50-0190] and cost repairs [pb50-0191]; "grandparents today relish in the title and do not know what help looks like" [pb50-0204]. What worked was concrete: "My mom came the week after baby was born and did our laundry and cooked and cleaned" [pb50-0093]; acquaintances who "dropped off food, snacks, loaded our dishwasher and left" [pb50-0145]; "walk the dogs or make groceries" [pb50-0110]. Interpretation: a helper brief (jobs, not holding the baby) is the content these quotes ask for.

**3. Her own mother is as much of a pressure as the mother-in-law. Confirmed (16.1).** 37 `own_mother` quotes against 46 `mother_in_law`: "we've all been pregnant before. ## 3. Asked to test

- **The "one dated plan": supported** for visit dates + his leave + lodging + due date [pb50-0007, 0036, 0037, 0152, 0180, 0187, 0196, 0222, 0226]. **Not seen here:** US prepayment deadlines, short-term-disability waits, HR confusion. This slice is family-side and does not cover those.
- **Cheaper or real-mum alternatives to Instagram birth courses:** not seen as such. The nearest is the dad-content complaint (too expensive or surface-level) [pb50-0230].
- **NHS elective-caesarean pushback, induction pressure over scan estimates, fear of the epidural itself:** not seen. Epidural appears only as a husband pushing *for* one [pb50-0099] (restricted) and as a hospital class shaping his view [pb50-0100] (restricted).
- **Country notes:** Japan postpartum hotels and no paternity leave [pb50-0039, 0040]; UK equal-care stock lines [pb50-0164]; a South Asian or Arab family's 40-day custom [pb50-0131]; an Indian family's expectation of birth at the mother's home [pb50-0234]; US Medicaid admission confusion [pb50-0209]; "being pregnant in America, medical bills" [pb50-0045].
