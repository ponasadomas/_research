## Patterns

1. **Birth preparation is wanted, but its pricing and marketing put people off.** A second-time mother wants a self-paced course under $100 instead of a $350 five-evening hypnobirthing class, and calls the marketing predatory ("I have the secret to a pain-free birth, but only if you pay me…") and asks "why is everyone trying to monetize birth" [pb02-0016, 0017, 0020, 0021]. What she wants from a cheap option is *structure and a practice plan* [pb02-0022]. A doula buying training sees the same split: live and expensive vs self-paced and cheap [pb02-0105]. Paid pelvic-floor classes are doubted as "stretches I can do at home" [pb02-0047]; free app users notice the ads [pb02-0059, 0060]; first-timers ask which classes are "worth the time or money" [pb02-0094].
2. **Preparation that met reality.** The richest seam in the success chunks. A hypnobirthing course and physiological-birth books left one overdue woman under "pressure to relax" [pb02-0042, 0043, 0044]. A European woman who did yoga, perineal massage and hypnobirthing found her only hospital allows one protocol; her "trust your body" mantra "feels like it is not valid anymore" [pb02-0106–0110]. Looking back, a first-time mother says her research was "my way of feeling like I had some degree of control" [pb02-0204, 0205, 0208]; another, after a long labour with interventions, credits asking questions and delaying decisions [pb02-0243, 0250]. "Literally nothing went to plan" [pb02-0255]. Half-done prep the first time is blamed for an exhausting first labour [pb02-0018].
3. **What women credit after a good birth** is mostly not a course: nurses and midwives [pb02-0182, 0203], the partner [pb02-0189, 0223, 0233], practised breathing/groaning [pb02-0180], small tools (birth comb, TENS) [pb02-0234, 0247, 0251], a book that built confidence to advocate [pb02-0244], and reading positive stories [pb02-0216, 0221, 0227, 0253].
4. **Positive birth stories are a sought-after product in themselves.** Pregnant women ask for them [pb02-0044]; new mothers say they were "desperate" for them and post their own as payback [pb02-0163, 0185, 0191, 0226, 0256]. Advice repeated: avoid horror stories [pb02-0227].
5. **Doulas: high price, mixed delivery.** $2,400 paid from overtime, no-show for an induction, refund refused, "I used to be 100% pro doula but now I'm just not" [pb02-0087–0093, restricted]. A doula missed a fast birth sitting in the car park [pb02-0217]. Against that: "They are 100% worth the money" [pb02-0236]; a $3,000 package weighed on a coworker's and OB's word [pb02-0155, 0156]; employer covering 70% still doesn't settle the doubt [pb02-0147, 0148]; a London mother interviewed doulas and decided "it's not for me" [pb02-0102].
6. **UK: paying to escape the NHS postnatal ward.** Private postnatal room £150/night [pb02-0070], private wing suite c.£1,050 [pb02-0012], a £20k private delivery weighed against nursery fees [pb02-0100, 0103], all driven by ward stories [pb02-0011, 0101, 0176, 0177] and partners' fears [pb02-0010].
