## Patterns

1. **"Nobody told me" / "this isn't what I read".** First-trimester sickness hits women who expected a glow: "NOT FOR ME. Why did no one tell me" (pb08-0011, 0012), "morning sickness to be actual all day sickness" (0257), "not expecting this to hit me so hard" (0089). The week-by-week script breaks in both directions: a worse second trimester when "Everything I've read says" it gets better (0097, 0098); 16 weeks with no glow, "this is it?" (0055–0057); symptoms that replace each other (0002, 0004); one woman with better energy than before pregnancy (0249). Social media set a wrong expectation for the due date (0258). A 6-week woman asks outright: "Please give me a guide" (0141).
2. **Searching on her own, then needing one person to explain.** Google and Reddit at night (0016, 0085, 0121, 0151); a clinic says "I shouldnt be googling results" to a woman who googled only because nobody explained (0069, 0070); a first-timer who found out at 25 weeks never went to a class and has "no idea what's normal" (0201); no mum friends to say what to look out for (0075); a woman too scared to read about epidurals who wants one fact, not the whole explanation (0124, 0125); swimming advice with no routine and "everything I find online is so overwhelming" (0158); conflicting food rules, "what constitutes a reputable source??" (0231). Taken together, a short, trusted, stage-specific source answers a stated need. That is my reading; none of these women asks to buy one.
3. **Pregnancy with a toddler is its own scene.** Guilt about the toddler while too sick to play (0001, 0215); a toddler who wants only her when she can barely stand (0077); 11 months + first trimester (0017); 12 months apart (0214); four back-to-back pregnancies and coworkers' comments (0051, 0052); the toddler's bedtime falling apart at 36 weeks (0176, 0177) or naps dropped at 28 weeks (0253); daycare bugs in early pregnancy (0238); preschool starting the week of the birth plus a likely section (0107, 0108); "easier having the baby and the toddler vs being pregnant with a toddler" (0079); with a first pregnancy, napping whenever she liked (0078). Third baby: "people don't seem to really care" (0245). One mother stopped working and "it made the world of difference" (0254).
4. **Visitors and in-laws, before and after.** Planned in pregnancy: visit rules drafted at 11 weeks (0034), a frail mother-in-law offering two months (0190–0193), a brother's two-week Christmas visit in the newborn weeks (0067, 0068), a mother who comes "to be hosted" (0211, 0212), late-pregnancy family events (0048–0050, 0142). After birth: hospital visitors regretted (0178), pressure to visit while she can barely walk (0179), in-laws who booked a 5-day hotel over the agreed dates and brought no food (0216–0218), "next time … they'll meet baby when everyone else does" (0219), feeding advice from relatives and a sister-in-law's TikToks (0183, 0184), in-laws cleaning her house (0185). What worked: her own mother's first-week stay without advice (0186), an aunt-in-law teaching the pump (0023).
5. **Partners: the gap and the good ones.** Most partner quotes describe a gap: no help with chores (0129, 0130, 0225, 0226), "two adults in the house, but only one actual parent" (0146), the last paternity leave spent gaming (0247), a partner who walked out at five months (0260, 0261), a boyfriend who comes only to scans (0154), "just wait until I get home" (0026), a bassinet-assembly fight before his leave (0188, 0189), "don't you get a baby for pushing" (0137). Some are good: a husband who reads pregnancy books and packs the hospital bag (0127, 0128), the €900 flute (0139, 0140), a husband with 4 months' leave planning night shifts (0109–0112).
6. **Leave, work and money (US, UK, Canada).** US: $1,500 owed after two prenatal visits, an $8,000 deductible on $34,000 a year, weighing no prenatal care (0042–0047); an HR error that left her without short-term disability, "lucky if I can afford to take 4-6 weeks off" (0116, 0117); no sick time (0255); a lactation consultant not covered (0020); early leave refused (0095). UK: working to 40 weeks to bank annual leave (0174); referrals refused, private physio weighed on maternity pay (0164, 0165); "useless" health visitors (0166). Canada: 12/18 months of leave but going back early to keep the car (0156). Leaving a career for SAHM brings guilt (0096). Paid help is weighed against family help (0033, 0149, 0192).
7. **Fears about birth are specific.** Needles, not pain (0126); the epidural/tearing/induction tangle and "the least room for error and unpredictability" (0080, 0081); grieving a vaginal birth at 37 weeks breech (0232); the fear of dying in a second birth after a traumatic first (0235, 0236); scared at 40 weeks after turning down an induction (0072); fear of fetal movement as "loss of control over my body", which turned out "not as weird as I feared" (0181, 0182); breastfeeding sensation and no woman in the family who breastfed (0221–0223); in UK units, induction delays and mixed messages (0195–0199) and nerves about calling triage (0234). Preparation appears as control: "decision trees" because "making a plan is a fool's errand" (0109), prepped early "because I'm insane" (0173), "should I wash my hair?" (0229).
8. **What others say to her.** "it's gonna get worse" (0093); "just wait it only gets worse haha", together with blocking videos of miserable new mums (0206); "you'll forget all about those days", which she didn't (0087); a stretch-mark comparison from her mother (0115); the midwife's "just eat healthy" (0205); a mother-in-law's "too expensive" for the shower venue (0031) and "how's MY baby?!" (0252); coworkers betting on morning sickness (0105); a whole list of the same questions answered at once (0169–0171).
9. **The first two weeks.** Sleep from 0 to 3 hours after the induction (0039); eat, sleep or shower, pick one (0021); a meltdown at being left alone with the baby (0022); doing it alone after a section once parents leave (0118); body image at four weeks (0122, 0123); a first solo outing to the health visitor that left her feeling she was "failing" (0208–0210); family taking over the baby (0167, 0168).

## Contrary evidence

Second-trimester energy better than before pregnancy (0249). Fear of fetal movement faded (0182). Her own mother's first-week help was "a great help" (0186), and so was an aunt-in-law (0023). Supportive husbands (0127, 0128, 0139, 0140). Stopping work fixed her patience (0254). A home-birth story told as a success, with an audiobook that "brought me so much peace" (0085–0088); treat it as one story, never as an outcome claim. Fully prepared by 32 weeks and just wanting comfort (0172, 0173). Coworkers' excitement standing in for family (0105).
