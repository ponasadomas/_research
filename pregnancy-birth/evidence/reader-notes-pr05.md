## Patterns

1. **Everyone tells her how to do it, and it lands as losing control.** A husband asks a female friend for tips and is told to do hypnobirthing: "I have beef with people telling me how I should be giving birth" [pb05-0004–0007]. At 39 weeks relatives ask her to "keep her in there" to fit their schedules; her husband is "the pregnancy police" [pb05-0074–0078]. A mother-in-law who says "I feel like I'm the one that's pregnant", withdraws the crib and runs her own "MOM's FAVORITE THINGS" registry [pb05-0149–0154]. A mother at 6 weeks plans to announce the pregnancy herself: "you're not that special, everyone gets pregnant" [pb05-0017, 0018]. A parent says she has to stop going to the gym [pb05-0167]. Unsolicited "it's hard, it's expensive" [pb05-0038, 0039]. A UK first-trimester post lists "who to tell, who not to tell" as preparation [pb05-0110].
2. **Control is the word they use about birth plans.** A moved c-section date: "I think it's the lack of control" [pb05-0050–0052]; two hours each way for a water-birth centre, then an induction date: "this all would have been for nothing" [pb05-0098, 0099]; "I am a control freak" about baby-shower house guests [pb05-0166]; a husband who wants a c-section to make "a bit more of a controllable situation" [pb05-0208, 0209]; "it's out of my control when she comes" [pb05-0075]. Restricted ones among these are feelings as told, never hooks.
3. **The partner gap is the loudest theme in these chunks** (47 quotes tagged partner_role). Husbands writing: choosing between leave and the job [pb05-0001–0003], a second baby in a small home [pb05-0008], a commute, daycare at 3 months [pb05-0020], "I just need to know what she needs from me" [pb05-0022], a wife "downplaying how hard recovery could be" [pb05-0019], a father of a five-week-old dreading his wife's morning shift [pb05-0011–0015]. Women writing: sardines left out after she cried about smells [pb05-0106–0108], "we'll just see how it is at the time" about visitors [pb05-0056], a boyfriend who helps only when told [pb05-0212], a husband who gets 16 weeks paid plus 12 state weeks against her 12 [pb05-0141, 0142].
4. **"He won't read."** A 38-week first-timer, 12 days from induction, has done one Baby Academy class, YouTube and the Happiest Baby, and "we know nothing" [pb05-0124, restricted]. Her husband "won't read, that's just not happening" and she hopes for "a podcast or audiobook version" [pb05-0125, 0127, restricted]. They could pay for a doula, night nurse or nanny, but "that requires me doing the research, interviewing and hiring and I'm tired" [pb05-0126, restricted]. Her real question was counter-pressure and tearing pain [pb05-0129, restricted], not the marriage advice she got. That is a direct brief for a partner section in a non-reading format, and for a guide that does the deciding for her.
5. **Generic timelines feel wrong in her body.** "I keep reading that pregnancy nausea often peaks around weeks 8–10 … PEAKS?!" [pb05-0069, restricted]; "I definitely try not to hold out on dates when it 'should' get better" [pb05-0045]; the "golden trimester" myth from friends and posts [pb05-0176, restricted]; "I knew the first trimester would be hard but I just wasn't prepared" [pb05-0121, restricted]; "how I'll make it 11 more weeks" [pb05-0140, restricted]. Week-by-week facts either frighten or miss. That is a personalisation signal, but a careful one: they want *their* week to be named, not a promise of when it ends.
6. **Second and third pregnancy: the last one is the yardstick.** "I should know to be doing better because it's my third pregnancy" [pb05-0080]; "after two times he hasn't figured it out yet" [pb05-0107]; "my first pregnancy was a breathe … with my second … should have been warned" [pb05-0171]; worse than the first while working on her feet [pb05-0143]; forgot what she packed or used last time [pb05-0026–0028]; second-baby registry after losing her gear [pb05-0029–0033]; a nicer previous hospital "I didn't even realize how fortunate that was" [pb05-0119]; fear of a second pregnancy because of the first [pb05-0040–0042, 0082, 0169, restricted]. **With a toddler**: nausea while busy with a 12-month-old [pb05-0043, 0044], "a newborn and a 2.3 year old" [pb05-0083], can't bear her 3-year-old's touch [pb05-0102, 0103], due on her son's first birthday [pb05-0104], "Having a toddler now makes a lot more logistics" [pb05-0117], husband on nights, alone with the toddler [pb05-0112–0114], "how we are supposed to manage with 3" [pb05-0081].
7. **Visitors and the first weeks are feared from mid-pregnancy.** At 29 weeks: "overwhelming dread at the anticipation of being postpartum, mostly around visitors"; a mother who'll "want to watch me breastfeed"; a mother-in-law with her own key; a plan to leave the key in the lock [pb05-0053–0058]. Two months after a hard birth people still come daily: "Everyone thinks I'm good now" [pb05-0088–0090]. A dad's 75-year-old mother's visit is "just more stress" [pb05-0013]. Contrary: a mother who stayed a week and held the baby at night "really saved me" [pb05-0009].
8. **UK NHS: logistics and no continuity, and private care as escape.** A local maternity unit closed, now two hours away; staying at a relative's flat from 39 weeks [pb05-0132–0134]. A London hospital: phones not answered, a different midwife each time, private scans "enormous" difference, a loan for a private birth considered [pb05-0190–0195]. A moved c-section slot [pb05-0050]. Two consultants with different views [pb05-0211]. A shift-change midwife saying goodbye at the pushing stage, then a birth debrief [pb05-0182, 0185]. A long complaint about the postnatal ward: no food, "Just give him a bottle", "You should just want your baby to be okay", "if I had been fed and medicated, I would have wanted to stay" [pb05-0196–0205]. Assessment-unit visits with no answer, from a husband [pb05-0016].
9. **Work and leave.** US: FMLA rules confuse and scare [pb05-0002, 0122, 0179, 0180]; a doctor won't sign leave at 38 weeks [pb05-0095–0097]; nurses and servers working through sickness [pb05-0047, 0048, 0155, 0156]. 10. **Isolation, and Reddit as the only place to ask.** "none of my friends have been through having a baby yet" [pb05-0063]; "I haven't been able to make a ton of close friends" [pb05-0094]; "No one I know has ever dealt with what I'm feeling and I haven't found much about it online" [pb05-0103]; not telling anyone before 12 weeks so "I could at least ask on here" [pb05-0123]; no family nearby and a husband working 12–15 hours [pb05-0066].
11. **First-trimester sickness is the trigger in early-stage posts**: constant nausea, "Basically if the internet has suggested it … I have it somewhere near me" [pb05-0067–0071, restricted], "I can't believe how rough I feel" [pb05-0120–0122], "I'm still shocked at how much it takes out of me" [pb05-0170–0172], "Scrambled egg that needs a nap" [pb05-0163, restricted]. Quote as experience only, never advice.
