# Product brief — The mother's own sleep

Written 2026-10-25 from the niche title, the owner's brief (`../../../brief.md`) and the 2026-10-25 Ad Library sweep (`../../main/ads-running/`). **There is no product yet; every line below is an assumption the research must test.**

- **Who suffers (assumption):** Pregnant and postpartum mothers whose own sleep is the problem: pregnancy insomnia, can't fall back asleep after a feed, lying awake listening for the baby, cumulative deprivation, unequal night duty with the partner, returning to work exhausted.
- **What they already buy (from the Ad Library sweep and `../../global/competitors/`):** Unknown. Candidates to test: CBT-I apps (Sleepio, Stellar Sleep), meditation apps, postpartum doulas and night nannies, sleep supplements, pregnancy pillows. No info-product seller to this need was found in the 2026-10-25 Ad Library sweep.
- **The candidate Mamivio product (assumption):** Quiz on the mother's situation (pregnancy week or baby's age, feeding method, who does nights, what keeps her awake) → a personalized plan for her own sleep and the household's night split. Price ladder already used by Mamivio: $17.95 / $29.95 / $49.95 for 1 / 3 / 6 months.
- **Research principle (owner, 2026-11-16):** Research records what people actually say and do, in their own words, whatever the subject; nothing is withheld, excluded, softened or paraphrased away because of its subject. Readers may label material, but every quote remains ordinary evidence and is quoted like any other. What is usable in copy is the owner's decision, made later; it is never a research decision.

## Questions the research must answer

1. Which moment makes them search and pay, in their own words?
2. What have they already bought or tried, and how did it land? Who quit, and why?
3. Does personalization by age or situation matter to them, or is generic advice enough?
4. What do they say a solution is worth? What do they call a rip-off?
5. Who else is in the decision (partner, grandparents, daycare)?
