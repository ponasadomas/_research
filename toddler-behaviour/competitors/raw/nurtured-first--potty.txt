## What You'll Learn

### Know When (and How) to Start

Read your child's readiness. Use a clear checklist to tell whether your child's body and brain are ready, so you start at the moment that makes this faster, not harder.

Work with your child's temperament. Find out whether you have an easygoing, sensitive, or strong-willed child, and adjust your approach so you stop fighting their wiring.

Build the runway first. Lay low-pressure groundwork, even as young as 20 months, that makes the real thing click sooner instead of springing it on them cold.

### Stay Calm and Lead the Way

Keep your cool in the messy moments. Learn what to do with your own frustration when there is poop on the floor again, so you stay the calm leader your child is taking their cues from.

Respond to accidents without shame. Get a simple, neutral script that treats an accident as information, not a failure, so it does not spiral into a power struggle.

Trade pressure for connection. Swap the rewards, the bribes, and "you're a big kid now" for the playful, connected approach that actually lowers resistance.

### Handle Every Stage and Setback

Move through the five goals. Follow a step-by-step plan from first successes to underwear, outings, and dry nights, advancing on your child's progress instead of a three-day clock.

Solve the poop standoffs. Get specific help for the child who pees but won't poop, who hides, who holds it, or who is caught in the constipation-fear cycle.

Handle travel, daycare, and dry nights. Manage public bathrooms, long car rides, different caregivers, and the biology of staying dry overnight, without losing the progress you made.

## What makes this course different

### It follows your child, not a three-day clock.

Most potty programs sell you a deadline and one script that is supposed to fit every kid. This one moves at your child’s pace and starts only when they are genuinely ready, because a body that is pushed too soon digs in and the whole thing drags out longer.

### A therapist's read on the why, not just a hack.

This is built by a Registered Psychotherapist who shows you what is happening in your child’s body and brain at each stage. When you understand why the holding or the hiding is happening, you can respond to the real cause instead of fighting the symptom you saw in a thirty-second video.

### A troubleshooting library for when it gets hard.

Most potty advice stops at the happy path and goes quiet the moment things stall. This course hands you a short video for the exact roadblocks that derail families, from poop holding to public bathrooms, so a setback has an answer instead of turning into a crisis.

### It treats potty learning as learning, not training.

Accidents become information, not failures. There is no shaming, no calling it “yucky,” and no sticker chart to bribe your way through, because those tend to work for a week and then backfire into resistance.

### It works on you, too.

The hardest part of a mess on the floor is often what it stirs up in you. A whole part of this course is about keeping your own calm, so your child always has a steady leader to follow.

### It grows with your child and your real life.

Whether you are laying early groundwork with a young toddler or resetting a stalled attempt with a stubborn three-year-old, the approach meets you there. Come back to it for daycare, travel, and dry nights as they arrive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Who is this course for?

Parents and caregivers of toddlers and preschoolers who are about to start, in the middle of, or starting over with potty training, and who want a flexible plan that follows their child instead of a rigid three-day deadline.

How do I know if my child is ready?

There is no magic window, and starting too early often makes the whole thing take longer. The course gives you a readiness checklist covering body, brain, and emotional signs, so you can tell whether to begin now or spend a few more weeks on the low-pressure runway first. The runway itself can start as young as 20 months.

My last attempt was a disaster. Can I start over?

Yes, and starting over calmly often goes faster than grinding through a stalled attempt. The course shows you how to reset, step back to more support, and rebuild without the power struggles, which is where a lot of families find themselves when they come to this.

Why pay for this when there are potty-training books for $15 to 20, or free videos online?

Books and free videos can genuinely help, and plenty of parents start there. The difference is that a book hands you one timeline and a script to read, while this shows you how, with Jess walking you through the exact words and what to do when your child pushes back. You also get a plan that bends to your own child and a troubleshooting library for the roadblocks the quick guides skip. If a free tip has already solved it for your family, you may not need this. If you have tried the tips and every attempt still ends in a standoff, this is the part they leave out.

How is this different from The Potty Toolkit?

The Potty Toolkit is a printable you and your child work through together: stories, games, scripts, and posters you can hang by the potty. This course is the full method on video, where you watch Jess teach the readiness check, the five goals, and every common roadblock, with a resource vault included. Many parents use both, the course to understand the approach and the toolkit for hands-on practice with their child. If you are in the middle of a hard standoff and need to know what to do next, start with the course.

How long are the lessons, and how do I fit this in?

The course is built from short video lessons, so you can learn one idea during nap time and use it that afternoon. There is a quick-start path if you are stuck right now and need help today, and you can skip straight to the stage or roadblock in front of you instead of watching all 37 in order.

Can I go at my own pace?

Yes. The course is completely self-paced, with no scheduled sessions and no deadlines. Watch it in order, follow the quick-start path, or jump to the troubleshooting library for the exact problem taking over your house today.

What about pooping problems, like holding it or hiding?

These are some of the most common struggles, and the troubleshooting library covers them directly: the child who pees but won’t poop, who hides, who withholds, and who is caught in the constipation-fear cycle. One important note: if there is any pain, hard stools, or ongoing constipation, please see your child’s medical professional, because that is outside what a course can address.

Will this work for a sensitive or strong-willed child?

Yes, and it is built with them in mind. Sensitive and strong-willed children are often the ones a three-day approach backfires on. The temperament module helps you spot your child’s style and adjust, giving them the extra time, choice, and connection they need to feel safe learning this.

Does this replace professional support?

No. This is parenting education. It does not diagnose anything and it does not replace medical or psychological care, and it does not address chronic constipation, urinary issues, or developmental differences that can affect potty learning. If your child has pain, significant anxiety around the toilet, or you have concerns about their development, please work with a qualified professional.

What if it doesn't work for my family?
