# Product brief — Toddler behaviour and potty training, 1–4 years

Written 2026-10-25 from the niche title, the owner's brief (`../../../brief.md`) and the 2026-10-25 Ad Library sweep (`../../main/ads-running/`). **There is no product yet; every line below is an assumption the research must test.**

- **Who suffers (assumption):** Mothers of 1–4 year olds facing tantrums, hitting and biting, not listening, picky eating, transitions, a new sibling, and potty training.
- **What they already buy (from the Ad Library sweep and `../../global/competitors/`):** Video courses (Big Little Feelings: Potty Training Made Simple leads 20 of 30 top ads; Winning the Toddler Stage), Good Inside membership, potty products with a method (UpAiry 838 active ads), potty consultants, books (Oh Crap!, Good Inside, How to Talk So Little Kids Will Listen).
- **The candidate Mamivio product (assumption):** Quiz on the child's age, the behaviour, the household and what was tried → a personalized plan (potty: readiness, method, day-by-day; behaviour: scripts for the specific situations). Price ladder already used by Mamivio: $17.95 / $29.95 / $49.95 for 1 / 3 / 6 months.
- **Research principle (owner, 2026-11-16):** Research records what people actually say and do, in their words, whatever the subject; nothing is withheld, softened or excluded because of its subject. Readers may label a quote, but it remains ordinary evidence and is quoted like any other. What is usable in copy is the owner's decision, made later; it is never a research decision.

## Questions the research must answer

1. Which moment makes them search and pay, in their own words?
2. What have they already bought or tried, and how did it land? Who quit, and why?
3. Does personalization by age or situation matter to them, or is generic advice enough?
4. What do they say a solution is worth? What do they call a rip-off?
5. Who else is in the decision (partner, grandparents, daycare)?
