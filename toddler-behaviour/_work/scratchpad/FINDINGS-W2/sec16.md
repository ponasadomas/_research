**What this changes for the Mamivio plan ($17.95 / $29.95 / $49.95)** (interpretation, built on the ids above):

1. The case for personalisation is stronger, and it now names its inputs: the daycare's written rule and date, leave and due dates, floors, heat, siblings, work hours, and a partner who refuses mess or pauses.
2. The differentiator moves from tone to coverage. The hole parents describe is the step the book skips (when to pause, how to restart, how to move from naked to clothes) and a part-time, working-parent version, not the book's voice.
3. A daycare section must work both ways: meeting a rule by a date, and handling a room that slows training. It should give the parent words for the teacher.
4. Philosophy cannot be the product. Backing off and holding firm both produced successes (16.2, point 3), so the plan has to branch on what the child does next.
5. Price is not the objection the evidence shows. Some parents buy $40+ courses repeatedly and BLF's course; they object to sameness and to ads [tb17-0090] [tb16-0252]. The $100 refusal [tb11-0233] shows a ceiling for some households. The ladder sits at or under what the course buyer called generic, so it will be judged on whether her answers visibly change the plan.
6. The paid behaviour brands are near-invisible in parents' words in both waves. There is still no evidence that this audience pays for behaviour plans, only for potty courses.
7. Someone may already be testing "a plan for my exact situation" in Reddit threads (16.2, point 10). Watch for it in the Ad Library and on the web.

### 16.5 Method notes

- **The parent's hardest moments stay under-represented.** The wave-2 ledgers name 95 units excluded whole under a line not to cross (TR10 9, TR11 26, TR12 15, TR13 7, TR14 7, TR15 17, TR16 3 plus 3 duplicates, TR17 11). My keyword tally of the stated reasons (16.7, item 3) puts 67 of them under a parent's or caregiver's harshness or force toward a child: yelling, restraint, forcing onto the potty, punishment. In TR11 that is 18 of 138 units (26 excluded in all, about a fifth); in TR15, 12 of 137; in TR12, 10 of 134. Wave 1 had 58 of 86 (section 12). TR11 notes that its clearest backing-off success sits in an excluded unit. So `maternal_rage` (97 wave-2 quotes) is a floor, and rage acted out is absent by design in both waves.
- **The restricted list is conservative, but not uniformly.** For TR10, TR12 and TR14 the orchestrator flagged every quote from a unit centred on a diagnosis or development question, even where the reader judged the words clean (TR14 itself restricted 5 at quote level; the merged file flags 78 tb14 ids). For TR17's units listed as id ranges (first id … last id), only the first and last id of each range carry the flag, so 65 quotes inside those ranges are `restricted: false` although their units carry a diagnosis or medical topic. TR17 judged their words clean and wrote "do not build a hook from these units". This section cites ten of them and quotes eight, each only for words with no diagnosis or medical content: quoted tb17-0087, tb17-0088, tb17-0090, tb17-0091, tb17-0256, tb17-0358, tb17-0359 and tb17-0473; cited without words tb17-0194 and tb17-0294. The orchestrator should decide whether to flag the 65 (command in 16.7, item 4).
- **Seed tags and priming.** Wave-2 readers had seed tags built from wave-1 conclusions (`advice_ignores_household`, `only_with_me`, `backing_off`, `possible_promo`) and checked section 10 by heading. Tag counts are therefore not comparable across waves (`advice_ignores_household` 0 in wave 1, 155 in wave 2; `only_with_me` 1 and 92), and confirmation is partly built into the brief.
- **Duplicates.** Cross-posts were read by more than one reader: tb10-0033 and tb14-0162 have the same words from two posts on the same day. Counts in this section are not deduplicated.
