My son is very stressed about various things.

He is afraid to do activities with children he doesn't know, and even though he would like to participate, he stays in his corner and plays alone.
He is in kindergarten (final year) and is already scared of the end-of-year school celebration. He wasn't able to join the end-of-year dance in middle and lower kindergarten because he was too anxious and cried too much. This happens from time to time to one kid out of 20 in a class in lower kindergarten, but for him it's gonna be the third year in a row.

He always needs to know that his parents, or perhaps more specifically, his father, are nearby. He doesn’t want to sleep alone and cries at the mere thought that we might not be the first ones to pick him up from school.

We have a lot of ideas about where all this might come from.

Talking to him about his feelings and try to put words on his fears. Reassure him that we are never going to leave him alone.
We tried various baby steps to get him to get better. For example simply "Say hi to people we meet". We also try to congratulate him on all his little successes.

This year, he is joining activities with other kids, and after 5 trainings of this activity, I made it to leave the room for a few minutes. One thing to add, is that he wanted to join these activities from the start. One of them he seemingly is really good at, so he is also with almost only older kids.

I feel that there are a few improvements but on some sides he almost gets worse. The crying at the thought that we're not coming first to pick him up started last week after my mother-in-law came 5 minutes late to the school. (I also arrived 10 minutes late last month following a misunderstanding with my wife).

We are going to take appointments with a teacher and maybe with a psychiatrist, as we are not sure how to help anymore. If anyone has other ideas/insights we would be grateful!

ANSWER 44482 | owner_id=45689 | license=CC BY-SA 4.0
Therapy

In my opinion he is young for a psychiatrist. A diagnosis may entitle you to government-funded therapies, depending on where you live, but that isn't necessarily what will most help him. He may get more practical benefit from a rock-star therapist who can help him work out his feelings. Choosing a therapist has to be done carefully, getting personal recommendations and being willing to pay for the best. At this age, art, music, drama, gardening therapies can all be very beneficial, depending on what he connects with.

Planning ahead

In addition to the emotional side, there is also the practical side of dealing with the stresses as they come up. Teach him how to plan for the potential "worst case scenarios".

For example, he is very stressed out when picked up late. Reassure him that it's normal to want to be picked up first. It's not a good feeling to be picked up late. But if it happens he will have a plan in place and the tools to manage. Let's say he can count how many kids are picked up already. If it is more than X (half the class?) he can ask the teacher to call you to find out where you are. You would have to pre-arrange this with the teacher that she would agree to call you, assuming this wouldn't happen often. This will give him a measure of reassurance that there is what to do when the issue comes up.

General Encouragement

Edited to add - his anxiety is likely a sign of high intelligence, thinking ahead and identifying potential problems. As he grows, he will likely continue to experience anxiety about perceived threats, but will hopefully develop the skills to prepare for and manage the stressful situations. As parents, we can't and shouldn't take away all the stresses from our children, but rather give them the confidence that they can handle them.

ANSWER 44682 | owner_id=9121 | license=CC BY-SA 4.0
I'm not a therapist.

Number 1 -- I agree with another answer here -- do not take him to a psychiatrist -- their job is to diagnose and prescribe. Psychotherapy could be an option however I think much of this is very normal. He's got a relatively recent younger brother, which is a huge change in the dynamic in the family, recently started going to school, which is another huge change; it's clearly a lot for him right now, and him being upset and worried is not the worst thing as he's not hurting others.

However he's clearly distressed I'm curious to know what life is like for him outside of school. Separation anxiety is quite normal, however 5 is a bit older than the usual but every child is different and he's going through a lot at the moment. Sounds like you're doing a lot of the right things -- helping him know he's safe, and he can talk about things (although he won't at 5yo know the right language necessarilly for his feelings so you can assist there). Going to clubs is good, but make it a normal thing -- no pressure to stay but let him be there and try it, like it sounds like you are doing.

If stuff has happened where he's been missed a bit or conversely he's not had the chance for whatever reason to be away from you guys much then that is something he might have to grow in resliency over time. Maybe make family friends, go over there with the kids and let the kids play whilst you chat with the parents so he's around you but definitely not necessarilly around you -- he can come back whenever he needs to. As he grows in confidence with that he'll grow knowing that he's safe to do that and be less reliant on you guys for his safety, but that's part of growing up.

I'd be curious to know things like how much time he spends with people at home vs how much time on screens, how much time he spends with friends at school; how much time he spends emotionally connecting with other humans. Does he have a particular teacher he graviates towards at school? Sounds like he doesn't. Lots of questions there.
