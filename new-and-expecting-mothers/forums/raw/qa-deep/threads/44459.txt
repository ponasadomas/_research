ANSWER 44460 | owner_id=13650 | license=CC BY-SA 4.0
You may not have found any authoritative guidelines on swaddling because it isn't considered a controversial or risky practice, and as far as I'm aware it has no negative effects at all.

In a nutshell, swaddling mimics the compactness and comfort of the womb, and helps ease the transition of a newborn into a world outside of the womb. It helps them feel secure for the first few months of their life.

However, you may want to do some reading on when to stop swaddling. It's been a while for us so my memory is fuzzy, but once your baby has the strength to move around you wouldn't want them to roll over without the ability to get out of the swaddle.

With all of this being said: authoritative guidelines may exist, but if they do I've never seen them. I did get quite a bit of help from the NHS in the UK when our kids were younger, so the following links may be useful for you:

https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/reduce-the-risk-of-sudden-infant-death-syndrome/

https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/product-information/swaddling-slings/

COMMENT 95501 | owner_id=9327
@Cdn_Dev - The infant startle response (aka Moro reflex) does, indeed, disturb the infant's sleep. The infant was used to being enclosed in the womb, which dampens the Moro reflex. What a shock it must be to be completely free, and sometimes such shocks aren't a wonderful thing. I'm very much in favor of swaddling. Agree 100% that those who use it read about it first. People can do incredibly dumb things (some of which keep ER doctors in business). (Don't get me wrong, I've done the same dumb things that I treated myself! D'uh!)
