This question comes up regularly on this forum.

Usually you'll have a suggestion of a Junior SIPP (it's probably the best financially, but it's a bit boring and they can't access the money when they might need it most, e.g. first car/house/etc), a JISA or childrens savings accounts.

Some (like myself) are a bit more pessimistic on giving an 18 year old unrestricted access to a large sum of money, fearing they will put it up the wall on takeaways, nights out and designer clothing in short order. Unfortunately I have seen it happen first hand in my wider family. Of course we can hope our kids will know better.

Personally I will be investing the money in my own S&S ISA, earmarking some for them, and then making gifts as and when needed (e.g. suprising them by buying their first car).

Young adults aren't renowned for their financial prowess, but others raise a fair point that if they do spaff it up the wall, it teaches them a lesson in the value of money (but I guess it depends on the sum, a couple of hundred might be worth teaching a lesson for, less so for £20k that you scrimped and saved for over the past 18 years).

There is no single right answer.

A bit late to the party but following our first 2 years ago, I will say the thing that caught me most by surprise was the lack of unexpected expenses!

Sure, you'll probably need to dish out a grand for a car seat, pram, cotbed, etc but aside from nappies… and bottles… and formula (if applicable), the costs are quite minimal. Newborn nappies are insanely cheap and you might only be buying a tub of formula a month at the start. Clothes are subjective, sleepsuits and vests are cheap from supermarkets - but it depends on the person. Some parents can go mad and put their babies in designer gear or mountains of clothes they'll never wear.

I will say, the biggest financial impact (though not surprising) is when they go to nursery. We went from spending less than £50 per month on our daughter (outside of one-off costs I mentioned above) to over 500 when she turned 1 and went to nursery - and that's ~3 days per week with '30' free hours.

Buy used - whatever you're comfortable with. Baby items cost a fortune new and then immediately lose nearly all their value. They're like wedding dresses, no-one wants to be the one buying used for the special occasion.

Except I'd argue it's worse with baby stuff, because aside from 90% of people buying new everything for their precious new family member, oftentimes babies will outgrow clothes/toys/etc before they even use them.

We've picked up countless things (including all the nursery furniture, sterilizer, toys, books, etc) for a fraction of the retail price, oftentimes unused.

The good bit is if you can pick this stuff up used, then use it for a while, you will be able to sell it again for pretty much what you paid for it.

I will say it's extremely easy to spend a lot of money if you're not careful of running on paternal/maternal instinct to buy the best of the best. There's so much gear out there… but babies are simple, they just want feeding, changing, holding and to sleep.
