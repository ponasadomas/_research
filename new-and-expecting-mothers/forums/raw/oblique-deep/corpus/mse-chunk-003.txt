If you don't currently have a tax credit award then no new claims are possible, you would need to claim UC. If you live with a partner then, as with tax credits, this would need to be a joint claim. Qualification for UC is dependant upon your household circumstances.

If you do have a current tax credits award;

Tax credits awards are based upon your annual household earnings with your current award based upon last years annual household income. If this years household income will vary by more than £2500 (up or down) then this needs reporting to HMRC and a revised tax credit award would be issued based upon the revised income figures you supplied.

Hi

Well done for researching. 

All i can say is save as much as you can! 

If your baby doesn't sleep well, like many babies, i think as a first time mum going back at 6 months will be very hard. You could be up 3 times between 11pm to 6am for example at that time still. 😬 

I think that you can get by in maternity by buying nearly everything second hand, or for free. Don't be drawn in to loads of expensive clubs, go to the plenty of free ones and also hand pick the chargeable ones. 

A wildcard option is saving on formula, it hadn't occured to me until someone said it in pregnancy. If you can't get a latch for breastfeeding consider expressing, but this is very hard work at times. It's dual purpose giving them your own milk and also "free". You can get machines second hand, just sterilise. You can stop at any time and combi feed with formula. 

Also don't forget nursery fees when returning, save for this.

A drastic option i know some people have done is to find a job with a better maternity package and have a child slightly later (drastic though).

Lastly, look at what you can cut back on financially to add to your savings pot whilst pregnant, e.g. Takeaways, subscriptions etc. 

Good luck!

Neither is taxable. However, it's also worth adding that even if you end up earning above your taxable allowance, you're no worse off as a couple if one of you has transferred 10% of your tax free allowance to your spouse / civil partner. 

A few years ago, DH transferred his 10% to me, because I was paying tax and he wasn't. We're now in a situation where it's a moot point which of us has the higher taxable income, and it's possibly no longer worth doing. But as we're no worse off leaving things as they are, and as his income may go down unexpectedly, we'll leave things as they are. 

Of course this only works in a situation where you don't see income as strictly 'his' and 'hers' - ours is all 'ours'!
