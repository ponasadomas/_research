Isofix is a european standard fixing. Any Isofix seat will fit any isofix car. That's the point of isofix.

Certain seats may be better in certain cars - most extended rear facing seats are very snug in ford focuses and the like. But the fitting is universal. Not all seats have isofix, and not all cars do either.

POST Comment_66257275 | reply | author_id=915031 | 2014-08-13T22:34:36+00:00
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5027847/hoarding-pre-baby/p2#Comment_66257275
Hoard:

Canned basics - baked beans, chopped tomatoes, tinned spuds (if you like them) - any tin you use regularly to cook.

Speed ingredients - spaghetti keeps admirably in the right container & cooks up in minutes (I'm a fan!) & multipacks of noodles also handy

Depending where you shop you can buy containers of rice - 7kg a time. (I then decant to a tub I can use more handily, but saves me a packet on rice & less worries about end of month feeding.)

Within reason cleaning chemicals, toothpaste, deodorant, & loo roll - storage is going to be the issue as well as upfront affordability, but if you see your favourite brand on special, stock up!

If you can afford it, get a bigger freezer & load it. Both with raw materials & batch cooked heat-and-eats.

In fact, if you have the cash & a pal with an estate car & a Costco card, go through your cupboards & draw up a detailed list of what you currently have & how much of it.

Then draw up a second list of what you'd like to have more of.

Shop *ruthlessly* to that list at Costco, after you've looped through at least 2 supermarkets seeing what they have on special offer! (It'll be tiring & expensive - plan the finances first as well as breaks to rest!)

Do not hoard clothes. Look at the size difference between newborn & one year old. More than 6 of any garment is probably wildly redundant unless & until there's a vomiting bug or your washing machine packs in. Anyway an outfit of sorts is a default present - store all gifts carefully so you can try to exchange them if they don't immediately appeal.

Also I wouldn't hoard washing powder in case your baby's skin doesn't like what you have stashed.

All the very best!

POST Comment_66328453 | reply | author_id=615205 | 2014-08-22T19:21:41+00:00
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5045208/cot-bed-matress-advice-how-to-choose#Comment_66328453
It's hard to know what to get, but there isn't one right answer and budget plays a big part. I think the quality (and budget) levels approximately are:

Foam

Sprung

Pocket sprung

Memory foam

I think you should by the best you can within your budget as pocket sprung and memory foam will last longer than foam and sprung mattresses and probably be more comfortable after a couple of years use. I personally feel foam is best for occasional use or for a small baby, but a growing child sleeping in their bed every day needs sprung or better. Also there's nothing wrong with sprung as a massive proportion of the population sleep on sprung mattresses.

You can buy ones of each mattress type with or without hypoallergenic or temperature moderating properties or removable washable layers. These are optional extras though and I wouldn't spend money on these if it meant dropping down a level on the type of mattress.

It's worth keeping an eye on the sales, particularly this bank holiday weekend, and using cashback sites if ordering online. I got a good deal in the Kiddicare sale a few months ago and got a better mattress for my money.
