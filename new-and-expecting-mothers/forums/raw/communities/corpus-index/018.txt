It's really scary to start solids! Especially because they are CONSTANTLY gagging, spitting, and coughing, never minding the anxiety of trying to feed a whole ass human being.
One thing that has really helped me is the concept of the division of labor when it comes to feeding littles. It's my job as a parent to decide when, what, and where we eat, it's my kiddos decision to decide IF (because sometimes they just don't) and how much. This obviously only applies if your baby is otherwise happy, healthy and experiencing normal development.  
As your kiddo grows, they'll try new things. Last week my kiddo (18 m) wouldn't even touch tartar sauce, but last night she was sticking her fingers in it and licking it.
As parents we often feel like we need to rush our kiddos through their development stages when really, they pick it up at their pace. Right now at 6 months, you're just exploring taste, texture, and the idea of eating.

You're doing great! I'm a casual type with this but when overwhelmed, plans often make me feel better, at least until I find my groove. I've heard of things like "100 foods before 1," some sort of checklist could help you feel more confident that she's actually getting a good variety. There's also lots of Instagram, YouTube, etc. that will give you good inspiration, you could just find a page you like and literally copy-paste what they're doing.
It's also okay if you don't like BLW. They'll have to eat real food eventually of course but there's no reason it needs to happen right now and it's also not all-or-nothing. Do what works!
Your current plan sounds great for 6mo! If not already, you will want to start introducing allergens, I would just pick 1 each week and make sure to serve it at least 3 times that week, then try to get it back in at least once every couple weeks. A lot of the allergens are easy to mix into cereal or purees, even shellfish could be minced and added to a savory version of their oatmeal! I also have easy foods prepped for when this just isn't realistic at all though (like if we're eating fast food). Besides the things you mentioned, I have some banana egg pancakes in my freezer, yogurt, peanut butter and bread, baby puffs, plain applesauce, cheese and soft tortillas, and some pouches. Between it all, it's easy to throw together a variety of meals without much time or effort.

If it’s anxiety-producing for you, take your time.
My second is a huge eater and would routinely, in the beginning, try to eat too much and gag a bit. I would just get him out of the high chair, lay him over my leg, and smack his back. Then I started mashing foods (banana, avocado). My baby is 8 months now, and we just recently started working on meltable solids and small pieces of soft chunk foods.
Iron should go in the bottle (if you do one, again at this point food is just for exploration not for nutrition). For variety, think about introducing the same food over and over for 4 days, then changing it up. This helps identify any sensitivities and gives children a chance to adjust to the food. Not sure if this is helpful but happy to answer questions if you have any!

I felt like I had no idea what or when I was supposed to be introducing certain food and its definitely a bit nerve wracking to start. Im the last of my siblings to have a child, so when mine was around 6mo at Christmas they kind of threw us right into it and the first solid thing she had was a sausage link to gum on. Not ideal, but it did a lot to reduce my stress around it because it felt like something way more advanced than where she was at, but she ended up being really interested in foods and flavor and now at 14 months she eats just about anything I put in front of her.
For the most part I tried to start with a mix of things she could gum on without choking on the whole thing at once, dicing my foods up really small and sharing (adjusting salt/sugar etc,) and getting the Teether snacks that more or less dissolve in their mouth. I’m weaning my 2nd baby now and it’s much easier that 1st time around. This baby just chows down on anything I give him. Because I’m cooking for the whole family he just gets what we do in a BLW friendly format. Breakfast is mashed weetabix, strawberries, raspberries, crushed blueberries, half a banana for him to hold and munch on, eggs, sourdough toast (nice and crispy) with peanut butter/avocado/butter on, pancake strips, porridge fingers to grab. Lunch might be some chicken cut into strips with veg, pasta. Dinner I use “What mummy makes” book to make dinner for us all, or whatever we are having but just make sure it’s all low salt. He’s fond of salmon.
I found the solid starts app really helpful and having a few freezer bits for when I CBA to cook so for example I have made spinach, sweetcorn and pea pancakes (all whizzed into a smooth batter) and will take them with cream cheese on for picnics.
It’s amazing, you really can give them most things except honey at this stage. Just have to be careful with things like carrots (needs to be mushy), apples (need to be cooked when first starting).
