1. **The most important thing is to be flexible with what you guys plan:** Its going to take a few weeks to settle into a routine that works and as soon as you do baby will grow and change and you will have to change again.
2. **Plan for your partner to do most if not all the household chores and upkeep for the first few weeks:** cleaning, laundry, cooking, cleaning, shopping...if he can take on most or all of this then this frees up an enormous amount of time for you to focus on recovery and learning how to breastfeed.
3. **Overnights:** you have basically two options - both of you get up every few hours to feed and care for baby (e.g. you breastfeed then he changes or burps baby and rocks them back to sleep) or you take shifts (e.g. you breastfeed & care for baby while he sleeps then later when its his shift he bottle feeds baby (typically while you pump) and cares for baby). A lot of what you end up deciding to do will depend on your supply, whether or not you pump, and whether or not baby will take a bottle.

Im getting ready to have our second child and our general setup is: mom takes care of the baby and partner takes care of mom. So household chores are nearly 100% on my partner. He also takes a night shift so I can get more than 2 hours of sleep at a time. My primary job was feeding and changing baby and he kept up with bottle and pump part washing for the most part. Other essential chores like laundry, trash and cooking also fall to my spouse, with me pitching in when possible or as needed.
This works for us because my spouse is interested in being helpful and realistic about postpartum recovery. He is a get it done kind of person. Sincerely your body just gestated life for close to a year and needs as much rest as you can get. Many other chores can fall by the wayside because you’ll both be in survival mode on minimal sleep. Vacuuming was an afterthought honestly.
Our schedule was (and will likely again be) something like: daytime mom cares for baby except when needing short breaks, showers.
