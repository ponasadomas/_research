I had planned for it to just be the four of us after my second daughter’s birth. No. The first thing I did the morning after she was born was call my mom to come help because I realized I had been doing the care taking math wrong. I thought husband takes care of toddler, I take care of baby. I forgot— who takes care of ME?! Do not underestimate “take care of the two year old” especially if there are no other caretakers/daycare for that kid. That is a full time job for a toddler who just went through a massive life change and is feeling insecure.
So anyway that’s all to say, here is my list:
- take care of older sibling
- laundry. Just assume every day.
- grocery shopping
- preparing food
- deliver 3 meals and 3 snacks a day to mom
- field visits/visit requests from community
- protect mom and baby from being overwhelmed by visitors and siblings
- wash and put away dishes
- take care of any pets
- take out the trashes full of bloody adult diapers
With my husband and two genuinely helpful grandparents here, I got the bonus of vacuuming and mopping and toilets getting cleaned (thanks dad!) but otherwise no way would any of those things have been done for a month or so.
ETA: some kids who have previous childcare become very clingy to home and don’t want to leave after a baby is born. So also just account for the existing care for that kid, if there is any, to shift more to dad than you might expect.

[Thursday October 02 2025] [+2 votes]
Oh man, the "rawdogging the first week" description hit me hard because thats exactly what so many of us do without realizing it. My partner and I had zero clue what we were walking into either, and looking back I wish someone had told us that making these lists isn't about treating your partner like a child - its about survival mode planning when your brain literally wont work properly from sleep deprivation.
One thing that saved us was putting basics on autopilot rather than decision-making tasks. Like instead of "bring me food" we wrote down specific meal ideas he could rotate through, because when you're that exhausted even choosing feels impossible. Also practical stuff like "take over all night diaper changes for toddler" and "handle any middle of the night wake ups that aren't feeding related" because those interruptions when you're already up every 2 hours feeding can break you. We learned at The Village that parents often underestimate how much the postpartum fog affects basic communication, so having these conversations now while you can think clearly is honestly brilliant. What specific parts of managing your 2 year old are you most worried about handling while recovering?
