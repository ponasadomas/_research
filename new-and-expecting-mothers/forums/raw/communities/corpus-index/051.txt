i blocked time on my calendar for pumping (just put BLOCK) so no one could schedule over it.
for getting baby used to the bottle — my lactation consultant recommended the “bait and switch” method where you start with nursing and then switch the bottle in. that and rocking/movement helped her a lot.
i pumped during the workday til about 13 mos and then my daughter was almost entirely on food and we just breastfed before & after work. it was such a sweet and cozy way to reconnect after the day.

    well, my story is a bit more complex because i exclusively pumped until 6 weeks (baby had trouble latching). so i had a bigger stash of backup milk.
    that being said, you don’t actually need a TON to get started. i would recommend pumping 1-2x a day for like a week or two prior to going back to work to build up a few days’ worth of freezer milk, and then going forward you’ll just give baby whatever you pumped the day before.

[Friday October 18 2024] [+2 votes]
Pumping sucks. And you need to fuel and hydrate SO MUCH. Baby is way more effective on the breast than the pump is, so just be ready for that.
I went back to work at 4 weeks postpartum and pumped till 11 months (when there was enough in the freezer to make it to 12 months).  I nursed till 2.5 years on demand.
I made sure to pump every 3-4 hours at work (or in the car) and fed my daughter on demand the rest of the time. Legit I never pumped unless I was missing a feeding. So if we were together, it was boob. If I was away and it would have been a boring time, I pumped. Work. Date nights. Before/after long runs.

I pumped while at work (returned at 10 weeks) and made it 13 months breastfeeding/pumping. I set 3 recurring meetings on my work calendar to block pumping time. It helped me make sure I didn’t skip pumping times.
