The glider we liked for our nursery had a long lead time, and we ended up with a different option so it would arrive by my due date. Plan time to try things in person if possible—there aren’t as many in person baby stores in my state as there used to be, so this took some planning.
If you are planning on using daycare, start your research/tours early so you can get on a waitlist and plan financially.
If you and your partner have paternity leave through your employer or state leave, figure out how long it will be and what paperwork is required. I wish I had asked my husband to make it very clear how we expected early visits to go (e.g., you can come, hold the baby for short periods of time, but we will not be separating from him until we decide we are comfortable doing so).

Two bigger ticket items I personally prioritized, both for safety reasons and for my own mental health:
1. Quality stroller - something that will be easy/quick to use for walks in neighborhood + throwing in car for outings. I’m a pretty active person so it was important for me and my postpartum physical/mental health to be able to continue getting outside and making that as safe and easy as possible.
2. Car seat - same reasons as above, including that the car seat and stroller are one system for convenience and safety. We got used stroller, high chair, bike, sleep sacks, bouncer, playmat (we got Lovevery and I'm VERY happy we didn't pay full price, mis-buy for us), co-sleeper. It will save you a lot of money that can be spent later.

On the prep:
- Batch cooking
- Setting up and doing a run through of all your equipment
- Lining up some support with people you trust - people who will get stuck in and do your laundry, washing up etc.
- Have conversations with your partner about some of the 'policy' decisions you want to make on how to raise your baby - anything from dummies, sleep training, vaccinations etc.
- Research postpartum and not just birth. Waking baby to feed was a big one I didn't have a clue I'd need to do!
- Familiarise yourself with the Safe Sleep 7 for co-sleeping. It absolutely will happen at some point, no matter your intentions, so better it be informed and planned than accidental.
General guidance - don't become the expert in your baby. Give your partner space to find their own way, even if it's not your way (providing it's safe etc).
