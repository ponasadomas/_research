[Sunday August 09 2026] [+43 votes]
Husband and I took shifts at night. He took 8pm-2am and I took 2am-8am. We had a bassinet with wheels on it so my husband would take it and baby into the living room and I could get some solid sleep with minimal to no interruptions. Then my husband sleeps like a rock so he would just roll the bassinet with sleeping baby back into our room and then go to sleep. So depending on your house set up, a rolling bassinet is extremely helpful.   
With household chores and everything else in life with a newborn, give yourself a ton of grace in the early weeks and months. And if something will make your life easier, do it/buy it! Paper plates, take out, premade meals, etc.

[Sunday August 09 2026] [+8 votes]
I did it. 😂 We didn’t really have a choice we’re immigrants living on the other side of the earth, with no family support nearby, and I had an elective C-section.  
Now, four months postpartum with an amazing little boy, I can honestly say the newborn stage was probably the easiest part. 😂 Nappy, feed, burp, sleep. Repeat.  
A lot of it comes down to how you plan things. I was working until 37 weeks in a pretty hectic job, and my partner worked until the day before the baby was born. Every weekend, we meal-prepped. We have a huge fridge-freezer absolutely packed with food, which has been a lifesaver. We also have a dishwasher, an integrated washer-dryer and a robot vacuum, which honestly make life so much easier. We even hired someone for a deep clean before I gave birth.  
My partner had paternity leave, so for the first two weeks he took care of the baby completely — all the night wakes, nappies, feeds, burping, everything — while I was on full bed rest recovering.  
I put snacks and water bottles in any space that I would sit in to remind me to eat.
I had a “ good baby” and a very needy one. I was never “ in the trenches”. It doesn’t happen for everyone. It’s not always easy, but it isn’t always miserable.

Well my motto was “This too shall pass”
I want to start off by saying that it’s very hard abut definitely doable.  
In my case, I had a C-section and it’s just me n my husband (we are both immigrants without any family or close friends here). I was allowed to only lift baby for first 4-6 weeks.   
My husband helped with cooking. For me the first month was the hardest. We came home from hospital severely sleep deprived, for first two weeks my husband n I took turns at night (3-4 hour shift with baby as she would sleep on us and we couldn’t sleep while she was sleeping on us.)  
My partner’s paternity leave ended after this. Next two weeks my partner did pitch in ( he was cooking all meals) , during this time my baby had a growth spurt and on the seventh day she kept cluster feeding from only one breast and that caused a lot of pain and I couldn’t breastfeed anymore. All I want to say that it is great if you have help if not things WILL WORK OUT and I wish you all the best.
Some advice-
Please have bottles , pacifier and sterilise handy ( you don’t know when you might need it- my drive from the hospital baby got hungry and she kept crying and I couldn’t pick her up as she was supposed to be in car seat)
For meals as others have said you can freeze some stuff. Also you want to eat protein rich food maybe you can have easy to prep meals (one pot meals etc)
