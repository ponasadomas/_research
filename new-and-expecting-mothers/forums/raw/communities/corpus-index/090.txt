Bring snacks, meals, water, coffee/tea often! FridaMom makes a great postpartum kit. Trading shifts has worked well for us, 6-7 hours of sleep each and time to eat/shower/decompress. Stock the freezer with crockpot/easy meals. Paper plates. Meds on schedule. Have formula on hand in case you need to supplement. Oh and good nursing/pumping bras. Try Bodily. Wait to order closer to end of pregnancy so the sizing stays true, I went up 3 sizes.

    Yes it’s a lot easier to just quickly feed and pass the baby off than to have to feed change and make sure LO gets to sleep every 2-3hr. Tissues, reassuring, space, love, and whatever she finds comforting - for as long as it takes.
Just before or after the above, engorgement as the milk comes in. Let her be topless, help with things to mop up the mess, encourage pumping to build a supply, and encourage warm showers with hand expressing and cool compresses for comfort. Also Tylenol.
Be the Visitation Gatekeeper. The first few weeks are overwhelming and she'll get tired very quickly, but everyone will want to come meet the new baby. Your job is to say "We'll let you know when we're ready for visiting!" (Or make sure everyone who comes actually helps with chores and doesn't just play Pass the Baby while y'all host them). Yes, this means you have to tell the grandparents No, too! (You're the husband and dad now, that's your job, and it's OK to put your family's NEEDS before anyone else's WANTS.)
If it seems like more than baby blues (a day or two of sudden emotion shift due to hormones changing), call her OB/midwife and ask. You're her advocate and she may need help from someone outside herself recognizing that "Honey, you're not you right now".
