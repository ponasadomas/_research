## The answer per niche

- **Baby sleep — the strongest case.** The main complaint about paid help is that it wasn't built for this baby ("standard wake windows she suggests for all clients"); consultants cost $500–1,000+, which pushes parents to do it themselves; parents ask for exactly this ("How can i know ahead of trying what suits my kid?"). App reviews add refund anger and stated prices for digital plans (£60 for pre-written schedules called too much). The plan must adapt to setbacks (the most common story shape), use the baby's real day not just age, and have branches for vomiting, no separate room and no partner.
- **Pregnancy and birth — a real gap, with a missing section.** Asks for "a guide", "one stop shop", "the very basics"; generic week-by-week content and care that ignores pregnancy number fail. The product brief leaves out the third-largest theme, **work and leave** (short-term disability, FMLA, maternity pay), and the first two weeks, the partner gap, visitors and the older child need real sections. $29.95 matches Pirmi, Complete Pregnancy Blueprint and Mother Compass ($29.99); reviews show anger at subscriptions, not one-off prices; nobody on Reddit bought a pregnancy guide.
- **Toddler behaviour and potty — spend exists, but on gear and on people.** The real deadline is daycare policy; advice that ignores the household fails; scripts fail even for trained people; parents buy gear, not courses ("300 dollar pdf's full of open doors"). Reviews: buyers pay for answers to their exact situation and for one plan every caregiver can use; book readers want finished steps.
- **Development — thin on Reddit, rescued by reviews.** Reddit shows no buying of development information and a filter that was mostly noise; the clock, not ideas, is the problem ("5 hours to fill"). App reviews supply the strongest evidence in all five niches for the Mamivio pricing model: paying users asking for a one-time price ("I would absolutely have paid a one time cost"). The owner brief's §8 rules out the "behind" angle Pirmi's ads run on.
- **Mother's own sleep — a module, not a product.** Real pain, no spend: her sleep can stay broken after the baby's is fixed, so it cannot be promised; the night split is the plannable part. Best as the "get yourself back" section inside baby sleep.

## What holds across all five

1. **"Personalised" is believed only when the answers visibly change the plan.** Every niche has buyers burned by quizzes that led to generic content.
2. **One-time price beats subscription**, in every review set; trials and paywalls are where trust breaks; a refund must be easy to claim.
3. **The partner and other caregivers are part of the job**: a plan usable by everyone in the house, in a format the partner will read.
4. **Lines not to cross are all over this market** (day counts, outcome claims, diagnosis and "behind" hooks); Mamivio's restraint is a differentiator, and 1,694 quotes are marked research-only.

What wave 2 changed, per niche (detail and ids in each §16):

- **Baby sleep — direction holds, the product's core moves.** Free fixes from threads often work the next night and wear off within days to two weeks; the plan must say what a relapse looks like, what to change first and how to recover after travel, illness and nap changes — or the 3- and 6-month tiers have no reason to cost more. Course prices are now named ($100–300, "robbery", "$100 down the drain"; consultants "$200+", "~$500", "$900 biggest waste"), all far above Mamivio's band; nobody names a price inside it. Parents also pay a person for accountability, reassurance and taking decisions off their hands — the plan must do that job on paper (decisions made in the daytime, stop rules, one partner script).
- **Pregnancy — still asks, no purchase; the shape changes.** Work and leave, US prepayment deadlines ("$3,519 by 24 weeks or I can be dismissed"), the partner's leave gap, who minds the older child and the visit window read as one dated plan that has to start in the first trimester. They belong in the product as questions and dates, with US and UK editions, never as entitlements. Price sensitivity is high ($10 "very well spent", NCT £300 "a waste"), which favours the $17.95/$29.95 tiers.
- **Toddler behaviour — holds; the opening is filling Oh Crap's gaps** (when to pause or reset, naked to clothes, working parents, carpet), not being gentler. Daycare cuts both ways (licensing bans on pull-ups, removal threats, but also daycares that slow or undo training). No method wins, so the plan branches on what the child does next. "All advice I see online ends up being an advert for a paid course or a particular type of underwear" is the objection to answer.
- **Mother's own sleep — still a module, for a new reason.** Families do pay for night help (overnight doulas $65–70/hr, $700/week) and for baby-sleep courses, but nobody pays for information about her own sleep. The night module must work whoever covers the night.
- **Development — sharper.** The first age band is 2–6 months (wake-window boredom, "the next hour is hell"); nobody buys development information in either wave; "worth it" means minutes of independent play, so the guide must deliver minutes, and "permission, not more homework" becomes the core.

What wave 3 changed, per niche (detail and ids in each §17):

- **Baby sleep — the verdict is sharpened, and relapse now has an answer.** Fixes that only nudge the clock on a nearly right schedule fade in 1–5 days; fixes hold for weeks to months when they change something else (hunger, activity, total sleep, schedule first then a method, a rule the parent keeps applying). Trained babies run on a second clock: months or years, then one trigger breaks it (travel, illness, teeth, standing, daycare, dummy weaning plus a toddler bed at about two). The relapse section must tell the two clocks apart, check non-clock causes first, say where to restart for each trigger, and give a stop rule for crying, which parents say no book, consultant or doctor gives them. The 3- and 6-month tiers earn their price only with dated re-checks, nap transitions and the toddler material. Paid help fails on fit and relapses the same way; $500 is the recurring paid figure; nobody names a price inside Mamivio's band, and the new objection is that the information is free (sometimes the partner's). Paying a person for accountability stays narrow. Seeding clusters in early April – mid May posts only.
- **Pregnancy — the core holds, the shape changes.** The asks for one ordered guide are the sharpest yet ("a book or something I can purchase"); still nobody buys or prices one. Wave 2's "one dated plan" held as a single plan in only about a dozen posts, almost all late third trimester. In spring, work is about surviving the job now (notes, accommodations, sick days, when to tell work). So: a "keep your job and your cover" section for the first and second trimesters, written as questions; the dated calendar (his leave, her stop date, the help window and its end, the older child, birth-date-dependent benefits) in the third; US prepayment as an insert, not the spine. Her own mother is cited more often than the mother-in-law (367 v 268 quotes). Price unchanged.
- **Toddler behaviour — holds; daycare now more often stalls training than starts it.** A branch for the daycare whose rule conflicts with the method becomes a must-have; the household inputs grow (one bathroom, carpet, heat, shifts, the money for help, what this child finds rewarding). No wave-3 parent names a price for a course, consultant, plan or app; the buying habit is still gear. A cheap plan competes with free employer coaching, AI and a paid person valued for answering, not content.
- **Mother's own sleep — still a module, with a new requirement.** Help arriving and her still not sleeping is the strongest new pattern, including paid help (a gifted night nanny, a paid night doula). The module must plan for the covered night that still brings no sleep, and must not promise to fix the part that is the mind. Still nobody pays for information about her own sleep.
- **Development — shape holds, first band widens.** The first age band should run to 9 months, not stop at 6; "permission, not more homework" is confirmed and hardened; "worth it" stretches from minutes to hours and months of use; one-off pricing stays consistent.
