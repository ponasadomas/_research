Before you start
Questions parents ask.
Is this a diagnosis?
No. Kinedu is a developmental check-in: it helps you notice strengths and the skills opening up next. It does not identify developmental conditions or delays. If something concerns you, your pediatrician is the right person to talk to — and the summary is built to be shared with them.
How long does it really take?
Less than five minutes. The check-in is 32 questions chosen for your child’s age, plus a few that never count toward the reading. You can leave and come back — your answers are saved as you go.
My baby was born early.
We use their corrected age automatically, following American Academy of Pediatrics practice, until age two — and every reading tells you when it has been applied.
What ages does it cover?
Birth to 48 months. The questions change completely with age: a 7-month-old and a 3-year-old are asked about entirely different milestones.
What do I actually get for free?
The full 32-question check-in, your child’s overall picture, the developmental age they are showing today, and their strongest area named. The full profile opens all four areas and the complete milestone set for their age.

Kinedu created the caregiver-report instrument and tested its initial psychometric properties with 2,135 Spanish-speaking caregivers. Michael C. Frank advised on the early revision of its 0–24-month milestone items.
2 · Expert research

children
Studied by developmental experts.
Stanford researchers Benjamin Stenhaug, Nilam Ram, and Michael C. Frank analyzed 177,934 Kinedu observations from 21,861 children to study how developmental dimensions change together over time.

A story, not a verdict
Where a child starts does not decide how fast they grow.
A child’s development changes. That is why Kinedu treats a first assessment as a starting picture, not a prediction of who they will become. Return when it is helpful and see what is new.
What this is, and isn’t.
Kinedu helps parents notice strengths and next steps. It does not diagnose developmental conditions or delays. If something concerns you, talk with a pediatrician or qualified professional.
