Don't waste your money on organic reusables for incontinence until you read this.

I bought the organic pad sets with absorbent underwear from Ultradries that keeps popping up in my feed and I'm going to give you my honest review.

I was super skeptical because after 8 years of relying on disposable pads for bladder leaks, the idea of switching to reusables felt scary.

How do you wash them?

Will they smell?

What if they leak through?

What if I'm just throwing away more money on something that doesn't work?

I almost talked myself out of it three times before I finally ordered.

But after using them for a few weeks I have to be honest about what happened.

The first thing I noticed when I opened the package was how normal everything looked.

I don't know what I was expecting.

Something medical looking, maybe.

Something padded and bulky like the reusable cloth diapers my mother used to talk about.

But it just looked like regular underwear.

Soft organic cotton.

And the booster pads were thin, neat, nothing like the thick disposables I'd been stuffing into my underwear for 8 years.

I put a pair on and waited for the catch.

There's always a catch.

The first few hours I kept checking.

Every time I had a small leak I'd press my hand against the fabric expecting to feel dampness.

Nothing.

Dry.

Every time.

I actually went to the bathroom to look because I didn't trust it.

By day two I stopped checking.

By the end of the first week something happened that I didn't expect at all.

I realized I hadn't thought about my bladder once that day.

Not once.

No planning around bathrooms.

No mental countdown to the next pad change.

No spare disposable pads crammed in my purse.

I just lived my day.

I can't tell you the last time that happened in 8 years.

But here's the part that really got me.

And this is why I'm writing this review.

I've been wearing disposable pads every single day for nearly 8 years.

And for most of that time I've had irritated skin.

Red patches.

Itching.

That raw burning feeling that never fully goes away because you're putting a fresh pad on damaged skin every morning.

I thought that was just part of having incontinence.

I thought it was my skin.

My age.

My body.

It wasn't.

Within two weeks of switching to Ultradries the rashes started clearing up.

Rashes I'd had for years.

I stood in front of my bathroom mirror and looked at normal skin for the first time in longer than I can remember.

It was the pads.

It was always the pads.

The organic cotton feels completely different against your skin.

There's no synthetic layer trapping heat and moisture.

No adhesive pulling at you every time you move.

No fragrance.

No chemicals.

Just clean cotton and bamboo sitting against your body.

My skin could finally breathe and it healed itself once I stopped suffocating it with disposables every day.

I also haven't had a UTI since I switched.

I used to get them every few months like clockwork.

My doctor told me disposable pads contribute because they hold warmth and dampness against you all day.

The perfect setup for bacteria.

Take that away and apparently my body figured out the rest on its own.

Now let me talk about the practical stuff because I know that's what you really want to know.

Especially if you're nervous about reusables like I was.

Each pair of underwear holds 100ml on its own.

That's about 3 to 4 disposable pads worth.

If you need more, the booster pads snap right into the underwear and add another 100ml.

200ml total.

That's handled everything I've thrown at it and I'm not a light leaker.

The booster pads are my favorite part honestly.

When I'm home I just use a booster on its own for light leaks and swap it out when I need to.

Takes seconds.

No undressing.

When I'm going out for a longer stretch I snap the booster into the underwear for full protection.

The starter set comes with everything.

Underwear, boosters, a mesh wash bag, and a little waterproof pouch that looks like a cosmetics bag.

I keep it in my purse with a spare booster.

My daughter picked it up once thinking it was a makeup pouch.

That's how discreet it is.

Washing — the thing I was most afraid of — turned out to be the easiest part.

You don't hand scrub anything.

You don't soak anything.

Mesh bag, into the machine, normal cycle.

Done.

I do it twice a week and it takes zero extra effort.

Honestly it's less work than the weekly trip to buy another pack of disposables.

And the smell question?

Nothing.

No odor.

The organic cotton doesn't trap smells the way synthetics do.

That surprised me more than anything.

Now here's what I'll say because I'm being honest.

These don't cure incontinence.

I still leak.

That hasn't changed.

But everything around the leaking has changed.

The rashes are gone.

The infections stopped.

My skin is healthy.

I'm not spending $30 to $40 a month on disposable pads that were making me miserable.

And I'm not pressing chemicals against my body every day anymore.

The set paid for itself within the first month.

One month.

After 8 years of spending money on pads, creams for the rashes, and doctor visits for the UTIs.

If you've been on the fence about switching to organic reusables, I get it.

I was terrified.

8 years of doing things one way is hard to walk away from even when that way is making you worse.

But I wish I hadn't waited so long.
