Starting solids doesn’t mean your baby is suddenly going to start eating full meals overnight 🥩🥦🥔🍝

Social media can make it seem like every baby is sitting down and eating huge plates of food from day one, but the reality is that this isn’t typical for most babies. 🤍

Some babies take to solids quickly, while others need more time to explore, touch, squish, taste, and get comfortable with new foods. And that’s completely normal. 

In the beginning, it’s not about how much food your baby actually eats. Prior to 12 months, breastmilk and/or formula should be your baby’s primary source of nutrition. Introducing solid food is all about helping them explore, learn, and build exposure to new foods and  allergens. Success looks different for everyone, but the goal is building familiarity, developing skills, and creating positive experiences around food.

✨ If your baby is showing interest, exploring different foods, and gradually becoming more comfortable at mealtimes, you’re on the right track!

Try not to compare your baby’s plate to what you see on Instagram. Every baby learns at their own pace, and progress doesn’t always look like an empty plate. 🤍

Wean with it, rock with it. May the odds be ever in your flavor…😉🪨📄✂️

But here’s the thing: when it comes to starting solids, it’s not about winning or losing. 

Purées, finger foods, or a little bit of both? There’s room for all of it!

Every baby learns at their own pace, every family finds their own rhythm, and no two feeding journeys look exactly alike.

🥄 Okay, so what’s wrong with spoonfeeding? Around here we talk about the many benefits of baby-led weaning, which means: letting baby lead. The research tells us that the risk of choking is minimized when a baby brings food to their own mouth and that placing food into their mouth increases risk. Spoonfeeding, while perfectly acceptable as a method for feeding baby, does not allow baby to self-feed. And our goal here is to offer the safest approach backed by research. 

🥣 But why no purees? To be clear, we love purées! There’s nothing wrong with offering purées, but offer them in combination with chewable foods to give baby the safest start. Learning to chew is important between 6-9 mos and babies can’t do that with only textureless food. 

The real win here is helping your little one explore new foods, build confidence, and enjoy the adventure (mess and all). The goal is let baby lead the way.🤍

Strawberries can help your baby absorb MORE iron 👀

Strawberries aren’t particularly high in iron themselves, but they’re rich in vitamin C, which helps your baby’s body absorb non-heme iron (the type found in plant-based foods).

And that’s especially important when starting solids. 

Around 6 months, babies’ iron stores begin to decline, making iron-rich foods an important part of their diet.

Try pairing strawberries with:

🥣 Iron-fortified baby oatmeal
🫘 Lentils or beans
🌱 Tofu
🥜 Smooth, thinned nut butter (once you’ve ruled out allergies)

Want to know how to safely serve each of these foods?
