## 1. The reader

The useful common thread is a parent trying to turn broad advice into an executable day. Baby play adds competing duties; solids creates meal-production decisions; returning to work adds facilities, meetings and caregiver coordination; postpartum preparation adds the question of who looks after the mother. These are different jobs at different stages, not one demographic avatar. Many posters and reviewers do not identify their gender, country or first-child status. Targeting mothers is the owner's intended business direction, not a demographic proportion established here. [aud01-0012] [aud01-0015] [aud01-0095] [aud01-0138]

Within solids, the sharpest small-case distinction is that some parents do not already cook regular adult meals that can simply be adapted for the baby. That is more useful for report inputs than a general label such as an overwhelmed new mother. It rests on two indexed discussions, with incomplete original context. [aud01-0022] [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030]

## 2. Stage-specific arcs

There is no evidence for one universal maternal journey. Four observed planning sequences are worth testing:

| Stage and trigger | What the parent does next | What a report might organize |
|---|---|---|
| Baby becomes more awake; parent seeks a weekly list | Searches activities, compares apps, checks whether ordinary care is enough | A small number of feasible play choices [aud01-0007] [aud01-0111] |
| First foods approach or have just started | Chooses a feeding approach, looks for progression, recipes and serving information | Shopping and preparation around the household's actual routine [aud01-0018] [aud01-0020] [aud01-0035] |
| Return-to-work date becomes imminent | Maps sessions around commute, room access, meetings and childcare | Preparation/coverage questions and a handoff around an established feeding plan [aud01-0074] [aud01-0082] [aud01-0095] |
| Birth or a second baby's arrival approaches | Assigns baby/older-child work, tries to arrange help, later revises the plan | Real support responsibilities and backup arrangements [aud01-0138] [aud01-0139] [aud01-0148] |

These sequences are editorial interpretations of selected cases. They are neither prevalence estimates nor developmental or clinical timelines.

## 3. What others say to them

Advice can assume resources the parent lacks: an existing adult-meal routine, control over work meetings, reliable relatives, or unlimited time for developmental activities. The value of a report would be tested by whether it respects those constraints. [aud01-0030] [aud01-0090] [aud01-0004] [aud01-0012]

One parent sums up the desired level of instruction: “I just need someone to explain it like I’m 5.” [aud01-0018] Another asks for a straightforward artifact: “It feels like a simple list (or a spreadsheet if you really need to get fancy) should do the trick.” [aud01-0113] Both are indexed excerpts, not evidence of a paid order.

Do not turn family or clinician remarks reported by a poster into verified professional guidance. Advice in raw threads includes unsafe or unsuitable instructions; the quote bank deliberately excludes those instructions as recommendations.

## 4. What they try, and how it lands

Parents try apps, books, free databases, calendar blocks, ordinary household adaptations and general-purpose AI. Results vary even within the same category. One first-solids user finds following an initial book plan reassuring, while another says free serving information was enough after paying with the first child. [aud01-0027] [aud01-0026]

Original app reviews support both sides of the report-format decision. A reviewer wanted directed food planning; another did not return to a long purchased plan while caring for a baby with dietary complications. Grocery organization is praised in BLW Meals, while a 2025 Solid Starts reviewer requests advance visibility of allergen-introductory meals. These are different use cases and historical experiences, not evidence that current meal planners generally lack shopping support. The implication is to test actual use and planning effort, rather than assume that a PDF or an app is inherently better. [rev01-0097] [rev01-0085] [rev01-0108] [rev01-0093]

For pumping, a commenter explicitly reports using Chat GPT to assemble a schedule from commute, shifts and preferences. That challenges a paid schedule's differentiation; it does not validate AI-generated physiological advice. For postpartum arrangements, morning revisions are part of one reported working approach. [aud01-0137] [aud01-0148]

## 5. The other side: partners, caregivers and people who stop

A concerned father wants organized observations to discuss at appointments; a mother wants a nanny to have developmentally appropriate activity ideas; nanny replies value explanation and demonstration. A printable handoff could help, but the evidence also points to features a static document loses. [aud01-0042] [aud01-0099] [aud01-0100] [aud01-0101]

The workplace has its own constraints. A self-described school administrator says pumping arrangements require timetable coordination, and a teacher reports colleague coverage mattered. A plan cannot create staff coverage. Likewise, a family support roster cannot create available people or paid help. [aud01-0130] [aud01-0132] [aud01-0140]

Parents who stop are not failed prospects to pressure. One switches feeding arrangements before returning to work, another stops pumping because it consumes scarce work time, and some stop tracking or using apps. These are alternatives that a report must allow rather than shame. [aud01-0085] [aud01-0126] [aud01-0127] [aud01-0054] [aud01-0056]

## 6. What they ask, want and fear

The questions are operational: what happens next week, what food to prepare, how meals progress, when workplace tasks fit, and who is responsible. Fears include missing something important, getting behind and failing to meet other people's apparent standards. The sources do not show that escalating those fears creates a useful or effective product. [aud01-0110] [aud01-0020] [aud01-0077] [aud01-0002] [aud01-0007] [aud01-0031]

One original reviewer requests capability-based filtering of activities; another asks for developmental ranges and context. These accounts challenge age-only customization. They do not validate a diagnostic questionnaire or precise weekly achievement deadlines. [rev01-0025] [rev01-0126] [guide01-001]

## 7. Advice and alternatives they already encounter

The market already supplies free development videos, checklists, food databases, full books, tracking apps, personalized plans and live experts. A payer needs a reason to choose a report beyond the existence of information. A direct app review praises a free food database; indexed parents recommend Pathways' free activities and demonstrations. [rev01-0120] [aud01-0096] [mkt01-0006]

More content can become another obligation. Original reviews describe tracking pressure, a desire for a focused tool, and filler that slows access to useful guidance. Other reviewers value ongoing tracking or continue paying for subscriptions; there is no evidence that all mothers want a single-payment PDF. [rev01-0042] [rev01-0064] [revb01-0008] [rev01-0113] [rev01-0069]

The follow-up original Google Play reviews add similar objections on another platform: capability feedback that reportedly did not change recommendations, a comparison with spreadsheet summaries, and praise for free Pathways. These selected public review bodies are not full histories or proof that the same issues persist in current versions. [revf01-0003] [revf01-0009] [revf01-0016]

## 9. Segments that sound different

- **First-solids parents without a regular cooking routine** need a different starting point from families who can adapt an existing dinner. This narrow distinction has only two indexed discussion accounts behind it. [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030]
- **Parents with another caregiver** may value clear handoff, but some specifically value live synchronized records. A static plan is a different product from a shared daily log. [aud01-0099] [rev01-0048] [rev01-0157]
- **Teachers and shift workers** name timetable, room, commute and coverage constraints that a standard office-day template misses. [aud01-0088] [aud01-0133] [aud01-0134]
- **Expecting second-time mothers** can have an older-child logistics problem and an omitted maternal-care role rather than a need for more newborn facts. [aud01-0138] [aud01-0139]
- **Parents who dislike tracking** ask for a small list or a simpler tool. Others value tracking strongly. Treat these as distinct preferences, not a uniform anti-app market. [aud01-0113] [rev01-0042] [rev01-0142]

There is no basis here for estimating segment sizes or claiming a mothers-versus-fathers purchasing difference. Country-specific evidence is thin and mixed; English-language access is not global representativeness.

## 10. Does the evidence support Mamivio's current method?

It supports testing organization, clarity and feasible activities. It does not establish that age alone creates a valuable paid customization, that a two-week age offset assesses ability, or that Mamivio's delivered guide improves development. The paid report was not bought or inspected. [aud01-0111] [rev01-0025] [mkt01-0004]

The current page's historical developmental-decline narrative, percentage figures, named-specialist credentials, testimonials and family counts remain unverified. CDC's checklist methodology does not by itself establish a historical decline in development. Awake supervised tummy time must remain distinct from sleep. See `product/product-brief.md`, `product/funnel.md` and `sources/guidance.md`. [guide01-001] [guide01-002]

The most defensible research direction is to test whether a report changes concrete household decisions. That is a product hypothesis, not clinical efficacy evidence. Detailed inputs, outputs, competitors, costs and proposed validation are in `niche-opportunities.md`.

## 13. Lines not to cross

Use neither anxiety about ordinary development nor claims of lowered standards as proof that a baby needs a paid intervention. Do not offer diagnosis or treatment through a report, promise earlier milestones, guarantee sleep/milk/allergy outcomes, or let calendar age determine readiness. Qualified review and referral boundaries matter for health-sensitive content. Crisis, violence, pregnancy loss, infant death and minors' accounts are excluded from marketing evidence. [guide01-001] [guide01-002] [guide01-003] [guide01-004] [guide01-007] [guide01-008]

Report clinical guidance separately from buyer demand. Country-specific health and legal guidance needs current primary verification before deployment; parents' comments and historical books do not establish current rights or treatment rules. No ads or products were launched in this session.

- **Unprobed/private sources:** no owner emails, buyer interviews, private Facebook/Discord material or consented survey data exist. No access to a private group is implied by this task.
- **Video and comments:** no full YouTube videos/transcripts/comments; the top-200 inventory is absent. Planned query topics that were never executed are named in `youtube-top-videos.md`.
- **Advertising depth:** all 63 retained body texts were read; creative videos/images/alternate variants and later Library pages remain unreviewed. Per-id reading status and hashes are in `sources/ad-body-reading-ledger.tsv`. Keyword result counts include off-topic ads. Owner advertiser Page identity and performance remain unverified.
- **Market completeness:** public offer discovery is not a census. Restricted brands, marketplace results, paid contents, account branches and supplementary follow-ups are named in `sources/competitors-not-harvested.md` and the competitor ledger. No actual report output or checkout purchase was evaluated.
- **Balance:** expecting-mother, recovered-parent, country-specific, low-income/non-app-user, prematurity and clinical-professional interview cells are thin or absent. `counts.md` separates known tags from unknowns. Large unspecified-parent counts must not become a maternal demographic claim.
- **Commercial validation:** no Mamivio purchase intent measured with a real decision, no conversion/refund/support data, no customer acquisition costs, no established production economics. Choosing a narrow pilot remains a decision under uncertainty.

The useful next work is permitted original evidence and a concrete preview/payment comparison with parents at the trigger stage, after defining the report's qualified-review responsibility. More indexed searches cannot close an original-corpus or purchase-data gap.
