# Which mothers could buy a customized Mamivio report?

Research decision, 2026-10-24. Initial launch preference: **English-speaking markets**, supplied by the owner. This is a prioritized validation hypothesis, not a proven profitable niche. No Mamivio buyer interviews, report purchases, price tests, conversion rates or acquisition costs were observed. Evidence provenance and counts are in `findings.md`, `counts.md` and `session-report.md`.

## Recommended first test

**First-time mothers preparing for the first month of solids who do little routine cooking and want a workable weekly shopping-and-preparation plan.** Recruit near the transition, roughly five to seven months of baby age, while distinguishing preparation from readiness to feed. Age does not establish readiness. The age band and first-time-mother restriction are proposed recruitment choices; the source corpus does not measure their prevalence or prove they buy more than other parents.

The useful distinction is the parent's existing food routine. In two separate indexed accounts, advice to feed baby a version of the adults' meals fails because the parent does not regularly eat suitable prepared meals. One mother explicitly describes herself as preferring simple, grab-and-go food. Her baby is eight months old and has already had purées for about two months; the other account concerns a six-and-a-half-month-old who recently started solids. Neither establishes first-child status. Extending this constraint to first-time mothers preparing for the first month is therefore a recruitment hypothesis. These are concrete customization inputs, but only a small number of accounts support the underlying distinction. [aud01-0022] [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030]

Direct original app reviews supply stronger evidence about the proposed deliverable: one reviewer wanted a clear directed plan, another reports not returning to an approximately 200-page plan, and another needed advance meal visibility to shop. The unused-plan account also involved a baby with dietary complications; the advance-visibility complaint concerns allergen-introductory meals in a 2025 app review, not all current meal plans. These are historical, self-reported experiences, not verified purchases or proof that the current app still has the same behavior. They support testing a short, fully visible plan; they do not establish a current competitor defect or willingness to buy Mamivio. [rev01-0097] [rev01-0085] [rev01-0093]

**A suitable research concept is a first-28-days household feeding-planning report**, with one visible weekly grocery list, repeated ingredients, realistic preparation blocks, an agreed feeding approach and a simple caregiver handoff page. Its job is to reduce shopping/preparation decisions. Qualified review is necessary for feeding content and escalation boundaries; it must not assess swallowing, prescribe allergy exposure, treat feeding problems or replace clinical advice. [aud01-0019] [aud01-0024] [rev01-0108] [guide01-003]

## Why this ranks first for testing

The category has a directly comparable one-time product: 101 Before One advertises a first-month plan at $29. More useful than price alone, the review corpus includes people describing payment for a plan and dissatisfaction with its format. This is closer to the intended transaction than a general wish for reassurance. Advertised prices remain offer observations, and reviewers' payment accounts remain unverified self-reports. [mkt01-0014] [rev01-0085] [rev01-0097]

The hypothesis has a named purchase moment, concrete inputs and an output a buyer can inspect before purchase. Those are product-design judgments based on the sources, not measurements of demand. Its defensibility is **unproven**. It is a stronger next experiment than copying an age-selected development PDF, rather than a claim that first solids is an underserved category. [aud01-0025] [aud01-0035] [mkt01-0004] [mkt01-0006]

## The strongest reasons it might fail

1. **Free information may already be enough.** One parent says they paid for the first child but did not need to for the second; another declined to pay after a trial. A reviewer values a free food database. Convenience must earn its price without withholding basic safety information. [aud01-0026] [aud01-0028] [rev01-0120]
2. **The proposed feature bundle already has direct substitutes.** Little Lunches advertises household preferences, pantry reuse, groceries and caregiver sharing, with a free initial plan and published $7.99/month or $49.99/year options. My Little Food Critic sells a 28-day meal-plan guide; its captured localized price was €34.95 and its delivered file format was not inspected. Orango advertises generating 1–100-day diet-adapted plans and grocery lists, although its listing does not establish traction or plan quality. This research therefore cannot claim an unoccupied gap in personalized menus, fixed-duration guides, groceries or caregiver sharing. [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0009] [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0007] [mkt02-0013] [mkt02-0016]
3. **A non-cooking parent may want prepared food or practical help instead of another plan.** The two relevant accounts establish a constraint, not which solution they will buy. A plan that introduces a new cooking hobby recreates the problem. [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030]
4. **The health-related work can exceed the economics of a low-priced report.** Age alone cannot determine readiness, suitable textures or an individualized allergy plan. Clinical review, exceptions, customer questions and revisions must be costed before scaling. [guide01-003]
5. **A PDF can also become clutter.** The long-plan complaint is evidence against the format being inherently useful. Page count and personalization claims cannot substitute for actual use in the kitchen. [rev01-0085]

## What genuine customization would have to change

Proposed input/output map; these are design hypotheses, not a validated questionnaire. A qualified reviewer should define any clinical exclusions. Collect only what the service needs, explain how information is used, and avoid asking for a child's full identity or medical records just to create a grocery plan.

| Input | A material change in the report | Evidence or rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Country and ordinary supermarkets/ingredients | Locally obtainable ingredients and terminology | An Australian commenter reports mismatched foods; one original reviewer reports a gap in Chinese vegetables [aud01-0029] [rev01-0084] |
| What the parent actually ate last week | Plan built around an existing routine, including households without regular cooked meals | [aud01-0022] [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030] |
| Available preparation time and equipment | A realistic number of preparation sessions and a low-effort fallback | Workload is the expressed problem; exact thresholds need interviews [aud01-0015] |
| Weekly shopping rhythm | Entire week's ingredients visible before shopping; alternatives for missing ingredients | [rev01-0093] [rev01-0108] |
| Chosen feeding approach and qualified readiness guidance | Consistent, reviewed content rather than a questionnaire-generated readiness decision | [guide01-003] |
| Who prepares/serves meals and childcare rules | Clear task ownership and a printable handoff sheet | [aud01-0019] [aud01-0024] |
| Feedback on what was actually used | One bounded revision to planning logistics, with clinical questions routed separately | A plan's use matters more than length; revision scope is a proposed service choice [rev01-0085] |

An answer that changes no substantive recommendation should not be sold as personalization. Do not treat an automatic two-week age offset as a developmental assessment. This is a product-quality judgment informed by the competitor assessment flow and capability complaints. [mkt01-0004] [rev01-0025]

The map above is a minimum useful specification, not a novel feature list: current competitors already advertise most of it. The proposed reason to choose Mamivio must be demonstrated with low-cooking households using actual outputs side by side. It could turn out that a free Little Lunches preview or an existing 28-day guide is the better answer. [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0009]

## A concrete validation sequence

Proposed future work only. No recruitment, messages, orders, ads or payments were performed in this research.

1. **Interview 12 adults at the trigger stage**, including people with little cooking routine, people who already use a paid planner, and people content with free tools. Twelve is an operational batch choice, not statistical sufficiency. Confirm whether they identify as first-time mothers rather than inferring it. Ask them to show a recent shopping/planning difficulty and what they actually used or bought.
2. **Show a small report preview alongside good substitutes.** Compare against the current free preparation database, Little Lunches' free initial plan, the $29 first-month guide and My Little Food Critic's 28-day guide. Ask which decision the proposed report removes and which existing product could do it. Do not hide that competitors already personalize. The experiment must show an advantage for a low-cooking household, not merely preference for a polished mockup. [mkt01-0014] [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008]
3. **Fulfill a small, qualified-reviewed manual pilot before automation.** A proposed batch of five households is a feasibility check, not an efficacy study. Measure preparation/review/support minutes, whether users open it while shopping, what they substitute, and whether another caregiver can use it. A medical outcome or developmental acceleration is not the pilot endpoint.
4. **Test price after defining the costed service.** For a US-dollar pilot, $19 and $29 are hypotheses to test against the observed $29 guide anchor; a reviewed/high-support service may need a different price. Do not transfer these amounts automatically to the UK, Canada or Australia. Proposed thresholds are not observed willingness to pay. [mkt01-0014]
5. **Require a real payment decision before calling the niche validated.** At the later sales stage, use a clear deliverable, turnaround, review scope and refund policy. Track qualified visitors, purchases, refunds and support costs separately. An email signup, favorable interview or request for a checklist is not a sale. [aud01-0032]

Unit economics must include review and support: **contribution per order = collected revenue − refunds − taxes/remittance where applicable − payment fees − preparation/review labor − support/revision cost − acquisition cost**. No numerical margin or acquisition ceiling can be inferred from the public evidence here. If those costs do not fit the chosen price, revise the service or reject the niche.

Stop/revise criteria are proposed decision rules: if interviewees choose the free substitute after seeing both; if the plan requires more cooking than their existing routine; if meaningful personalization cannot be delivered consistently; or if qualified review/support consumes the available margin, do not scale the report because the broad motherhood market looks large.

## Confidence and missing decisions

Confidence is moderate that **planning burden is a real expressed problem in the selected sources**, low that this proposed narrow audience will buy Mamivio, and very low on profitability. The strongest original evidence is selected app reviews; the narrow non-cooking segment rests on two indexed discussions with incomplete original context. Evidence is largely from parents without verified gender, country or purchase status, so it cannot establish a mothers-only market advantage. [rev01-0097] [rev01-0093] [aud01-0023] [aud01-0030]

Before committing: choose the first country, establish qualified content/review responsibility, compare the exact current competitor outputs, and obtain real buyer decisions. Keep postpartum household coordination as the leading alternative if feeding review is not feasible. The complete source and reading gaps remain explicit in `findings.md` and `session-report.md`.
