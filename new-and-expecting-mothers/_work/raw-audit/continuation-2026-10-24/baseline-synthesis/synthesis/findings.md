First-time motherhood and low cooking skill are no longer defining requirements. This is an editorial choice for a finite-report business, not a proven profitable segment. ## 1. The reader is defined by a task

The useful commonality is translating available information into an action that fits the actual household. One parent already has Solid Starts yet wants an exact starting sequence. Another already cooks and freezes family dinners but cannot manage baby lunch during the day. A third normally enjoys cooking but finds infant meal preparation stressful. These support a workflow problem, not a general claim that new mothers cannot cook. [rjnp01-0072] [odeep03-0043] [rjfc01-0097]

“I find planning and preparing food for him to be weirdly stressful. I normally love to cook.” [rjfc01-0097]

The original non-cooking hypothesis now has full-original evidence too: one household cooks separately for the baby while adults order takeout. A different Q&A household rejects a 24-hour preparation suggestion when the adults need an immediate meal fallback. That child is thirteen months, so this second case is adjacent evidence rather than proof of a first-solids persona. [rjfc01-0003] [qdeep01-0101] [qdeep01-0102]

Many authors do not identify as mothers or state first-child status. Mother-focused recruitment is the owner's proposed audience, not a measured demographic advantage.

## 2. Several task arcs, rather than one universal journey

| Trigger | What happens next in the selected cases | What a finite aid might organize |
|---|---|---|
| First foods or more regular baby meals | A reference app/book leaves sequencing, lunch or ingredient-use questions | One visible shop/preparation cycle [rjnp01-0072] [odeep03-0080] |
| Birth or another child's arrival | Lists and supplies exist, but helpers offer different work than needed | Agreed tasks, arrival timing and realistic support [rjpg01-0002] [rjpg01-0042] |
| Return to work or a caregiver change | Work hours, nursery availability, room setup and routines fail to line up | Review of an existing draft with confirmed inputs [rjfc01-0041] [rjfc01-0042] [odeep01-0047] |
| Routine, sleep or activity planning | A plan adds work or becomes unusable after a deviation; some parents abandon it | Fewer decisions and recovery examples, without outcome guarantees [rjnp01-0143] [rjnp01-0131] |

These are interpretations of selected stories, not prevalence estimates or clinical timelines.

## 3. What other people assume

The family-meal shortcut assumes adults already cook suitable meals at a compatible time. Help offers can assume the parent wants baby holding when the unfilled task is dog care. A shared schedule can assume a caregiver can or will follow it. Those assumptions are commercially relevant because they can survive a more detailed document. [rjfc01-0003] [rjnp01-0081] [rjpg01-0002] [rjnp01-0082]

A successful free contrast is a reusable packing list that lets a partner pack independently. A Trello board, repeated checklists and a shared registry sheet fail to create initiative or compliance in other accounts. The plausible mechanism is an agreed task made easier to execute; a report cannot manufacture agreement. [rjpg01-0004] [rjpg01-0005] [rjpg01-0006] [rjpg01-0007]

## 5. The other side of the household

Relatives and caregivers reveal different jobs. A grandparent's handmade cloths end up used differently than intended; another wants somewhere local to try a pram physically. A shower helper faces unassigned costs and work. A nanny onboarding account includes routes, household expectations and hands-on setup. These are not interchangeable new-mother testimonials. [odeep01-0030] [odeep01-0056] [rpay01-0013] [odeep02-0072]

Fathers and partners also describe workplace constraints, shared records and motives beyond immediate net pay. A financial calculator alone can miss independence, career continuity and time together. Historical peer benefit or employment claims are not current advice. [odeep04-0038] [odeep03-0018] [odeep05-0045]

## 6. What they ask, want and fear

The requests are often concrete: a starting food, lunch without another cooking session, ingredients that do not go to waste, a base schedule to adapt, a compatible component, or instructions for a rejected reimbursement claim. These are better intake questions than a generic measure of overwhelm. [rjnp01-0072] [odeep03-0043] [odeep03-0080] [rjfc01-0063] [odeep03-0068] [rjpg01-0037]

“I don't really have time to prepare him anything tidy during the day so I end up giving him a jar” [odeep03-0043]

Clarity does not establish willingness to pay. Free-only requests and explicit inability to afford an app remain material contrary evidence. A one-off fee cannot automatically solve an affordability problem. [rjpg01-0027] [rpay01-0007] [rpay01-0009]

## 7. What existing help already supplies

Free forums, library books, trial kits, manufacturer information, retailer questionnaires, ordinary household adaptations and AI already solve some jobs. Other parents dislike AI or want lived dialogue even while valuing it. Their experiences do not justify assuming either universal AI rejection or universal acceptance. [odeep04-0012] [odeep05-0032] [odeep03-0103] [qdeep01-0051] [rjfc01-0007] [rjfc01-0052]

The free base-schedule example was useful, but it depended on a spouse, nanny, housekeeper and early-morning productivity. Those resources must travel with the example; copying its timetable alone would misrepresent its fit. [rjfc01-0062] [rjfc01-0063]

## 8. The market is already personalized

There are **41 competitor/substitute notes from 48 fully read public offer pages**, and **20 Ad Library searches with 68 retained IDs' visible text read**. These are discovered offers and advertising observations, not a global census or profitability evidence.

Food planning has direct substitutes: a $29 first-month guide, a 28-day guide, household meal/grocery apps and a generator advertising plans across 1–100 days. Little Lunches already advertises caregiver access. A short duration, grocery list or personalization claim is not an established feature gap. [mkt01-0014] [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0013] [mkt02-0016]

Registry tailoring also exists free: The Starter Set advertises personalized choices/priorities, and Babylist advertises a lifestyle checklist plus free consultants. Current Starter Set ads promote free recommendations. Paid registry services bundle conversation, registry work and follow-up. [mkt04-0001] [mkt04-0002] [addeep01-0001] [mkt03-0004] [mkt03-0005]

The closest explicit report analogue is MyMatLeave's advertised $249, human-reviewed twelve-page California PDF. Planned advertises a $69 business-specific leave tool; a UK editable maternity/nursery spreadsheet is advertised at £5.99. These demonstrate formats and competing offers, not customer purchases or Mamivio pricing. Leave expertise, current local rules and support are substantive parts of those offers. [mkt03-0001] [mkt03-0002] [mkt03-0003] [addeep01-0002]

## 9. Segments that sound different

Competent cooks still have lunch/snack workflow gaps; non-cooking households cannot simply share adult dinner. Experienced parents can forget feeding details or confront changed products. Two-parent households with substantial leave differ from parents coordinating rotating carers. Physical space and transport can dominate gear decisions. None of these source counts establishes the relative size of the segments. [odeep03-0043] [rjfc01-0003] [odeep04-0054] [odeep03-0088] [rpay01-0012] [rjnp01-0128] [odeep02-0015]

Hong Kong/expat material adds language, shipping, helpers and small-space constraints. It is historical, primarily 2004–2022, and cannot establish present local availability or prices. [odeep02-0086] [odeep02-0088] [odeep02-0091]

## 10. Does this support Mamivio's method?

**It supports testing useful, constraint-dependent outputs. It does not validate age selection alone, a fixed PDF format or the existing developmental claims.** The product brief records unsupported numerical/scientific promises and unverified specialist/testimonial claims. Official guidance is separate from customer demand. [mkt01-0004] [mkt01-0006] [guide01-003]

The stronger customization inputs are what the household already does, when it shops, what equipment/space exists, who executes the task, and what happens when the plan changes. A finite report earns value only if it removes work without adding an unrealistic new routine. An actual custom sleep plan could still feel too burdensome to implement. [rjnp01-0131] [rjnp01-0143] [odeep03-0080]

## 11. Objections and failure conditions

The evidence-based objections are: I already have enough information; the free example works; the baby or equipment may not fit; another adult will not follow it; I need observation or physical help; I cannot afford it; and the app/report adds work. Treat those as possible reasons to reject the offer, not merely persuasion problems. [rjfc01-0002] [rjfc01-0063] [odeep02-0037] [rjpg01-0005] [rjfc01-0077] [rjpg01-0027] [rjnp01-0131]

Prepared food, childcare and someone doing a task can be the preferred purchase. A report that requires more cooking or management may be the wrong product. [odeep02-0045] [odeep05-0031] [fdeep02-0010]
