# Which mothers should Mamivio test a customized report with?

2026-10-24. **Start with mothers introducing regular baby meals who already have feeding information but lack a workable weekly lunch and preparation routine.** Test a short applied plan around existing food, equipment, shopping and caregivers. Include confident cooks and experienced parents. The research supports this as the best first experiment for the requested finite-report format; it does not establish a profitable winner. [rjnp01-0072] [rjfc01-0097] [odeep03-0043] [odeep03-0080]

English-speaking markets are the owner's preference. Country, qualifications, acquisition budget and actual customers remain unspecified. Findings come from the [1,296-record database](counts.md) and [completed reading ledger](session-report.md), with no Mamivio sale, report-price acceptance or measured acquisition cost.

## What changed from the initial recommendation

The first pass leaned on two indexed accounts to define a first-time, low-cooking persona. The deeper originals show a broader constraint: some adults rarely cook, some cook good dinners but lack baby lunches, and some enjoy cooking but find infant meal planning stressful. **The missing routine is the useful distinction; cooking skill and first-child status are recruitment questions.** [rjfc01-0003] [odeep03-0043] [rjfc01-0097] [odeep04-0054]

A fixed 28-day PDF is also unproven as the desired format. Existing offers already include 28-day guides and personalized multi-day plans. A shorter, fully visible week with substitutions is a product hypothesis designed for the observed workload, not a discovered empty category. [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0013] [rjnp01-0143]

Postpartum household coordination moves down as a **bare checklist** opportunity: Trello, written routines and spreadsheets already failed where participation or agreement was missing. Registry selection remains a real job, but current free competitors already personalize it. [rjpg01-0005] [rjpg01-0006] [rjnp01-0082] [mkt04-0001] [mkt04-0002]

## The lead buyer and trigger

The buyer has reached an ordinary baby-meal planning problem: what to serve at lunch, what to buy before the next shop, how to use remaining ingredients, or how to prepare without creating a separate cooking project. They may already use Solid Starts or own a book. Starting solids and the move to more regular meals are different moments; the first pilot should select one. A proposed recruitment range around six to twelve months is an operational hypothesis, not a readiness assessment or evidence of superior purchasing behavior. [rjnp01-0072] [odeep03-0043] [odeep03-0080]

The strongest illustrative cases are specific:

- A parent already has Solid Starts but wants an exact starting sequence and reports ordering prepared food. That is a reference-to-action gap and an order, not a report purchase. [rjnp01-0072] [rjnp01-0073]
- An eight-month-old's parent cooks family dinner and freezes extras; daytime lunch is still hard. A ten-month-old's parent has tried cookbook/online snacks but wastes leftover ingredients. [odeep03-0043] [odeep03-0080]
- A seven-and-a-half-month-old's parent normally loves cooking yet finds the planning/preparation stressful. Their child's temporary illness is not the marketable need. [rjfc01-0097]
- One household cooks only for the baby while adults eat takeout. A separate thirteen-month case rejects 24-hour preparation when an immediate fallback is needed; that older case informs workflow without proving first-solids demand. [rjfc01-0003] [qdeep01-0101] [qdeep01-0102]

The corresponding exclusion is important: a child refusing food, having pain or needing clinical assessment is not an ordinary shopping/preparation problem. One apparently relevant forum story later reached a reported GP diagnosis. Qualified review must define product limits rather than a questionnaire claiming to diagnose the child. [odeep05-0023] [guide01-003]

## What the report would actually do

Proposed specification, not researched customer preferences or a validated questionnaire:

| Input | A material output change | Evidence behind the input |
|---|---|---|
| What adults actually ate last week; existing baby meals | Reuse the real household routine, including a fallback when adults do not cook | [rjfc01-0003] [qdeep01-0101] |
| Available weekday preparation time; equipment already owned | A realistic number of preparation blocks, with minimal extra equipment | [odeep03-0043] [rjpg01-0027] |
| Shopping day, local ingredients and available quantities | One visible list, repeated ingredients and substitutions rather than many single-use purchases | [odeep03-0080] [rjpg01-0028] |
| Existing feeding approach and qualified guidance | Use reviewed standard examples within scope; do not generate a readiness, allergy or treatment protocol | [guide01-003] |
| Who prepares and serves lunch; different homes or care days | A brief agreed handoff with task ownership and fallback choices | [rjfc01-0041] [rjpg01-0004] |
| A missed shopping/preparation step or changed day | A recovery example that preserves a useful routine instead of invalidating the whole plan | [rjnp01-0143] [rjnp01-0144] |

A plausible first deliverable is **one week visible in advance, one shopping/preparation page, a small set of repeatable meal options and a short caregiver/fallback page**. Offer an editable companion if the pilot needs it. Page count and exact revision scope are design choices to test. Do not collect a child's full identity or detailed medical records just to organize groceries.

Personalization must alter decisions. A name, age or two-week offset on a template does not establish that the plan fits this household. The present Mamivio product brief documents age-selected development plans and unverified scientific claims; those claims cannot transfer into a new food offer.

## Why this is the first test, and why it can fail

The category has reported information spending: an annual food-app subscription and a purchased BLW book. Public sellers offer one-time meal guides as well as apps. The task is concrete enough to preview and observe during one shopping/preparation cycle. Those are reasons to test, rather than reasons to call demand proven. [rpay01-0004] [rjfc01-0002] [mkt01-0014]

The strongest contrary evidence travels with the recommendation. One purchased book was enough for two children; another parent cannot afford the app; twin tracking is explicitly requested free. Prepared food or actual household help may be the better purchase. A plan requiring more cooking than the parent wants would reproduce the problem. [rjfc01-0002] [rjpg01-0027] [rpay01-0009] [odeep02-0045]

**The feature bundle is already competitive.** Little Lunches advertises household preferences, grocery planning, ingredient reuse and caregiver access. My Little Food Critic sells a 28-day guide; the delivered file format was not inspected. Orango advertises generated plans across 1–100 days. The unproven advantage is finite, low-friction fulfillment around one household, not a novel menu or grocery feature. [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0009] [mkt02-0013] [mkt02-0016]

## A concrete validation sequence

Proposed next work only. This research performed no recruitment, messages, accounts, orders or advertising.

1. **Choose one country and one meal-planning moment.** Keep the first grocery/measurement vocabulary and qualified content consistent. Do not simultaneously sell a first-food sequence, older-baby lunches, postpartum recovery and clinical feeding help.
2. **Interview an operational batch of twelve adults.** Include existing paid-tool users, satisfied free-resource users, competent cooks and takeout households. Twelve is a manageable batch, not statistical sufficiency. Ask to see the last real planning problem, existing tools and actual purchases; ask who does the work.
3. **Show a concrete applied preview beside strong substitutes.** Compare with current free app resources and a one-time guide, not only a blank page. Determine which decision the sample removes and whether the parent wants a document, editable sheet, observation or prepared food. Existing competitor outputs and post-purchase contents have not been evaluated here.
4. **Fulfill a small paid manual pilot before automation.** A proposed five households checks feasibility and use, not efficacy. Log preparation/review/support minutes, whether the plan is used before shopping, substitutions, unused ingredients and whether another caregiver can execute it. Clinical outcomes and milestone acceleration are not success measures.
5. **Price the costed service, then measure purchases.** For a hypothetical US-dollar test, $29 one-off is a candidate anchored to an observed $29 first-month guide, not established willingness to pay. It is only sensible if preparation, qualified review, revisions, fees and acquisition fit. A higher-support service may require a different price. Do not transfer a dollar amount to every English-speaking market. [mkt01-0014]

There is no CPC, search-volume, click-through or conversion dataset here. Public competitor ads establish advertising messages, not a viable acquisition cost. A later acquisition test must track qualified visitors, paid orders, refunds and support costs; email interest and requests for free plans are different outcomes. The main practical acquisition hypothesis is reaching parents already looking for a meal plan or evaluating an upgrade, without assuming forum questions are a traffic forecast.

Contribution per order must cover variable fulfillment, qualified review, support/revision, payment/refund costs and acquisition from net collected revenue. No numerical margin can be estimated from these public sources.

Reject or revise the offer if parents choose the free substitute after seeing both, prefer food/help over instructions, cannot use the plan without more work, do not pay for the concrete deliverable, or require review/support that the price cannot sustain. The archive provides the problems and language; a costed purchase/use test must establish the business.
