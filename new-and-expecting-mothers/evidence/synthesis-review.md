## Material weaknesses that remain

### 1. Current competitors closely match the proposed artifact

The proposed combination of family preferences, shared ingredients, grocery lists and caregiver access cannot establish differentiation. [Little Lunches' captured meal-plan page](https://www.littlelunches.com/en/mealplans) describes those functions, including a 6–8 month starting-solids stage, pantry reuse and adjustable preferences. Its [captured pricing page](https://www.littlelunches.com/en/pricing) advertises an initial free plan and website prices of $7.99/month or $49.99/year. These are seller descriptions and offer prices, not independently demonstrated output quality or paid adoption. Raw: `_raw/competitors/little-lunches.txt`, `little-lunches--pricing.txt`.

[My Little Food Critic's captured product page](https://mylittlefoodcritic.com/products/28-day-meal-plan) sells a literal 28-day first-solids plan with purée/finger-food presentation options. The capture displays €34.95; this is a localized price, not a universal dollar anchor. Raw: `_raw/competitors/my-little-food-critic-28day.txt`.

The first-wave sources already establish a $29 first-month guide, tailored first-food planning and diet-filtered menus [mkt01-0014, mkt01-0026, mkt01-0027]. The second wave makes the comparison closer still. These competitors do not prove Mamivio cannot win. They do mean that the experiment must test an advantage specifically for a parent with little cooking routine, rather than whether people like having a meal plan.

**Required handoff check:** the draft already refers to the second-wave inventory. At the initial audit, those raw captures existed but the corresponding completed teardown entries and final inventory update had not yet landed. Before final delivery, replace that forward reference with the actual named comparisons and evidence ids/links. Do not leave a vague promise that family planners were compared. This review does not assign or change their counts while the market branch is finishing.

### 2. First-child status and the recruitment stage are proposed choices

The crucial grab-and-go account is from a single mother of an eight-month-old who has already been serving purées for about two months. The other relevant account concerns a six-and-a-half-month-old shortly after starting solids. Neither account identifies first-child status. [aud01-0022, aud01-0023, aud01-0030; raw units `_raw/communities/corpus-index/015.txt`, `018.txt`]

The narrow first-time-mother/first-month framing is therefore not an observed segment definition. The draft correctly labels the age band and first-time restriction as recruitment hypotheses. The body now also explains the ages of the actual supporting cases. That is adequate for an experimental recommendation; it would not support copy claiming these are proven buyers or the main audience.

### 3. The strongest paid-plan evidence is historical and clinically complicated

The original review supporting payment for a directed plan describes a 2024 app change and a separately purchased PDF [rev01-0097]. The unused approximately 200-page plan account also concerns a baby with dietary complications [rev01-0085]. The advance-shopping complaint concerns allergen-introductory meals in a 2025 review [rev01-0093]. Those are useful experiences, but they are not a present-day usability comparison of ordinary family meal planners, and they do not measure demand from the proposed low-cooking segment.

The draft already warns that product versions, purchase status and payment claims are unverified. Context was strengthened so a reader does not mistake historical or allergy-specific experiences for a current generic missing feature. Actual plan use, qualified review burden and real payment still need testing.

### 4. The ranking remains an editorial choice

The audience corpus supports planning burden across several jobs. It does not supply comparable purchase probabilities, acquisition costs or margins that rank first solids above postpartum household coordination. Current competitors reduce the apparent novelty of first solids, while postpartum planning also has direct professional-service competitors. A conditional first-solids pilot is reasonable; a categorical claim that it is the best business is not supported.

The draft handles this limitation well: it labels the ordering editorial, makes qualified feeding review a condition, keeps household coordination as the alternative, and requires a real payment decision. Preserve those conditions in the final chat summary, where the headline recommendation may otherwise sound more certain than the files.
