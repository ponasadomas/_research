# Follow-up market wave — what weakens the first-30-days hypothesis

2026-10-24. Seven new notes, eleven successful first-party page reads, sixteen new seller excerpts. All five named follow-ups are covered: Starting Solids: BLW & Purees, Annabel Karmel, My Little Food Critic, BabyPlate and Little Lunches. Two closely related offers were added because they directly test the proposed report: My Little Food Critic's 28-day guide and Orango Labs' chosen-duration plan generator. No buyer-prevalence or commercial-success estimate is possible from these pages.

## The differentiation assumption does not survive the wider offer inventory

Calling a report household-specific does not by itself distinguish it. Little Lunches describes family ages from six months, selected meal slots, dietary restrictions and changing preferences; automatic groceries, pantry reuse and shared caregiver planning are already part of its described offer. Its public web price is $7.99/month or $49.99/year, with a free-plan starting point. This is a feature/price observation, not evidence that a generated plan is good or that parents prefer its app. [mkt02-0006] [mkt02-0008] [mkt02-0009] [mkt02-0010]

Food culture is not an empty segment either. BabyPlate explicitly lists halal, kosher, vegan and vegetarian setup, and describes weekly ideas adapting to food history. Those claims require actual product and specialist review before judging their quality or compliance. They nevertheless challenge a claim that the existing market ignores cultural diet and prior foods. [mkt02-0005] [mkt02-0011] [mkt02-0012]

Duration is already sold as a format. My Little Food Critic offers a 28-day guide; its captured product page gives both purée and finger-food serving choices. Orango's developer listing goes further, describing generation from one to 100 days, fit to family diet and conversion of a selected date range into groceries. Orango has no rating overview in the current listing, so it illustrates direct feature competition and imitation risk, not validated demand. [mkt02-0004] [mkt02-0007] [mkt02-0013] [mkt02-0016]

Established recipe apps also cover ordinary manual customization: Annabel Karmel has family meal planners, and My Little Food Critic builds weekly menus/shopping lists with dietary and quick-meal categories. A report cannot assume that parents' only alternatives are unrelated blog posts. [mkt02-0002] [mkt02-0003] [mkt02-0014] [mkt02-0015]

## What remains plausible — interpretation, not a proven gap

A finite report could suit parents who want a small number of decisions made once, a printable shopping/preparation sheet, and a version another caregiver can use. The present sources do not establish that these parents will pay instead of using the described apps or an existing four-week guide. Caregiver sharing itself is already supplied in Starting Solids and Little Lunches. [mkt02-0001] [mkt02-0010]

The next test should compare an actual proposed report with these alternatives, not ask whether parents like the abstract idea of personalization. A useful comparison would ask parents preparing for solids to choose between their current method, a relevant app/free resource, a fixed guide, and a concrete limited report. Observe which constraints change the choice: household food, preparation time, shopping effort, clarity for a second caregiver, desire to avoid ongoing logging and whether later changes would make a static plan stale. These are proposed validation variables; current seller pages do not measure them.

The broader niche recommendation should remain conditional. Market evidence supports a real problem category and many available solutions. It does **not** identify a defensible untapped first-solids subniche, prove a report outperforms an app, or establish willingness to pay Mamivio. Postpartum household planning and work-return handoff remain useful comparison hypotheses, but their own paid demand and ability to deliver actual help are also unvalidated.

## Freshness, trust and health boundaries

My Little Food Critic's original app page says £1.49/$1.49 a month, while its US App Store lists $3.99. Its 28-day guide served €34.95 in this environment, versus £27.99 in an indexed UK result. Use the captured location/term; do not present one universal cheapest price. Annabel Karmel's website robots exclude GPTBot; its App Store developer listing supplied the inspected offer instead. BabyPlate makes specific numerical allergen-protocol assertions. They are seller claims, not guidance reviewed by this reader, and must not be copied into a Mamivio report or used to guarantee safe introduction. Religious diet compliance, nutrition adequacy and feeding safety were not tested for any app. Orango's privacy/local-data claims were also not independently tested. Described functionality is a floor for competitive comparison, not medical or security validation.

## Coverage and continuation

All retained successful page text in this follow-up wave was read. For five pages, exact lines already read in earlier captures were folded in saved reading views; all remaining distinct lines were read, and original HTML/text remains untouched. This removes duplicated navigation/review copies without sampling the offer or silently dropping substantive text. See `sources/competitors-wave2.md` for paths and method.

No paid app content, actual generated plans, checkouts or buyer interviews were examined. Affiliate marketplaces were probed and remain unharvested, with exact URLs/statuses in `sources/marketplaces.md`. The seven notes close this named follow-up wave, not the entire changing app category. Remaining related-app names are discovery leads; no invented score or missing-feature claim is drawn from them.
