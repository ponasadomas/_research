# Expanded parenting forum reading

## Scope and reading status

The access sweep examined 54 candidate URL/host variants (51 after collapsing www), then captured every page of 8 finite JustParents boards. Reconciliation gives **505 unique visible threads,530 thread pages and 3,472 posts**, not the 605 incorrectly reported in an early progress message. Each individual board count was correct; the early sum was wrong. All 505 cards were locally triaged before treating the capture as a kept reading corpus. Exact source / triage / read state is in `sources/forums-deep.md` and `sources/forums-deep-effective-kept-manifest.json`.

All **17 retained chunks, 94 provisionally retained threads and 706 original posts/responses** have now been fully read across all visible saved pages. Final reading excludes 16 more discussions, leaving **78 effective retained threads/555 posts, all fully read and none unread**. These are distinct from the 505 captured raw discussions: 411 were dropped at card triage, then 16 after full reading. All raw/all-board material remains preserved. Exact SHA256 chunk ledgers and per-thread outcomes establish reading; downloading does not. Dates are often relative in the original page; approximate original years describe an archive, not exact reconstructed timestamps.

## Feeding — complete retained read

**The new evidence strengthens practical coordination as a job, while weakening any claim that confusion alone will sell a customized report.** The work-transition examples involve shift length, actual pumping output, bottle acceptance, childminder arrangements, workplace breaks and partner feeds. A generic report would repeat the very failure some parents describe in free replies. In jp-10762 the OP plans 12 hour day/night shifts and has tried different pumps; after being told to rest and improve diet, the OP points out those problems were never reported. This supports asking about the actual situation and avoiding assumptions. It does not show a report can solve expressing difficulty or replace qualified feeding support. Quote fdeep01-0001.

Some spending is observable as **equipment use, failed purchases or purchase intent**, with no report transaction. In jp-9037 a respondent bought expensive bottles intended to mimic breastfeeding and found them unsuccessful (fdeep01-0002). In jp-4091 a seller reports paying £60 and offers resale at £30; the OP expresses interest and asks whether particular bottles fit. The sale is not confirmed, and the historical peer price is not a current offer (fdeep01-0003). In jp-4483 the buyer wants a dual pump that connects to mains, considers a £50 listing, and rejects a suggested pump that fails the dual requirement. Compatibility and actual household/work constraints matter more than a broad “best pump” ranking. No price here validates willingness to buy a report.

The most useful **first-solids counterevidence** is in jp-2967. A respondent owns an Annabel Karmel book, has repeatedly browsed it but has never used a recipe, and instead uses own recipes or the family meal. The same respondent likes convenient ready-made pasta. This is book ownership/non-use, not verified book purchase or a general verdict on the brand (fdeep01-0006). Another participant says free forum advice and trial-and-error may be more useful than a book. Later beetroot-related replies are challenged by other members as possible coordinated promotion; those contested voices are excluded from audience quotes. The ordinary book-nonuse reply comes from a separate regular participant, not the contested beetroot testimonials. A report must reduce an actual task; supplying more reading or recipe choice may make the problem worse.

In jp-8593 the OP asks what child-led weaning means, receives a free website link, and then says the existing approach already works and the term had merely been heard. That is a clean example of **curiosity resolved without changing method or buying information**, not a request for a new plan (fdeep01-0005). In jp-10094 “wean” partly refers to stopping breastfeeding, while several replies interpret it as introducing solids. A simple keyword tally would mix different jobs. In jp-5496 the first weeks of integrating milk and meals do create a concrete routine question; the OP later says intake preference has changed again. A fixed calendar can become stale quickly. None of the advice in these old replies is adopted as current feeding guidance.

The **free/human-support alternative** is substantial. jp-9629 recommends a feeding expert before birth and describes prior positive experience; the reported free/cheap cost is a peer claim, not a verified current service price (fdeep01-0004). jp-6817's OP finishes by planning to insist on help from midwife/health visitor. Several replies recommend local groups, phone support and library books. jp-2432 ends with the OP crediting free peer reassurance for a decision (fdeep01-0011). This argues for a report that organises practical questions/contacts and handoffs, rather than claiming informational certainty or manufacturing guilt.

Work constraints also limit what a report can change. In jp-4514 an expecting trainee nurse has breaks but considers another career because of time away from the baby (fdeep01-0007). That preference and workplace structure do not disappear when a pumping timetable is clearer. jp-2170 fears feeding will leave insufficient time for 3 existing children; other parents disagree about how difficult it was and point out both breast and bottle feeding take work (fdeep01-0008). The first expecting parent's information overload in jp-1646 is real (fdeep01-0009), but respondents mostly seek actual support and practical experience. These are hypotheses for interviews, not proof of a purchasable report format.

## Post-read exclusions and source quality

The apparently useful father daytime-feeding question jp-8076 is excluded from marketing evidence. It is about clinical milk/intake safety, and another user challenges the author's child-age timeline; the archive does not resolve that inconsistency. Do not cite it as strong validated father-handoff demand. The full thread and responses remain preserved. jp-1770 is also excluded after full reading because clinical/prematurity/medication and distressing feeding stories dominate. It is not an ethical segment-selection asset. These 2 drops are recorded individually in `sources/forums-deep-feeding-final.jsonl`.

Across retained threads, unsupported clinical directions, historical legal claims, duplicate quoted posts, formula-vendor promotion, probiotic promotion and testimonial-looking replies are not independent evidence of parent need or effective treatment. No explicit minor is quoted. The observed benefit of full-thread reading is concrete: it finds OP resolution, actual constraints, later changed circumstances and credibility challenges missing from a search card or page 1 excerpt.

## Preparation — complete retained read

All 11 provisional preparation threads/46 posts were fully read across both frozen chunks. Two further drops follow full reading: jp-9996 exposes severe mental-health, housing and financial vulnerability; jp-9960 is clinical birth stories and emergency care. Neither supplies marketing quotes. The other 9 remain as practical preparation/context or counterexamples, with individual outcomes in `sources/forums-deep-preparation-final.jsonl`.

jp-9382 asks a specific household question at 35 weeks: how to prepare a 2.5 year old and 10 month old for another baby while preserving parental attention. The constraint is siblings and who gets attention, not a generic checklist. In jp-9894 the parent forgot personal belongings, including glasses that the partner fetched from home; another respondent describes forgetting their own items despite preparing for the child. Yet the same free crowd checklist includes parents packed months early and a respondent who needed little of what had been packed. A concise personalized packing action could reduce an errand, but the thread also shows a readily available free substitute.

The human/free alternatives remain prominent. The doula discussion jp-10061 has an OP whose partner already supplied coaching; a respondent used hospital antenatal classes and YouTube and felt no doula was needed. This is a reported substitute, not measured outcome or verified free service pricing. The overwhelmed first parent in jp-9791 responds to too much online information by explicitly wanting a knowledgeable person face to face and seeking events/groups. jp-9608 asks for pregnant friends and chat. Neither should be rewritten as a request for more pages in a report.

The product must also accommodate ordinary confidence and preferences. jp-8327's late-pregnancy parent is excited with the bag and car seat already ready. In jp-9954 one person dislikes a full body pillow because it is uncomfortable and occupies bed space, while several others value theirs. That is evidence of differing physical preferences and product tradeoffs, not something a report can promise to settle. No preparation thread establishes paid-report demand.

## Activities and first trips — complete retained read

All 12 provisional threads/68 posts were read across both chunks. The old airport-security argument jp-8662 is excluded after reading; its unsupported policy/explosives discussion is not usable guidance or commercial evidence. Eleven remain as practical/adjacent cases, with their ages kept explicit. In particular, jp-6995's followup reveals a 3 year old and jp-707 explicitly concerns a 2 year old. These are not infant-play demand.

jp-707 is the clearest example of why context matters: a parent in a flat without a garden or car needs holiday activities when usual toddler groups close. A nearby soft play suggestion fails because its toddler area is too small and the child wants the older area. A long free answer supplies many ideas, other participants appreciate them, and the site asks to turn it into a free article with permission. A generic list already has a free substitute; a constrained selection by transport, space, age and availability might be the value. This is adjacent toddler evidence, and it does not prove willingness to pay.

The baby outing request jp-1307 caps the activity budget at £10; free and discounted local options are suggested. jp-9878 asks for free/nearly free discoveries and gets existing directories. The library discussion jp-6995 has a preschooler enjoying a regular programme, but another family's competing group schedule prevents attendance. Cost, actual local availability and schedule fit are more concrete than a generic need for stimulation. Do not count a free programme recommendation as a current verified service price or a paid report purchase.

First trips expose another bounded job for future interviews. jp-8027 asks what to pack for a first weekend visit with a 7 week baby; the free reply recommends a simple checklist. jp-1157 has an already-booked 7 hour flight with a 1 year old and questions about food, packing and onboard activities. jp-1789 has a 14 month child, a booked serviced campsite and train travel that limits baggage. In jp-8555 the OP has bought ready-sterilised bottles to reduce immediate arrival work and handle delays. That is tangible convenience spending, not report spending; no sterilisation or old airline advice is adopted. These cases justify asking about a first-trip packing/handoff product, not ranking it above the existing hypotheses from one old forum.

Counterexamples matter: jp-918 describes a relaxed trip with a 5 month baby and helpful venue staff proactively identifying baby facilities. jp-100 contains sharply different travel readiness preferences and easy trips as well as worry; one parent explicitly rejects routines. In jp-1130 the OP won event tickets and a respondent cannot afford a ticket. Neither paid attendance nor willingness to buy a report follows from that thread.

## Work/home choices — complete retained read

Both chunks and all 13 provisional threads/100 posts are fully read. Four are dropped after reading: pure job/startup or bingo-career discussions without specified early-parent care context (jp-3051, jp-4309, jp-2433), and a school-age work dispute involving prior controlling-partner history and later dismissal (jp-7103). The remaining 9 are mostly context/constraints and alternatives, not specific return-to-work handoff demand. This board's old work-at-home commercial purpose makes its raw thread count a particularly poor proxy for a new-mother report segment.

jp-8660 is a structural limit: the parent cares all day and works after bedtime but says childcare would erase earnings; a responding father describes the same household cost calculation. A report cannot create affordable care hours. jp-4332's OP needs income around childcare and rejects a generic direct-selling suggestion because the existing circle already buys from other representatives. Most later replies recruit or advertise earnings; those claims are not adopted. Several “payment” mentions elsewhere refer to earning from work or app testing, not buying information.

Actual preferences differ. jp-9858 contains people who miss adult conversation and colleagues yet prefer working at home, and others who report no disadvantages. jp-10545 says work after the child sleeps can reduce rest, but values time together. In jp-9889 visitors and family do not respect work time; a new-mother respondent says the partner assumes she does nothing. These experiences motivate questions about household cooperation and protected work time, not a promise that a more detailed schedule changes other adults' behaviour. Free peer exchanges and job choices are the observed alternatives. No thread confirms a paid-report transaction.

## Baby — complete retained read

All 27 provisional Baby threads/217 posts were read across all 5 chunks. Seven further drops follow full reading: commercial-link photoshoot wording (jp-10929), severe father distress and self-described depression (jp-10592), premature-twin feeding (jp-10171), rolling during sleep (jp-10184), sleep temperature/bedding (jp-8268), room-transition/safe-sleep discussion (jp-7232), and high distress plus harmful behavioural replies (jp-1762). No marketing quote comes from those discussions. jp-1762 eventually contains a £20 equipment intention, but the transaction is unconfirmed, the context is unsuitable for marketing use, and it is not report payment. Individual final outcomes preserve these distinctions.

Ordinary food questions are more varied than “which BLW calendar?”. jp-12170 asks what blender makes a completely smooth puree; first solids should not imply a single method (fdeep02-0009). jp-2726 has a 7 month child and already-bought baby pasta but questions about sauces and fillings; free recipes, family meals and an existing book are suggested (fdeep02-0014). jp-9172's parent considering a vegan child diet worries about preparation time; this supports a workload question, not an endorsement of a vegan infant diet or nutritional advice (fdeep02-0011). jp-4216 reports unsuccessful branded/unbranded cleaning products on food stains: the job is cleanup and household work, not another recipe (fdeep02-0013). In jp-9877 many replies concern older toddlers and changing preferences; they are not first-month solids proof.

Hands-on help and flexible physical solutions recur. A friend in jp-10503 asks how to help new parents without being overbearing; suggestions and experiences concern chores, food, company and protected rest. No report gift is purchased. jp-10012's OP eventually naps while the partner takes the child on errands (fdeep02-0010). jp-7461's OP shifts the main meal to a time the shift-working partner can help and credits peer solidarity; clinical history and distress are not used in marketing quotations. A report could make agreed tasks visible, but the relief comes from someone doing them.

Physical product choices also differ. jp-10027 describes carrier/wrap use to free hands for chores and siblings, alongside dislike, fit concerns, social discomfort and free groups/video help. No babywearing safety, weight-loss or sleepy-handling claim from replies is adopted. jp-5903's OP reports ordering a reusable-nappy pack, while another person declines over upfront cost and laundry (fdeep02-0012). In jp-9997 some parents have unused pacifiers because the baby refuses them. Broad “must buy” lists can waste money. The baby-signing thread jp-9482 reports class attendance and perceived communication benefit but does not verify fees or language-development effectiveness; a brand link also limits independence.

jp-9952 is an especially useful complete-thread counterexample. An apprehensive parent asks for first-flight tips for a 1 year old, receives free peer advice, then returns during the holiday to say it mostly went well; other passengers were kind (fdeep02-0015). A card alone would preserve anxiety and miss resolution. More travel questions in jp-9000 concern airline, feeds and packing, but the historical replies are not current policy guidance. jp-9219's first-birthday games question mixes infants and children up to 11 years, so a party plan would need heterogeneous ages. jp-2079 is simply a positive 3 month milestone celebration and sibling bond: no problem or buying need is inferred.

## Implication for the report hypothesis

This older single-forum archive cannot establish the current relative size of first solids, work transition, play or postpartum planning segments. Its selection and age prohibit a frequency-based market ranking. The findings support testing whether a **short household action plan** avoids extra research and helps other caregivers execute agreed decisions. They also require showing an owner what existing free information or books failed to do. They do not independently make a household-specific first 30 days solids report the best commercial niche. Paid-report demand is still unproved; actual report payment, price objections to a concrete sample, and use after purchase remain the discriminating tests.
