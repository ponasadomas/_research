# How to choose the report niche

Editorial framework for the reopened wave,2026-10-24. These are decision rules, not measured market scores or new customer claims. Quote frequency is not market size; selected painful stories are not purchases.

Evaluate each proposed segment against the same questions:

1. **Bounded decision and trigger.** What is the parent deciding this week? Registry publication, caregiver handoff, first booked trip, first grocery shop, and employer leave deadlines are different jobs. Generic maternal confidence has no bounded deliverable.
2. **Meaningful input-to-output changes.** Does country, actual equipment/home/car, current routine, other caregivers, budget or dates alter the recommendation? Merely putting a baby's name/age on a calendar is weak personalization.
3. **Observed solution spending, at its proper level.** Actual reported information purchase is stronger than an equipment purchase, and both differ from a question, conditional willingness or paid service requiring human judgment. None verifies Mamivio demand.
4. **Can a finite document do the work?** Compare a reusable report with editable spreadsheet, app, human conversation, physical trial or somebody doing a chore. If the missing input changes every day, a static PDF may be the wrong format.
5. **Strongest free and paid substitutes.** Compare current specific functions and delivered formats, not a generic assertion that existing advice is overwhelming. Include a do-nothing/satisfied-parent case.
6. **Expertise and fulfillment burden.** Current laws, benefits calculations, product safety/fit and clinical content require different review. Count research, verification, revision and support work before proposing a low price. Disclaimers do not substitute for a reliable method.
7. **What would falsify the idea?** A real parent choosing the existing free tool after seeing a concrete example; preference for physical help; a report requiring more work than it removes; no paid orders at a costed price; review/support destroying contribution margin.

No numerical opportunity score is assigned without measurable inputs. Seller prices show offers, not sales. Proposed launch prices must remain hypotheses. Acquiring interviews, accounts, ads and payments requires later explicit work; none was done during research.
