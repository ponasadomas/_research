They question whether advice will still apply when the baby's pattern changes next week. They have **not bought it**. National currency, named offer, actual intake, accepted fee and standalone PDF price are unknown. This is research-only, and the parent’s infant-care judgments are not clinical facts.

The narrow implication is that this account doubts the durability and value of a bundled service under changing circumstances. It does not establish “parents will pay $600 for a PDF,” a document-only preference, a price ceiling, demand for an update subscription, or a reason to launch a product. `rpa05-0016` separately shows a person paid for more support and remained dissatisfied; combining the two does not create evidence of a report purchase.

`rpa05-0013` seeks an experienced online human specialist after PLS/forums and explicitly wants help tailored to the child while preserving holiday money. That is prospective willingness to invest in a good person, not an observed transaction or preference for a static artifact. Stated generic desire for personalization is weaker than proof of what inputs change an output and what the customer actually paid for.
