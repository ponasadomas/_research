# Reader notes — original app and book reviews

2026-10-24. Interpretations below come from selected, platform-chosen reviews, not a representative customer sample. Source and full-read ledger: `sources/reviews-media.md`. 171 app excerpts + 9 book excerpts. Every cited id below was looked up in the JSONL, not reconstructed from memory.

## Most report-specific evidence

1. **A long paid PDF can be an unused purchase.** `rev01-0085` describes a parent who paid for a roughly 200-page first-food plan and never returned to it. This is one case, with a baby who also had dietary complications; it argues for testing usable length and time-to-action, not for avoiding all PDFs.
2. **The desired output is a next-action plan, not another library.** `rev01-0097` says the reason to subscribe was clear direction, and `rev01-0090` values meals mapped day by day with shopping guidance. `rev01-0093` says sequential meal unlocking obstructs grocery planning. Interpretation: a near-term food plan that shows the whole shopping week could be a distinct report job.
3. **Household customization must change the recommendations.** `rev01-0084` misses Chinese vegetables; `rev01-0107` objects to repetitive, nonseasonal meal plans; `rev01-0101` asks for meals using ingredients already available; `rev01-0110` values adaptable recipes; `rev01-0120` values a free food database. A report needs actual household choices, substitutions and shopping outputs beyond facts already free.
4. **Age is insufficient personalization.** `rev01-0025` explicitly requests filtering by capabilities; `rev01-0034` objects to textbook-age settings despite entered milestones. `rev01-0027` describes feeling falsely behind. These accounts do not validate any ability-assessment method; they test the credibility of age-only customization.
5. **Support can become another task.** `rev01-0042` describes obsessive logging; `rev01-0059` describes relief after stopping; `rev01-0123` compares app use to homework. `revb01-0008` objects to filler in a sleep book. Interpretation: reduce daily decisions and mandatory tracking rather than quantify parental adequacy.

## Other side and alternatives

Shared caregiving appears across parents, partners and grandparents: `rev01-0048`, `rev01-0054`, `rev01-0157`, `rev01-0163`, `rev01-0167`. Their job is to understand what happened without asking the mother to repeat it. This supports a possible handoff document, but many reviewers specifically value live synchronization. A static report cannot replace a live shared record and should not claim that it does.

Pumping has a concrete work-return trigger (`rev01-0142`) and values focused reminders, inventory and goal visibility (`rev01-0143`, `rev01-0145`, `rev01-0151`). Much of the value is ongoing measurement. A static report might support shift planning or workplace preparation, but it is a weaker substitute for live tracking and would require lactation expertise for physiological recommendations. No willingness to pay for that report was observed.

Some reviewers gladly pay recurring fees or continue with another child (`rev01-0113`, `rev01-0114`); others value free databases (`rev01-0083`, `rev01-0117`) or want a single payment (`rev01-0069`, `rev01-0153`). Do not turn subscription complaints into an unsupported claim that this audience uniformly prefers a one-time PDF. Likewise, smooth cancellation can strengthen trust (`rev01-0005`), and resolved historical problems should retain their updates (`rev01-0021`, `rev01-0035`, `rev01-0087`).

## Counterevidence to premature conclusions

- The strongest solids complaints concern a competitor's interface or delivery, not an absence of feeding expertise. Fixing those problems alone does not prove a market gap or a profitable acquisition route.
- Development reviewers often appreciate videos and real experts, which a PDF loses. Some say the activities merely describe what they already do (`rev01-0037`). More pages or the label customized do not solve that.
- Positive sleep reviews and confident predictions coexist with reports of poor fit, and reassuring explanations coexist with skepticism about their mechanism. Subjective relief is not scientific validation.
- No review belongs to a verified Mamivio buyer. Price and past-payment accounts validate spending on adjacent products only. There are no sales, CAC, conversion, refund or retention data for a Mamivio report.

## Suggested validation questions, not ad copy

For a first-solids report, test whether a caregiver can use a one-page first week and shopping list without reading a long manual. Ask what changes with their household foods, grocery day, cooking time, preferred purees/finger foods, caregiver arrangement and foods already tried. Keep allergies, swallowing concerns, growth and readiness outside automated recommendations unless a qualified professional owns assessment and referral. Test paid orders with a clear scope; favorable reviews elsewhere are not a substitute.

## Missing voices and source classes

This batch is app/book review evidence. It does not supply clinician interviews, nonreviewer community histories, YouTube comments, complete low-rating archives, expecting-mother interviews, mothers who do not use apps, or market prevalence. Full books and paid courses remain unread. The strongest recommendation remains a product hypothesis until customer validation.
