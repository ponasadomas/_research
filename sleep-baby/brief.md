# Product brief — Baby and toddler sleep, 0–36 months

Written 2026-10-25 from the niche title, the owner's brief (`../../../brief.md`) and the 2026-10-25 Ad Library sweep (`../../main/ads-running/`). **There is no product yet; every line below is an assumption the research must test.**

- **Who suffers (assumption):** Mothers (and partners) of babies and toddlers whose nights are broken: frequent wakings, short naps, contact naps, early rising, the 4-month regression, bedtime battles. The buyer is the exhausted parent, not the child.
- **What they already buy (from the Ad Library sweep and `../../global/competitors/`):** Sleep courses ($49–249: Taking Cara Babies, Let's Sleep, Just Chill, Good Inside), quiz-to-plan programs (Betteroo: quiz → personalized plan → 28-day challenge, money-back), sleep consultants, apps (Huckleberry SweetSpot, Napper), hardware (Cradlewise, Nanit, HALO).
- **The candidate Mamivio product (assumption):** Quiz on age, current wakings, feeding, where the baby sleeps, the parent's values on crying → a personalized, age-specific plan → a day-by-day program. Price ladder already used by Mamivio: $17.95 / $29.95 / $49.95 for 1 / 3 / 6 months.
- **Research principle (owner, 2026-11-16):** Research records what people actually say and do, in their own words, whatever the subject; nothing is withheld, excluded, softened or paraphrased away because of its subject. Readers may label a quote, but it remains ordinary evidence and is quoted like any other. What is usable in copy is the owner's decision, made later; it is never a research decision.

## Questions the research must answer

1. Which moment makes them search and pay, in their own words?
2. What have they already bought or tried, and how did it land? Who quit, and why?
3. Does personalization by age or situation matter to them, or is generic advice enough?
4. What do they say a solution is worth? What do they call a rip-off?
5. Who else is in the decision (partner, grandparents, daycare)?
