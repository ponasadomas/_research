## 1. How long fixes last, what a relapse looks like, what parents changed first (the core question)

**Relapses inside two weeks are common in success posts too (confirms §16.2's "a day to two weeks").** "the amazing 12 hour nights didn’t continue" four days in (sb43-0319); a toddler better after a week of the chair method, then "a shit show all over again" (sb43-0166); Ferber/TCB worked three nights, then a 2-hour scream, the parent held him, and now "he screams for 1-2 hours every night going down" (sb43-0573, -0574); an "extinction bust" on nights 5-7 (sb43-0548); paci-dropping wakes a week after Ferber, "scared that the sleep training will be completely ruined" (sb43-0475); 5am wakes start the week the baby first sleeps through (sb43-0518, -0338); "nothing would work longer than a few days" (sb43-0439).

**What a relapse looks like, in their words:** "Her naps and nighttime had become so erratic" a month after training, with false starts lengthening from 5 to 20 minutes and wakes rising from 2 to 4-5 (sb43-0450); "split nights when schedules are wrong" (sb43-0348); "every time I thought “we got this”, something knocked us back" (sb43-0385).

**What they changed first, and whether it was right.** Wake windows almost always come first. Sometimes it is right: "I pushed her morning window by 15 min and let the rest of the day slowly fall in line" (sb43-0567); a father found a 6-hour last window by night 8 (sb43-0361, -0363). The free coach in sleeptrain-1shxa07 gives the same first move every time: "try a schedule change when you start seeing frequent night wakes for a few nights in a row" (sb43-0273); "if you start to notice more night wakes, slowly stretch her wake windows" (sb43-0280); "don’t get discouraged if night wakes start happening later on! You can always cut down on daytime sleep and add in more awake time" (sb43-0281). This matches the father in §16.2 [sb36-0208]. **But the first change was often the wrong one:** a mother shortened windows, naps improved, nights got worse, and the real cause was a feed-to-sleep association (sb43-0451, -0452); "While you might say we sleep-trained again, it was NOT like it was the first time" (sb43-0453); "don’t be afraid to “do it again!” You didn’t fail." (sb43-0454). Split-nights rules from a parent of a nearly 3-year-old: "wake them up at the DWT no matter how bad the night was" (sb43-0349).

**Holds longer than two weeks, with tuning:** a month (sb43-0503); two months through teething (sb43-0466, -0467; sb43-0555); about six months for the 333-method poster (sb43-0262, -0282); a coach-led success at 7 months that then needed "re train several times" until 15-17 months (sb43-0381, -0382, -0383). The retrain was quicker than the first time in sb43-0453 and sb43-0528 ("it only took two days to retrain"). The parents who held best had a rule for what to do when crying returned: "If I lay him down, and he cries for more than a few minutes, I *know* something is off" and cuddle-then-transfer during illness (sb43-0398, -0399); "I don’t stick to ferber when she’s in pain" (sb43-0466) (interpretation: the relapse rule is "respond, then return to the method", not "start over").
