## Patterns

### 1. The parent's own body and life (the "get yourself back" angle)
Parents describe physical symptoms, not just "tired": headaches, nausea, dizziness, racing heart, hair loss, weight gain, pain from being kicked all night, a body that "won't settle" even when the baby sleeps. [sb03-0102, 0178, 0179, 0167, 0224, 0279, 0311, 0316]
- **Identity loss** phrased as "shell of a person / myself", "not myself", "a completely different person". [sb03-0167, 0180, 0281]
- **Wanting time back for themselves**: "my bed back… free while she naps"; an hour of alone time in the evening; a morning to "get myself together"; a "longer break… so I can regulate". [sb03-0295, 0220, 0221, 0276, 0326]
- **Work**: the ICU nurse on four hours of broken sleep before shifts; a father sleeping on a couch at his workplace; leaving work early to rescue a nap; driving in circles after daycare. [sb03-0103, 0105, 0003, 0227, 0226, 0179]
- **Mental health**: depressive thoughts with the exact ask, "I urgently need a realistic sleep plan"; rage at night; PPA/PPD that parents name themselves; a father in therapy. [sb03-0142, 0240, 0241, 0191, 0252, 0065]

### 2. The marriage around the night

### 4. What they tried, and how it went
- **Ferber/CIO that failed**: vomiting from crying is a repeated reason to stop; so is screaming at day 10–15; and an hour of crying followed by a wake 30 minutes later. [sb03-0233, 0268, 0297, 0225, 0290, 0217, 0258, 0153, 0163]
- **Ferber/CIO that worked**: TCB plus Ferber with twins; a 7.5-hour stretch on night 2; crying down from 20–45 to 5–10 minutes. [sb03-0259, 0307, 0200, 0188, 0277]
- **Gains that then reversed**: progress undone by teeth, illness, standing, or the chair leaving the room. Parents fear they "created a habit" or "lost all progress". [sb03-0129, 0187, 0270, 0271, 0304, 0305, 0136]
- **Rescues that worked**: moving the nap later [sb03-0234]; accepting being awake [sb03-0243]; dropping the nap [sb03-0020].

### 5. Drowning in numbers and advice
Parents run wake windows to the minute, track in Huckleberry for months, use ChatGPT, and still cannot tell "over or under tired". They say exactly this. [sb03-0117, 0121, 0122, 0131, 0165, 0176, 0190, 0248, 0282, 0283, 0320, 0325]
- **Generic rules don't fit their baby (personalization)**: "the numbers make sense in theory…"; "the right method for her temperament"; "does the book cover starting around one year"; "a realistic sleep plan". [sb03-0123, 0143, 0144, 0145, 0067, 0142, 0218]

### 6. Comparison and guilt
Friends' babies all sleeping through; "good night mommy and they walk out"; "I just thought by 2 years". [sb03-0154, 0302, 0023, 0069, 0267, 0265]
Guilt shows up as "failed as a mom", "horrible mom", and yelling at a toddler who then cried to sleep. [sb03-0134, 0092, 0116, 0197, 0199, 0269]

## Contrary evidence
- Some parents are content and do not want a fix [sb03-0069, 0073]; one pumping mother pushed back on hundreds of comments and asked only to be heard [sb03-0183]. A product built to "fix" sleep will not speak to them.
- A sleep consultant told one parent she was doing everything right; she was no better off [sb03-0132]. A coach's rigid 6:45 bedtime clashed with family time [sb03-0137]. Paid help is not always the answer.
- Tracking apps and "more information" made several parents worse, not better [sb03-0121, 0122, 0284].
- Partners are not always the problem: some take whole nights [sb03-0236, 0288] or rescue the parent from rage [sb03-0241].
