## Patterns (each backed by quote ids)

### 1. How long fixes last, what a relapse looks like, what parents change first (the core question)
OP follow-ups are rare (most units have 0–2 OP replies), so these are first relapses, not durations.
- **Free fixes, next day to two weeks.** A nap cap "went great" the next day, with no later report (sb48-0101). Moving bedtime later stopped the split night but brought 5:15 wakes two nights running: "I can’t win." (sb48-0110). A Ferber tweak gave "5 min with minor grumbling" the first night and a 5:40 wake (sb48-0174). Commenters' longer wake windows "made relatively no difference" after two days (sb48-0366). The longest clean arc: a Fuss-It-Out parent extended wake windows by 5 minutes and it "definitely helped" (sb48-0159); ten days later illness forced a "recalibrate" and "Feeling a bit lost again" (sb48-0160, sb48-0161); a trip was already feared (sb48-0162); sixteen days after posting a reset of the first wake window fixed nap refusal and bedtime (sb48-0163), credited to one commenter who coached across three weeks (sb48-0164). Confirms §16.2 and §16.3 ("a commenter becomes a free coach").
- **Paid help, a few nights.** A UK consultant gave "a few nights of 6–7 hour stretches … life changing", then "Now it’s slipping again … after seeing what he can do, it feels so discouraging" (sb48-0335, sb48-0336). Same shape as the free fixes.
- **Training that held for weeks or months, then broke at a named event.** Travel: a work trip (sb48-0156), a two-month trip abroad (sb48-0301), a two-week holiday (sb48-0311), Fiji at 18 months, "the sleep training was ruined" (sb48-0369). Illness: responding to a sick baby became the new pattern (sb48-0040, sb48-0041); a virus put a 5-month-old "firmly back at square one" (sb48-0093); night weaning undone by an illness (sb48-0206) and by sickness plus travel (sb48-0269); Ferber night 7 good, night 8 sick (sb48-0350). A move and new daycare after one good week of the chair method (sb48-0096). Big-kid beds for twins who were "absolutely perfect sleepers" (sb48-0265); a floor bed for a 23-month-old (sb48-0395). Holds of months: CIO at 7 months held to 21 months (sb48-0010); Ferber at 11 months held to 13 (sb48-0222); Ferber at 6 months "life-changing" until about 10 (sb48-0386). Short holds: four days of good naps, "then the weekend happened" (sb48-0271); "one week … Then it just all randomly derailed" (sb48-0363); "a week or 2" (sb48-0188); "wow we’re back baby!" for 1.5 months, then the second birthday (sb48-0371).
- **What parents changed first:** feeding back in (sb48-0029 "I caved … started nursing to sleep"; sb48-0076 fed before the timer); holding or rocking (sb48-0237 "I cave after about 2hrs"; sb48-0288 "do I just go back to holding him"); the car ride (sb48-0291, sb48-0294); handing bedtime to the other parent (sb48-0324); a wake-window change (sb48-0159, sb48-0110). Then the retrain question: "Do I basically re sleep train her using the same method?" (sb48-0316); "do I need to sleep train again?" (sb48-0220); "I haven't really explored re-sleep training" (sb48-0266); a CIO repeat ruled out because a 1-year-old cries harder and is "too aware" (sb48-0322), echoed for a 17-month-old (sb48-0187).
- **The fear of undoing it** is its own pain: "Have I ruined our progress" (sb48-0077); "I’m scared to have to start over" (sb48-0099); "I just undid whatever little progress I made" (sb48-0200); "am I being inconsistent and potentially undermining the process" (sb48-0285); "scared to undo everything or that we undid everything" (sb48-0351).
