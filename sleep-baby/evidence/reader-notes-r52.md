## 1. How long fixes last, and what a relapse looks like (the core question)

Wave 2's "a day to two weeks" holds. Most posts here *are* the relapse: a trained or good sleeper who fell apart, and the parent asking how to get back.

- **Days:** three days of self-settling at 4 months, then "back to zero" [sb52-0070] [sb52-0071], and it feels "even WORSE than before he self soothed" [sb52-0073]. A consultant's longer wake windows made things "a little better for a few days", then "back to false starts and early morning wakes" [sb52-0397] [sb52-0398]. Ferber nights 1–3 good, 4 a backslide, 5 a disaster, in two separate families [sb52-0323] [sb52-0102] [sb52-0103]. A father: nights 1–2 "We smelled victory", night 3 back in the parents' bed [sb52-0329] (restricted).
- **Two to three weeks:** one nap "Right off the bat, things were great", early waking "resolved briefly", back at about two weeks [sb52-0371] [sb52-0372]. A dummy-and-back-rub method gave "3 weeks" of good naps, then 7 months hit and "fight after fight" [sb52-0193] [sb52-0194]. One nap at 5.5/5.5 held "for about a month" [sb52-0190].
- **Months:** trained at 10 months, then "What a glorious (nearly) 3 months" before 17 months broke it [sb52-0173]. Bottle-and-rocking removed with 10-minute check-ins worked in 3–4 nights, held until daycare and illness, and then "the same sleep-training approach … no longer seems effective" [sb52-0456] [sb52-0457].
- **What a relapse looks like:** standing in the cot and crying [sb52-0022] [sb52-0034]; refusing the transfer [sb52-0368]; losing it at every put-down, day and night, after months of good sleep [sb52-0461]; a toddler sitting up for hours [sb52-0314] [sb52-0315]. The triggers parents name are teeth, illness, travel and a skipped routine: "Any time we make any small progress he cuts a tooth, gets sick, somthing wild." [sb52-0027]; "either gotten sick, sleep regression, or teeth coming in" [sb52-0106]; a 14-hour time difference [sb52-0050]; one dinner party an hour and a half late [sb52-0449]; illness comfort that became a habit [sb52-0248] [sb52-0369].
- **What parents changed first:** the schedule, by small steps, before any retrain. More awake time [sb52-0210] [sb52-0409]; a later bedtime [sb52-0295] [sb52-0418]; one nap [sb52-0371]; capping naps [sb52-0399]. The second lever is to restart the method: "my mistake was thinking once she had the skill I no longer had to continue with the sleep training" [sb52-0412].
- **Why retraining is dreaded:** "Thinking about having to sleep train her again makes me feel physically ill" [sb52-0202]; "This is our third serious attempt" [sb52-0101]; the guilt of having "ruined our sleep training" [sb52-0024].
- **Long holds, as contrary evidence:** CIO for a 21-month-old took "a week and a half", and two months later she still slept 12 hours [sb52-0038] [sb52-0039]; an 18-month-old's sleep "kinda fixed itself" seven weeks on, with no method [sb52-0204]; a dummy wean was "a distant memory" a month later [sb52-0422].

## 2. Free advice: wins, and whether they lasted

Most wins were reported the next morning, so they show only the first night. More awake time gave a 4.5-month-old her first full night [sb52-0210]. A later bedtime cut the crying [sb52-0295] [sb52-0418], and three naps instead of four helped [sb52-0185]. Ferber plus a commenter's wake windows had settled things two weeks on [sb52-0054] [sb52-0055]. One parent felt helped just by having the problem read back to them: "Sometimes you just have to type it out and have someone read it back to you!" [sb52-0192]. A chair method copied "based on a Reddit post we saw" works but stalls at 30–60 minutes [sb52-0486]. **Free advice backfired too:** "someone say that babies need sleep pressure, so I cut his naps down a lot", and the baby was up until 4:30am [sb52-0252] [sb52-0255]. No OP in this set came back weeks later to say a free fix had failed. So the follow-ups show wins but say nothing about how long they lasted.
