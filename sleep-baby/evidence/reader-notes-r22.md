## Contrary evidence and thin spots

- **Consultant successes are real.** Patterns 1–2 are not the whole picture: about seven quotes report a consultant fixing nights [sb22-0010, sb22-0164, sb22-0176, sb22-0285, sb22-0288, sb22-0298, sb22-0353]. What failed was usually naps, early waking or wake windows after the programme ended [sb22-0012, sb22-0092, sb22-0145].
- **Some parents choose a consultant because a stranger online cannot hold them to a plan** [sb22-0353, sb22-0238], even though the sub knows the material, as the parent herself admits ("Thank you, sub friends!!!!" [sb22-0356]). This cuts against "free advice wins": what the paid help adds is a person (interpretation).
- **Apps also earn praise:** Huckleberry "right 90% of the time" [sb22-0199]; Napper "almost down to the minute" sometimes [sb22-0119].
- **A temperament view against all methods:** "You just got dealt a difficult baby and whatever you will do that baby will remain difficult" [sb22-0227, sb22-0223]; "this is normal for my baby" [sb22-0363]; "Is sleep training only effective if you have a good sleeper?" [sb22-0048].
- **No posts look like seeded marketing** (`possible_promo` not used). One poster pushes back on sound-machine marketing [sb22-0267].
- **Products called worth it:** DreamEgg [sb22-0009], Merlin suit (restricted) [sb22-0271], Slumberpod as a training aid [sb22-0350]. **Called a waste or disappointing:** Hatch [sb22-0007], Momcozy [sb22-0008], Babysense [sb22-0257], Huckleberry [sb22-0204, sb22-0207], and consultants [sb22-0094, sb22-0165].
