# Reader notes, R02: success stories and the other side (2026-10-25)

Source: 73 units in `read-v1a-keep_success-001` to `-006` and `read-v1a-keep_otherside-001`, `-002` (ledger: `sources/reddit-reading-r02.md`). 294 quotes in `voice/reddit-r02.jsonl`. **Counts below are counts of quotes I chose, not of people.** The success threads come mostly from r/sleeptrain, so they lean pro-training; the other side is mostly the spouse, grandmother and daycare voices, seen through the poster.

## 1. What turned it (success posts)

- **Usually a smaller change than the parent expected, not a new method.** Putting the baby down awake once, out of frustration [sb02-0012]; burping instead of nursing to sleep [sb02-0103]; one move away from timed contact naps [sb02-0001]; swapping nursing for rocking at night wakes [sb02-0017, sb02-0018]; the father doing the put-down, not the mother [sb02-0049, sb02-0046, sb02-0056].
- **Sticking to one method for a fixed stretch, "rather than chopping and changing"** [sb02-0116]; the parents who failed describe starting and stopping [sb02-0026, sb02-0086, sb02-0138].
- **Reading the schedule as part of it: undertiredness.** "UNDERTIREDNESS. Outside of this subreddit, I've only ever heard about overtiredness" [sb02-0144, sb02-0145]; logged wake windows and nap totals used to decode bad nights [sb02-0066]; a paid course left one mother "terrified of an overtired baby" and afraid to stretch wake windows [sb02-0294]; the opposite camp blames the googling itself [sb02-0233]. The "4 attempts" post blames the first failure on 8 hours of awake time at 4 months [sb02-0084].
- **The night-by-night log is the success-story genre.** Parents post minutes cried per night as proof [sb02-0073, sb02-0098, sb02-0106, sb02-0189, sb02-0162]. Night 3 or 4 is often named as the worst or the turn; "extinction burst" is expected and named [sb02-0011]. (No timelines claimed here; these are what they report.)
- **Method switching by baby response.** Check-ins escalated some babies, so they went to full extinction [sb02-0094, sb02-0099, sb02-0199, sb02-0133]; for others Ferber caused vomiting, so they went gentler (chair) [sb02-0112, sb02-0187]; one family needed three Ferber rounds before a gradual fade worked at 10 months [sb02-0084 to sb02-0089].
- **What they'd tell others:** "don't overthink it" [sb02-0020]; "do the sleep training even if it hurts your parent heart" [sb02-0025]; "Don't force a method if it doesn't feel good in your bones" [sb02-0089]; "absolutely worth it" for a one-year-old [sb02-0107]; practical coping: one earbud and a podcast [sb02-0194], earbuds to bed [sb02-0023], the father handled night one while the mother went outside [sb02-0042].
- **The doubts before:** "I was SO scared to start" [sb02-0039]; afraid of the crying itself [sb02-0044, sb02-0024]; afraid it would not work for "the worst sleeper" [sb02-0069]; waiting for medical pain to be ruled out first [sb02-0009]; safe-sleep worry about rolling [sb02-0072]; fear of being called negligent for posting [sb02-0063]. After success, the fear doesn't stop: "waiting for the other shoe to drop" [sb02-0149], "is he just a unicorn" [sb02-0104], travel [sb02-0111, sb02-0130], and guilt from another community [sb02-0090].
- **Regret is about timing, not method:** "wish I sleep trained before 7.5 months" [sb02-0081]; at 11 months [sb02-0060]; at 20 months [sb02-0180]; "I only wish I did it sooner with both kids" [sb02-0198]; one regret of breastfeeding as long as she did [sb02-0053].

## 2. Personalization and paid help (product-brief Q3, Q4)

- The strongest **pro-personalization** voice: a parent who had read everything still could not follow a free schedule. "I didn't have confidence that my random internet schedule would work so I never fully tried" [sb02-0007]; the consultant was "worth it" because of "a personalized schedule and the science behind baby sleep" [sb02-0006]; she needed to be *told* the baby has to cry, and why [sb02-0008]; the consultant gave belief to stick it out [sb02-0005]. A consultant's one instruction (dark room plus white noise) fixed naps [sb02-0109]; another's line on a calm start to the night [sb02-0082].
- **Free sources did the job for many:** Precious Little Sleep "fuss it out" [sb02-0045, sb02-0116, sb02-0162], the Ferber book, four books read [sb02-0070], a free online guide recommended "over Taking Cara Babies" [sb02-0191], the subreddit itself [sb02-0139].
- **"Fit to my baby" is the success-story moral:** "What works for one baby may not work for another" [sb02-0089]; "You just gotta do what works best for you and your baby" [sb02-0192]; "CIO was never my plan but it was what worked for our son" [sb02-0099].
- **Contrary evidence, sceptics of the industry:** "Many voices online sell the illusion that if you just adopt their Ten Step Plan…" [sb02-0034]; "preying on the exhaustion and desperation of new parents to sell them overpriced PDFs and courses" [sb02-0235]; "skeptical of the sleep training industry/influencers" [sb02-0164]; the book is "pretty militant" [sb02-0163]; tracking apps blamed for anxiety [sb02-0233, sb02-0236]; "stop googling wake windows" [sb02-0122].

## 3. The other side of the door

**The partner.** The disagreement runs both ways:
- The father who wants to train, the mother who refuses: "In her mind 'sleep training' meant 'callously letting kid cry' which meant 'kid feels abandoned'" [sb02-0174]; paediatrician and behavioural specialist both said to do it, "Wife didn't want to" [sb02-0184]; he did Ferber on the couch without telling her [sb02-0177], after watching her cry herself to sleep [sb02-0181, sb02-0185]; she was "glad" afterwards [sb02-0179]. Also: "my partner wanting her to CIO" until the mother "gave in" [sb02-0266, sb02-0267]; a husband who read Precious Little Sleep and persuaded a mother who had promised herself never [sb02-0154]; a non-nursing partner whose partner fears "the potential long term issue" [sb02-0229].
- The mother who wants a structure, the father who thinks the baby "will just sleep when he's tired" [sb02-0240, sb02-0244, sb02-0246]; "what's wrong with him?" [sb02-0243, sb02-0247]; he gives up and hands the baby back [sb02-0242]; a husband frustrated she quits on a nap too early [sb02-0203]; one who gets angry when she says the baby needs feeding [sb02-0226].
- Arguing mid-training: "my partner and I argued back and forth on whether this was right. I do not recommend having this conversation at 3am" [sb02-0143]. Agreed limits: "an hour is too much" [sb02-0167].
- The nights divide unevenly: "4 hours of sleep broken up and he's going a good 7 hours straight" [sb02-0252]; "Just sleep then" [sb02-0210]; the bedsharing scare with a sleeping husband [sb02-0249 to sb02-0251]; the nursing parent can't hand over at night [sb02-0206, sb02-0216]. Dad taking over as the fix [sb02-0046, sb02-0049, sb02-0056, sb02-0136].

**Grandparents.** The mother-in-law who naps the baby 30 minutes before bedtime because the baby "told" her [sb02-0221, sb02-0223]; "come socialize" at 11 pm [sb02-0273]; "well we have people coming over just keep him up" [sb02-0276]; the swing and loud noises [sb02-0277]; "my kids never did that… because they were so good" [sb02-0113]. The husband sides with his parents [sb02-0275]. One grandmother is the rescue: she will mind the baby at home after daycare failed (unit `NewParents-1w2y21r`, not quoted).

**Daycare / nursery / sitter.** Two voices: daycare that "just works" (wake windows, dark room, white noise) [sb02-0278], "Daycare helped us establish a really good schedule" [sb02-0151]; and daycare that breaks sleep: "sleep went to hell in a hand basket" [sb02-0105], "10-ish minutes" naps with lights and music on [sb02-0281, sb02-0282], putting him down "whenever he rubs his eyes" [sb02-0284], pushing pacifier, bottle and nap changes at one [sb02-0280]. The sharpest line: "the daycare wouldn't be adapting to her, she needs to adapt to them" [sb02-0260]. Fear before starting is mostly about contact nappers [sb02-0258, sb02-0264, sb02-0265, sb02-0291].

**Parents who refuse sleep training.** Their reasons: "I refuse to let my baby cry it out" while drained and asking if night nursing is just expected [sb02-0212 to sb02-0215]; "I don't personally see the need… the second we pick her up she calms down" [sb02-0254]; attachment parenting against "every single person I meet" [sb02-0261, sb02-0263]. The content ones: a nursing mother of two who "scrapped all the 'shoulds'", is "still tired… just not a zombie anymore" and calls it "sleep trusting" [sb02-0121 to sb02-0129]; the newborn-rules post [sb02-0233, sb02-0234]. Contrary: several success posters came from that side ("I was always part of the 'it's abusive' crowd until now" [sb02-0200]; "so determined not to use any 'harsh' sleep training" [sb02-0022]).

**Strangers online.** The judgement is loud: "multiple people told me that I should have my child taken from me and that the police should be involved" [sb02-0166], "I should go to jail" [sb02-0161]; Facebook mom-group comments that the baby only sleeps because "you have abandoned them" [sb02-0289]; guilt from a cosleeping sub [sb02-0090]. Parents answer with necessity, not ideology: paid leave and work [sb02-0164, sb02-0169], "very few 'right versus wrong' situations" [sb02-0159].

## 4. Pain, scenes and the body

Physical cost: carpal tunnel from rocking [sb02-0239]; "my back is destroyed" from standing bounces [sb02-0037]; wife with back issues from clinging to the bed edge [sb02-0057]; sleep paralysis and auditory hallucinations [sb02-0031]; "My body still naturally wakes up every hour" after the baby sleeps through [sb02-0092]. The 29-minute nap that leaves no time to shower or eat [sb02-0047]. "Sworn off another kid ONLY because of the sleep" [sb02-0178]. Four years without a full night [sb02-0211]. The parent's own first nights of training: sobbing, checking the monitor, not sleeping [sb02-0148]. Even after it works, the crying at every put-down "breaks my heart" [sb02-0067, sb02-0068]; not knowing how long the crying will be is its own stress [sb02-0065].
