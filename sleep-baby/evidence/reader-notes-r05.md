## 1. Patterns earlier readers flagged: confirmed or contradicted

**Paid plans that weren't personal: confirmed, strongly.** One six-month-old's mother hired a consultant, filled in "a pretty extensive questionnaire", sends daily logs, and by day 9 says the only thing changed is wake windows and "I don't know if she gives the same general plan to everyone or if it's actually personalized" [sb05-0225, sb05-0226]. The consultant's first plan (3h wake windows) left the baby "EXHAUSTED" [sb05-0220], then blamed daycare for wakings that came before daycare [sb05-0222]; the net result was "the same thing in her crib but with soooo much crying" [sb05-0221]. Other coaches applied blanket rules: night-wean and let him cry or get him up [sb05-0134], "should be on two naps by now" [sb05-0252], "forcing naps because ... kids at this age usually do", with the motto "more sleep, will bring more sleep" [sb05-0303]. A father counts "15-20 sleep trainings" with professional trainers, and one "didn't even send us the invoice, because she had absolutely no idea what to do anymore" [sb05-0301, sb05-0302]. A free consultant was booked "but have heard mixed reviews" [sb05-0106].

**Free advice beating paid: confirmed.** "My sleep consultant was leaning towards shortening them and I'm so glad I listened to you all first" [sb05-0175, after sb05-0173]. The day-9 consultant client asks a Reddit commenter for private help [sb05-0228]. Several next-day turnarounds came from thread advice on wake windows, nap caps, last-feed timing or dropping the dummy [sb05-0059, sb05-0097, sb05-0128, sb05-0172, sb05-0254]. *Contrary:* free group advice can be just as generic. "The Precious Little Sleep group of course suggested more awake time" did not fit a toddler who had slept well on that schedule [sb05-0150]. Some parents pushed back on commenters' wake-window targets [sb05-0009, sb05-0265, sb05-0310] or on attitude: "I don't need someone who has never met her giving me attitude on Reddit" [sb05-0183].

**Panic-buying: partly confirmed. Desperation buying is clear; "at night" can't be confirmed from the text.** "do I just suck it up and give in and buy taking cara babies?" [sb05-0095]. "I'm looking for a program that can help us daytime struggling parents" [sb05-0019]. A night nanny whose "price goes up if we want her to sleep train" [sb05-0248]. Hesitation on price: transition swaddles "soo expensive even on vinted" [sb05-0010]; the Snoo not "worth it ... if they're only in it for like 6 months" [sb05-0270]. Courses used: Moms on Call [sb05-0102], Gina Ford / Contented Little Baby [sb05-0236], Precious Little Sleep book [sb05-0086]. A parent asked for "the name of the guide" [sb05-0077].

[sb05-0170, sb05-0172]. Anxiety: "I use the Huckleberry app as a crutch ... I like to know exactly how long he's sleeping" [sb05-0195]. "the 'experts' say ... 12-15 hours a day, I stress if he is under that" [sb05-0196]. "an anxious mess of numbers and googling" [sb05-0067]. Some quit tracking: "it got to be too much without seeing a change" [sb05-0054]. Huckleberry SweetSpot did not help a nine-week-old [sb05-0023]. One mother "debated following their personalized schedule" [sb05-0292]. The parent's own wearable: an Apple Watch logging "getting up over 20 times" [sb05-0281].
