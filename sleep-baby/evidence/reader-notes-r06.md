## Patterns

### 1. The night scene, in numbers and clock times
Parents post the night as a log: "14 resettles in the cot since 10pm" at 5:45am [0281]; eight wakes timed to the minute while "feeding my baby to sleep for the eighth time tonight" [0119]; "up at 9,11,12,1,3 and then ready to start the day at 6" [0260]; "3am or 4am or 5am or all freaking three" [0011]; a toddler "running around the living room" at 4:15am [0140]; door-banging and light-flipping at 2am [0112]. The bomb-technician transfer [0173] and "pray to our ancestors" is the most vivid line for the put-down.

### 2. The exact asks
- **A plan, not tips**: "We just desperately need a plan that actually works" [0089]; "We really need to pick a game plan and stick to it" [0020]; "what to tackle first" [0044]; "what sleep crutch to stop first" [0178].
- **A method for *this* child** (personalization): "best for this type of child" [0135]; "reteaching a skill he temporarily lost rather than starting from scratch" [0110]; "I think it works for some babies but not ours" [0216]; "I so wish it was an exact science!" [0070]; gentle methods "for older babies who are horrible sleepers" [0167].
- **Permission and comfort**: "Pls don't mention sleep training, I don't have it in me" [0080]; "Please use kind words and don't bully me" [0131]; "just need some gentle advice" [0061]; "When does it end? How do we end it quicker?" [0221].
- **Whether to give up**: "Do we give up at some point?" [0116]; "Do I just push through??" [0229].

### 3. The parent's own state
- **Own sleep counted**: "average of 2,5 hours of sleep every night" for ten months [0052]; longest stretch 5 hours, "on one hand" [0152]; "haven't had a single full nights rest" in a year [0179]; "up with her for 16 hours. I am depleted" [0079].
- **Body**: always feeling like fainting [0145]; week-long headache [0140]; tweaked back [0098]; "Holding him that long is killing me" [0069]; back pain from walking a crying baby [0166]; a pinching toddler making her body "a fidget toy" [0285]; C-section and emergency end of pregnancy [0322]; 36 weeks pregnant with a toddler up at 4am [0232].
- **Mental health, in their words**: rage when the baby cries and fantasies of leaving [0153, 0154]; "I regret having my baby" [0146]; "I don't want to wake up anymore" [0182]; burnout, sick leave, therapy rationed by money [0300, 0306]; PPD/PPA named by the parent [0253]; grief after a parent's death [0223]. The sleep training itself is described as a mental-health decision: "I'm sleep training to better my mental health" [0290].
- **Identity**: "I thought I would be better at this. I didn't expect sleep to consume me" [0032]; "jealous and lonely" [0034]; "I am a sleeper, I love sleep" [0333]; turning down outings because one parent must walk the baby [0194].
- **Work**: a teacher who stepped back at work [0046]; a single mother working nights who needs bedtime by 7 for the sitter [0081]; 5am shifts after a daycare re-train [0092]; night-shift mother up 1-5am [0145]; a nurse told "It's mommy's fault" by colleagues at her own workplace [0126]; husbands whose jobs exclude them from nights [0031, 0160, 0261].

### 4. The marriage around the night
- **Default parent and resentment**: "He checks his phone for the time and rolls back over" [0319]; "I don't want to have to manage him as well as the baby" [0320]; "22 hours a day" [0027]; scorekeeping that "always ends up in a fight" [0303]; "we have a difficult child" and "it is not about you" [0302].
- **Explicit threat to the marriage**: post titled "Please help save my marriage" [0172]; "If my son keeps waking us like this our marriage will continue to suffer" [0288]; "mental health and relationship taken major hit" [0200]; a father who "haven't seen her in a week" because evenings vanished [0272]; a husband sleeping on the couch while an older sibling's grades fall [0219].
- **Cruel words**: a partner mocking "it's not that hard to just get up" and threatening custody [0025].
- **Partners as rescuers too**: the husband who puts the baby down in minutes after two days of failed naps [0042]; "glad my husband is more determined than I am" [0071].

### 5. Partners fighting over sleep training (confirms earlier readers)
Both directions appear: "My husband never wanted to sleep train, but I did. And I couldn't win him" [0181]; "My wife isn't comfortable anymore with the cry out method" [0165]; a husband who wants the baby to stay in the room and is sad when he loses ("that's my little guy") [0141, 0142]; husband refuses bedsharing now the toddler wants it [0307]. Fathers in the first person who cannot bear the crying: "I can't listen to her yell Dada from her crib through tears. I'm not strong enough" [0075].

### 6. Paid help that wasn't personal (confirms, with nuance)
- A consultant's five-hour wake window gave a 6:30pm bedtime that didn't fit the child [0187]; Huckleberry's sleep plan "confuses me at times, the instructions aren't always precise", then "fine ditching Huckleberry" [0243, 0246]; TCB and Precious Little Sleep "have conflicting views on Paci use" [0210]; chiropractor and osteopath "said that they fixed it. They didn't." [0047].
- Contrary: a consultant who helped ("much better nights") [0239]; a consultant who "accidentally" got independent sleep but not the early wakes [0201]; a medical sleep therapist with two good months before reversal [0047, 0048]; one parent booking a consultant call again after three or four failed trainings [0305].

### 7. Free advice beating paid, and advice threads hurting (both confirmed)
- **Free fixes that worked**: a later bedtime "worked pretty much immediately" after 1.5 weeks of extinction [0106]; a floor bed for a scared toddler [0275]; an AP thread telling a nurse "there's nothing to be fixed" [0132]; going back to feeding after quitting training [0062, 0063]; a mother reassured by a thread enough to sleep [0325].
- **Advice threads making it worse**: "criticism for asking for help" [0009]; "your dogmatic approach to responding about a child you don't know is absolutely wild" [0097]; "I'm not looking for a 'magic answer'" [0123]; "It's the waking up to him almost choking that is NOT NORMAL" [0256]; "how much gets shrugged away" [0311]; a father "running out of gas replying to everyone" [0078]. Strangers off Reddit too: a doctor and a receptionist saying "It's mommy's fault" [0126] (a pharmacist said the same, per the post); a neighbour texting "do some thing to stop her crying" [0304]; family pressure to "break his habit" [0214, 0215].
- **Advice that conflicts with itself**: the sub said lengthen windows, a friend said one nap, ChatGPT said shorten — and four hours awake followed [0330, 0331, 0332]; "My head is just spinning from all the differing information" [0068]; "everyone preaches wake windows as gospel when they aren't mentioned by actual health authorities" [0149].

### 8. Tracking apps: lifeline and anxiety (confirms)
Lifeline: "I use huckleberry because I'm awful at tracking" [0265]; Napper's to-the-minute windows and graphs [0037]; wake windows from Huckleberry used to judge sleep needs [0004, 0271, 0340]. Anxiety: "I'm trying not to obsessively track wake windows because I did that with my first baby and it was terrible for my mental health" [0108]; "really stressed about the fact that he had too much awake time" [0040]; guilt for missing the app's window by 15 minutes [0266]; "I might need to delete the apps now that we have a decent routine" [0240]; a newborn's sleep logged eyes-closed to eyes-open, research that "furthered concerns" [0308, 0310].

### 9. "Low sleep needs" (confirms; parents self-diagnose)
"I think baby is low sleep needs" at 16 weeks, from Napper graphs [0038]; "lower sleep needs overall" [0023]; "very sensitive child with low sleep needs & I believe my second is as well" [0133]; "10-10.5 is just his max … which is ok!" [0233, 0235]; "her natural sleep requirement or … needs more sleep" [0241]. The label is used both as an explanation for failure and as a reason to stop trying.

### 10. Re-training after setbacks (the most common arc in these chunks, 30 quotes)
Teeth, colds, shots, travel, renovation, daycare start, a new skill (standing, crawling, rolling) undo the training: [0015, 0016, 0084, 0085, 0092, 0098, 0112, 0185, 0186, 0257, 0267, 0268, 0297, 0337]. Parents fear the window has closed: "missed the sleep training train" [0104]; "missed the boat" [0176]; "afraid it's going to take at least a good week … of insane crying" [0134]; "re-sleep train after sickness, vacation, etc." as a recurring chore [0186]; "It worked but then it went back to zero" [0305].

### 11. Panic-buying and buying one theory at a time
No night-time course purchase appeared in these units. What appears instead: a new mattress, then blackout curtains, then a mini-split budget [0159, 0162]; new pacifiers bought "to try again" [0064]; a Moses basket at six days old [0030]; a Yoto that "helped for a little bit" [0114]; a Nodiee light [0278]. The 5am posts themselves are the panic moment [0119, 0281].

### 12. Vomiting as the reason to stop (weak in these chunks)
Only one instance: a two-year-old who "throws up" during check-ins, ending in parents sleeping on her floor [0185]. Other stop reasons dominate: crying length not falling [0115, 0118], check-ins making it worse [0095, 0293], "His little voice is gone" [0067], bedtime anxiety after extinction worked [0096, 0314].

## Contrary evidence
- Content non-trainers: night-owl family with a 10pm-10am toddler [0180]; "Sleep training wasn't a good fit for our family" [0286]; a mother who stopped training and "we both feel so much better" [0062, 0063]; the AP nurse told her baby is normal [0132].
- Training that worked cleanly: full extinction after chair and Ferber failed [0094]; PUPD at 11-12 weeks with twins [0125]; CIO "the only method that worked" [0014]; a first full night in months [0312].
- Daycare and carers aren't the villains here; the constraint is schedules (fixed 11/3 naps, 12:30 preschool nap, on-demand daycare) [0086, 0093, 0203, 0270].
- One mother wants no fix at all for contact naps but feels she "should" change [0017, 0018].

## Surprises
- Fathers speak in the first person often (33 quotes with speaker `partner`): one counts his own 2.5 hours a night for ten months [0052], another says he is "not strong enough" for crying [0075].
- Many parents gave up training because of the parent, not the baby: "I can't let him cry … it legit hurts my chest" [0191]; rage, not the baby's sleep, is why one mother trains [0290].
- Several posts open with "no response to my last post" or "I posted a few weeks ago"; the parent is on a second or third round of strangers' advice [0124 context, 0330].
