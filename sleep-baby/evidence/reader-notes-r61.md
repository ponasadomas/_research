## 1. The core question: how fixes relapse, and what parents change first

This set has a lot of before-and-after posts. **Confirms wave 2 (§16.2): the first relapse usually comes within a day to two weeks. But for trained babies this set adds a longer arc: months of good sleep, then a single trigger.**

- **Thread fixes that wore off within days.** A commenter's wake windows → "The following night, he only cried for 3 minutes" [sb61-0058], then six days later "After only two days of success… crying for 30-45 minutes" [sb61-0059]. A one-night fix for a 2-year-old: "So I thought that was the formula, but he went back to waking up again the next day." [sb61-0147]. Cot naps that fade "the second only 30 or so minutes and then the third day, only a couple of minutes" every time she tries, monthly [sb61-0363] [sb61-0364]. Dropping the dummy cold turkey held until "around day 5" [sb61-0391], and the middle path held "a week or so" [sb61-0392]. "a week of clean sleep… I tried to recreate the same pattern but nothing seems to be working." [sb61-0276]. "OK I've been doing it a few days and still thr same thing" [sb61-0181]. Back from holiday: "Then one day back okay sleep then crap again." [sb61-0188].
- **Training that held for weeks or months, then broke on one trigger.** Two weeks [sb61-0046]; "4-5 weeks" until crawling and standing [sb61-0217]; Ferber "worked great" until he learned to stand [sb61-0248] [sb61-0249]; "nearly 3 months" until separation anxiety, six teeth and an illness [sb61-0195] [sb61-0196]; CIO at 4 months "went well for a few months" [sb61-0425]; Ferber at 5 months until a hotel trip at 8.5 [sb61-0478] [sb61-0479]; "For over a year" until 22 months [sb61-0080]; "slept wonderfully for years" until the dummy went [sb61-0039]; gentle training at 14 months until 18 [sb61-0461]; a handover week to the father at the mother's return to work [sb61-0322] [sb61-0324].
- **What a relapse looks like:** the old prop creeps back first. "'comfort' quickly became breastfeeding to sleep again" [sb61-0196]; rocking again [sb61-0463 (restricted)] [sb61-0462]; bringing the baby in on holiday [sb61-0479]; "I caved tonight and am worried I ruined all our progress." [sb61-0048].
- **What they changed first:** nearly always the schedule, not the method. Wake windows [sb61-0059] [sb61-0250] [sb61-0181]; nap caps [sb61-0083] [sb61-0446]; a fixed wake time [sb61-0399]; "stop pulling bedtime forward" [sb61-0398]. Retraining came second, and a method that worked before was reused: Precious Little Sleep's "sleep wave" again, done by night 3 [sb61-0043] [sb61-0044] [sb61-0045].
- **Longer follow-ups.** At six weeks "somewhat resolved but also evolved into something else" and "I also don't know if what I did helped that much or it's really her age" [sb61-0399] [sb61-0400]. Four months on: "Honestly we just needed time and patience, on top of a little schedule tweak." [sb61-0446]. Six weeks after a consultant's plan: nights partly better, naps "back to 30-40min" [sb61-0163]. Fixes that held in the short run: [sb61-0083] (5 days), [sb61-0420 (restricted)] (10 days).
- **The fear of relapse comes before it.** "If so, what actually worked and how long did it last?" [sb61-0082]; "I don't want to undo this amazing night 1 progress." [sb61-0367]; "I don't want to undo the weeks and weeks of work" [sb61-0110]; "rocking him to sleep isn't going to reset the sleep training" [sb61-0462]. "I was really hoping my time with Reddit was over when it came to sleep lol wishful thinking." [sb61-0330].

Interpretation: the product's setback plan has to cover two different things. One is the fix that fades in days, where the parent tweaks numbers. The other is the trained baby who relapses after a milestone, a trip, teeth, a handover or dropping the dummy, where the parent's question is whether to repeat the old method.
