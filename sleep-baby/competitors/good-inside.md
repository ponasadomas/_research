**Buyer / trigger:** Parent of 0–24 months torn between sleep training and their instincts; night wakings, feeds vs sleep, short naps, regressions; the "guilt and worry spiral".

**Mechanism (≤8 words):** Connection plus a plan, coach-personalized.

**Promise:** "Stop guessing. Start sleeping."; a clear plan for every 2am wake-up; feeding and sleep working together; a schedule that sticks; the only program needed from 0 to 24 months.

**Proof:** "Rated 4.8/5.0 (4,328 Reviews)", "helped 60,000 parents get more sleep", press logos, Dr. Becky brand, named sleep lead (Jilly Blankenship, RN, CLC), three first-name testimonials.

**Price:** Not observed. Value stack on the page: the Complete Sleep Program "yours to keep", one 1:1 session with Jilly's team "($125 value)", 3 months of the Good Inside app "($84 value)".

**Format:** Videos, guides, private coaching session, chatbot, tracker, and the full Good Inside app (feeding, development, behaviour). Access to the sleep program never expires.

**Personalization:** Human: "My team of coaches and I will personalize a sleep plan based on you, your baby, and your goals" via the one coaching session. No quiz seen.

**Guarantee:** 14-day money-back ("Don't love it within 14 days? It's on us.").

**Angles and hooks:** The third way between strict sleep training and "all empathy, no roadmap"; "More than a PDF and 'good luck!'"; no single method works for every baby; attachment is not harmed; buy once instead of separate age-band courses ("never buying separate courses or bundles as your baby grows"), a direct shot at TCB-style ladders.

**Objections answered:** access length; crying (none for newborns; "there can be some crying" for independent sleep, said openly); attachment harm; ages; difference from other programs; too early for a newborn.

## Comparison with Mamivio — interpretation

The second big brand in the sweep that defines itself against "a PDF". Good Inside's personalization is one human call bundled with an app subscription; the program is the same for everyone. It also answers the values conflict head-on ("you don't have to choose") and admits some crying, which is more honest than the "never cry-it-out" sellers. For Mamivio, the "third way" positioning is taken by a brand with far more trust; what is left is the parent who wants a written plan for her own baby without a subscription app and a booked call.
