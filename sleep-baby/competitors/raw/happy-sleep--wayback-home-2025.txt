Feel like you’ve tried everything but baby will
[img alt: Feel like you’ve tried everything but baby will still only cat-nap?

First, remember that it’s very normal for babies under about 4.5-5 months to take mostly short naps. It’s around 4.5+ months that babies begin to be more developmentally-ready to take longer daytime snoozes.

If still struggling, consider these three things:

1️⃣ Is baby’s room conducive to good sleep - make it dark (blackout blinds covered by blackout curtains - you would not believe how much this can help with longer naps!); make it quiet (a sound machine on a white noise setting can work wonders!); and avoid distractions (no twinkly mobiles or toy fishy aquariums distracting from sleep)

2️⃣ Is baby overtired? - overtiredness can lead to overstimulation, agitation, and tougher and shorter naps, as baby has trouble getting in and staying in a deep, restful sleep. So, be clear on your baby’s appropriate wake window and try not to go over it, to avoid that overtired energy!

3️⃣ Does baby know how to fall back asleep on their own when they wake? - If baby does not know how to go TO sleep on their own (i.e. if they need to be rocked, fed, or pacified to sleep at the beginning of the nap), then they are more likely to wake 30-45 minutes into their nap without the ability to settle BACK to sleep on their own, and this will cause short naps. Working on independent sleep skills can help to lengthen those naps.

What does “Adjusted Age” mean (and why it’s
[img alt: What does “Adjusted Age” mean (and why it’s important) here’s why:
⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀
If your baby was not premature, you may have never heard the terms “Adjusted Age” or “Corrected Age.” But, if your baby was born early (earlier than 37 weeks), calculating baby’s adjusted age can be very helpful for sleep!
⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀
Adjusted age is your baby’s age based on her due date, rather than birth date. We take your baby’s chronological age (how old they are from the day they were born) and subtract how many weeks before their estimated due date they arrived.
⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀
Ex. if a baby is 12 weeks old, but was born 8 weeks early, their Adjusted Age is actually 4 weeks.
⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀
(Of note, early term is considered 37 weeks, so generally we only consider Adjusted Age if baby was born prior to this.)
⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀
We usually consider baby’s Adjusted Age, rather than actual age, when we are considering awake times!! ☁️]
