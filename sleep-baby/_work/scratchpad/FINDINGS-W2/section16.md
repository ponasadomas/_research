### 16.2 How long free fixes last

§13 said nobody knew. Wave 2 gives a partial answer. It comes from the few threads where the original poster came back, and most of those returns came within days or a few weeks, so **this shows the first relapse, not how long a fix lasts**.

- **R22's "2–4 weeks"** rests on one clear arc: "It worked last night. She slept 10.5 hrs." [sb22-0134], then 17 days later "your advice worked for a time. Then sleep fell apart" [sb22-0136], and "This cycling between optimism and failure feels unbearable." [sb22-0137]. Also "worked for us for the last two weeks … BUT" [sb22-0209].
- **Later readers mostly saw shorter.** A day or two: "It worked immediately for 2 days. Now we’re back in EMW territory" [sb31-0206]; "following the exact same schedule" the next day, 80 minutes of crying [sb27-0034]; "SEVEN straight hours", then up four times on the same changes [sb27-0324] [sb27-0325]; "slept thru!!!" then "Welp now she’s back to waking" [sb33-0073] [sb33-0074]; "it works for a 2 or 3 days and suddenly it doesn't" [sb39-0088]. A week to two: "For about a week his mood improved dramatically" [sb31-0402]; "resolved the early mornings for about 1-2 weeks" [sb24-0001]; "That lasted about two weeks" [sb38-0245]; "This lasted about 2 weeks." [sb28-0022]; "after two weeks of this working really well" [sb26-0349]. R39 names a two-week plateau: "I wonder why it’s always at 2 weeks!" [sb39-0425].
- **The repeating version** is the most useful quote here, from a father: "every few weeks his naps and bedtime suddenly become difficult again, we add about 10 to 15 minutes of awake time, and things settle down" [sb36-0208].
- **Some held longer, usually with tuning.** One mother followed one commenter for eight weeks: "5/7 days were good" [sb36-0319], then at eight weeks naps still slipped "1-2x a week" and "It’s stressful for me." [sb36-0322]. A toddler bedtime still worked 18 days later [sb29-0172]. A Ferber success held until about six months, and retraining then took "5 days" and was "sooo much easier" [sb32-0385] (the middle of that arc is [sb32-0384] (restricted)). Trained babies who stayed trained for months: [sb37-0251] [sb37-0192] [sb31-0260]. Against these, one failed at 14 days [sb29-0186] and one was "still an issue" six weeks on [sb39-0004].

Weighed honestly: R22's 2–4 weeks is not wrong, but it is one of the longer reads. Across R27–R40 the first relapse more often came within **a day to two weeks**. A minority held for months, and the long holds mostly came with ongoing tweaks or a quick retrain. With so few follow-ups, none of these figures is a rate (interpretation).

### 16.6 Method note

- **Restriction in wave 2 was per quote.** A unit with one unsafe line still gave usable quotes; only the line was flagged. `counts.md` gives 457 restricted of 14,179 for the whole file; 244 of the 7,286 wave-2 quotes are flagged (my count). No restricted id is quoted in this section.
- **Speakers.** Wave-2 readers set a mother label only when the author's own text said mum/mom, nursing, pumping or pregnancy. So many authors in r/Mommit, r/FirstTimeMom and r/beyondthebump are `parent_unspecified` (R26, R29, R39). Wave 2's split: `parent_unspecified` 3,297, `new_mother` 2,902, `experienced_mother` 700, `partner` 383. "Parent" in this section often means a mother who did not say so.
- **The brief shaped the readers.** Wave-2 readers were given section 10's claims and the earlier readers' tags to check. Some confirmations, and the high counts of tags such as `free_advice_wins` (340 in wave 2 against 81 in wave 1), `sleep_math`, `pre_empting_replies` (140, both spellings) and `retraining_after_setback`, partly reflect what they were asked to look for. Where a reader found nothing, that is shown above.
- **Tag spellings** were merged for counting (`undertired_overtired` / `overtired_undertired` / `overtired`; `pre_empting_replies` / `preempting_replies`; `neighbours` / `neighbours_space` / `space_limits`; and the success-story and exact-steps variants). `counts.md` lists each spelling separately.
- **Cross-posts.** 11 quote texts appear twice under two ids, because the same author posted in two subreddits and two readers quoted both posts (for example [sb23-0073] and [sb37-0371]). Counts include both copies.
- **Purchase set.** R22 read the purchase-keyword chunks, and `sleep_consultant` is on 110 of its 367 quotes, so consultant evidence is concentrated there.
- **Age range.** R22–R32's prompt said 0–24 months. Readers used the README's 0–36 months except R32, which skipped ordinary 2.5–3.5-year-olds, so per the orchestrator two in-scope units in R32's chunks were left unread.
- **Commands** (run from `mamivio.com/research/sleep-baby/`, helper `scratchpad/FINDINGS-W2/w2.py`, which reads `voice-merged/all.jsonl` and selects ids `sb22-` … `sb40-`): `python3 -I scratchpad/FINDINGS-W2/w2.py stats` → 7,286 quotes, 244 restricted, the speaker split; `… w2.py money` → 28 quotes with an amount; `… w2.py theme T1,T2` → each theme count above (spellings joined by commas); `… w2.py dup` → the 11 cross-posted pairs.

### 16.7 What this changes for the Mamivio plan

The verdict in section 10 stands: parents ask for a personal plan in so many words, paid help keeps failing them by being generic, and the price sits under every ceiling they state. Wave 2 changes three things (interpretation, from the ids above):

1. **What to do when a fix wears off is now the core of the product, not an extra.** The first relapse usually comes within days to two weeks (16.2), and parents ask for the restart rule before they will begin [sb40-0378] [sb37-0083]. The plan has to say what a relapse looks like, what to change first (the father's "add about 10 to 15 minutes" [sb36-0208] is the shape), when to restart the old method rather than start from scratch, and how to come back after travel, illness, teeth and nap transitions. The 3- and 6-month tiers are only worth more if they include this.
2. **It has to stand in for the person, on paper.** Parents pay people for accountability, reassurance and having a decision taken away, and they praise the consultant who came back after a setback. A document cannot watch the monitor. It can make the night decisions in advance [sb34-0095], give the stop rule and the trial length the parent is already inventing [sb36-0332] [sb27-0130], give the partner the same script [sb22-0355] [sb31-0259], and say what to do if it is not working yet. Next to Betteroo's €44.62 every 4 weeks for a person, Mamivio's case is a one-time price plus a plan that already contains the next step, not "personalised" alone.
3. **The price evidence moved.** Parents now name course prices ($100, $250, $300) and call them robbery or a waste, and consultant ceilings sit at $200 to $500. A $17.95–$49.95 plan sits below all of these, but no parent has named a price in that band, so its acceptance is still unknown.
