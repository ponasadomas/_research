### 17.2 Relapse, settled as far as the data allows

**The disagreement.** Most wave-3 readers (R45, R47, R49, R56, R57, R59, R60, R61, R62, R63) found what wave 2 found: a thread's fix holds a day to a week. "it worked very well for 3 days. Now we're back to square one" [sb59-0084]; the same parent's next good night lasted one day, "It went to shit after that lol but we did keep the schedule" [sb59-0095] [sb59-0094]; "first two night slept through it and I couldn't believe it, but last night and tonight i'm back at square one" [sb63-0148]; a six-hour last window worked one night, then "lol nvm. Did the same thing yesterday with the 6 hour final wake window" [sb58-0380] [sb58-0379]; weeks of changes "seemingly working for a night or two" [sb63-0380]. R42 and R55 found the opposite: late returners whose fixes held for weeks or months. "Returning to this thread after 2 months" with a baby who "regularly sleeps for 6-9 hours blocks" [sb55-0164]; a toddler who sleeps through "as long as we flex her bedtime based on when she woke up" three months on [sb55-0217] [sb55-0218]; ten weeks on, "Moving her to her own room is what really helped" [sb63-0337] [sb63-0338]; a father's home-built plan, "we built a version of the Ferber Method combined with many of the methods from The Happy Sleeper" [sb60-0323], four months on "only needs one check-in" [sb60-0325]; three wake-window fixes that held for weeks to months [sb42-0266] [sb42-0259] [sb42-0392].

**What decides it.** Three things separate the fixes that faded from the ones that held (interpretation, from the ids here).

1. **What the fix changed.** Fixes that nudged the clock by a few minutes on a schedule that was nearly right faded within days [sb59-0084] [sb58-0380] [sb63-0380] [sb59-0348]. Fixes that held either changed something other than the clock, or corrected a large mismatch. R58 shows the first kind: the baby was hungry ("he is a completely different baby" [sb58-0165]; [sb58-0164]); daily exercise, after which he "goes down for the night with 1 wake up" [sb58-0148]; less total sleep, "He was getting too much sleep!" [sb58-0371]; and the schedule first, then Ferber: "Three weeks later he is putting himself to sleep totally tear free" [sb58-0326]. A move to the baby's own room is a fourth [sb63-0337]. The large mismatch: "he was wildly under tired!" [sb42-0266]; extra daytime feeds plus 11 hours awake [sb55-0164], which also changed feeding, not only the clock.
2. **Whether a standing rule was kept.** The multi-month holds came with a rule the parent goes on applying: flex bedtime by wake time [sb55-0218]; adjust to daycare's one nap [sb63-0338]; one check-in, then wait [sb60-0325]; "If I lay him down, and he cries for more than a few minutes" something is off, and she checks the list [sb43-0398]. The free coach's rule for the future is the same shape: "You can always cut down on daytime sleep and add in more awake time" [sb43-0281]. So is wave 2's father [sb36-0208] and R50's "I did increase his first wake window by 15min and cut his nap down to 1hr45min" [sb50-0355].
3. **When the parent came back.** R42 warns that late returns lean towards success; most returns in R58 and R59 came within days. Tags for a fix that lasted (`fix_lasted` / `success_lasting`) are on 3 quotes and `restart_rule` on 4. This rests on a few dozen dated follow-ups. It is not a rate.

**Two clocks.** Several readers (R44, R54, R58, R59, R61) separate two arcs, and the data supports them. The thread fix fades in days; formal training holds for weeks to years and then breaks on one trigger. Training that held about two years ended when the dummy went and a toddler bed came in the same fortnight: "it feels like we've completely gone backwards" [sb59-0291] [sb59-0290]. "That lasted nearly 3 months", until separation anxiety, six teeth and an illness, when comfort "quickly became breastfeeding to sleep again" [sb61-0196] [sb61-0195]. CIO worked in two nights, "Had 3-4 months of great sleep", and since then "Typically he would have a rough 4-7 days and then go back to sleeping through" [sb58-0375]. One mother counted every fix: a room move, "That lasted 2 nights" [sb57-0429]; training, then "we only got a week of this before the baby got his first cold" [sb57-0431] [sb57-0430]; "This amazing routine lasted until he turned 10 months old" [sb57-0432]; the retrain, "hysterical after 10 minutes so we stopped" [sb57-0433]. The triggers readers name, across sets: travel (often one night away), illness, teeth, standing and crawling, daycare starting, a sibling, a house move, a bed move, and dummy weaning at about two [sb41-0406] [sb56-0295] [sb51-0377] [sb63-0221] (`dummy_weaning` 44 wave-3 quotes, a tag wave 2 did not have).

**What parents change first.** Every reader who looked found the same order. First the wake windows or the nap caps. Then the comfort they had trained away comes back during the setback: "Every time she cuts a tooth I end up comforting her more during the night" [sb45-0514]. Then the retrain question. R43 adds that the first change is often the wrong one: shortening windows "seemed to work! Her naps were much better. But her nights were not at ALL" [sb43-0451]; the cause was a feeding association, fixed in a few days [sb43-0452]. One mother says the order mattered the other way too: "no technique was going to help us. The schedule was severely messed up." [sb60-0434].

**Retraining.** It is often harder than the first time: "she cried so much, way more than she ever did when we first sleep trained" [sb54-0220]; "Nope. He was then up every 2 hours all night." [sb45-0024]; "it. just. does. not. work." [sb47-0105]; "It is so much harder this time cuz" she is more aware [sb50-0353]; and a parent who has tried four times says it "Just doesn't seem worth it to do it again for only a few weeks." [sb58-0195]. Sometimes it is quicker: "it only took two days to retrain" [sb43-0528]; "Nights are completely back to normal now." [sb49-0082]. One parent planned for travel and it held: "We'd tried to keep our bedtime routine as similar as possible" [sb49-0349].

**The restart rule is asked for in so many words.** "How do you handle vacations, sicknesses, teething, etc?" [sb63-0397]; "I'd like a strategy for when things go south." [sb55-0430]; "Do I just restart the TCB night weaning process from scratch?" [sb55-0295]; "Would you recommend I try Ferber again?" [sb55-0300] (the same parent, two posts); "Do you recommend going back to chair method night 1 or picking up from night 10+?" [sb54-0219]; "scrap everything and reset? What does that even look like?" [sb58-0072]; "Did it get better if you pushed through, or did you end up needing to reset?" [sb59-0294]; and before training has even started, "I just want to prep for if we happen to encounter those rare" wide-awake nights [sb59-0337]. Parents also set their own trial lengths: "we've given it 3 or more nights to see if it made any difference" [sb49-0176].

**What the plan's relapse section must contain** (interpretation, from the ids above):

1. **Tell the two clocks apart.** A schedule wobble (days) gets one change at a time, in steps of 10–15 minutes, judged after three nights [sb49-0176] [sb50-0355] [sb36-0208]. A trained baby's relapse after a trigger gets the retrain branch.
2. **Check the non-clock causes before touching the windows:** hunger, daytime activity, total sleep against this baby's need, where the baby sleeps, and the comfort that has crept back [sb58-0164] [sb58-0148] [sb58-0371] [sb63-0337] [sb43-0452].
3. **A rule for comforting during illness and teeth, and the way back.** "Respond, then return" rather than "start over" (R43's reading of [sb43-0398]); say which night of the method to restart from, and change it for an older or standing child [sb54-0219] [sb54-0220] [sb50-0353].
4. **Branches for each named trigger:** travel (keep the routine and the cot the same [sb49-0349]), illness, teeth, standing, daycare starting, a sibling, dummy weaning, the move to a bed.
5. **A stop rule and a vomiting rule** for the retrain (17.6).
6. **What to expect:** retraining can be harder than the first time, and a relapse is not a failure [sb54-0220] [sb43-0454].

**What the 3- and 6-month tiers must contain** (interpretation): dated re-checks of the schedule as sleep need falls ("her sleep needs have changed and I need to adjust" [sb55-0161]; [sb43-0281]); the nap transitions inside the period; the retrain protocol and trigger branches above; and, for babies who will pass about 18–24 months inside the tier, dummy weaning and the bed move. Without these the longer tiers are the same document for longer.

### 17.9 What this changes for the Mamivio plan

Section 16.7 stands. Wave 3 sharpens it in three places (interpretation, from the ids above):

1. **The relapse question has an answer the plan can use.** Fixes that only move the clock fade in days; fixes that address the real cause, or come with a standing rule, hold for weeks to months; trained babies hold for months and then break on one named trigger. The plan's setback section must tell these apart, check the non-clock causes first, and give the restart point and the trigger branches parents ask for [sb63-0397] [sb54-0219] [sb55-0430]. That section, plus dated re-checks as sleep needs fall, is what the 3- and 6-month tiers sell.
2. **The night has to be decided in the day, including the stop.** The gap parents name most sharply in books, consultants and doctors is what to do when the crying does not stop [sb62-0445] [sb42-0314] [sb60-0180]. A plan that gives a stop rule, a vomiting rule and a partner script answers a question nobody else in the corpus answers.
3. **Paying for a person is still the competition, and still narrow.** It shows up where people are already shopping for consultants, not across the ordinary night posts. Against it, wave 3 adds parents and partners who think the information is free [sb60-0423] [sb58-0343], and the first parent asking which online sleep plan is cheapest and whether it is a scam [sb63-0224]. Mamivio's price sits far below everything named; the open question is trust, not price.
