## Patterns

**1. The "bored in every wake window" cluster at 3–6 months is real and large here.** Triage flagged it at 4–5 months; this batch has it from 13 weeks to 6 months, in the parent's own words: "his wake windows are a nightmare […] grown so so bored of everything" [0049]; "How on earth do I keep my 5 month old entertained?" [0139]; "so much more alert but can't do much […] I don't know what to do to entertain her all day" [0163]; "ive always felt like he just gets bored […] understimulated half the time, or overstimulated the other half" [0202]; "can't be occupied or satisfied for more than 5 minutes" [0010]; "She gets bored with everything after about a minute" [0134]; "wants to be entertained all the time" at 6 months [0122]. The scene is the same each time: the parent has run through every activity before mid-morning and is crying [0205]. Searching makes it worse: Google says "babies that age are too young to be bored!" [0204], and "let him be bored" was tried and failed [0206].

**2. The ask, in their words.** "How do you spend your wake windows? Any fun activities?" [0052]; "what do YOU do all day? How do you keep them entertained?!" [0216]; "how do you like to structure your day? […] how do you handle the having to make-up stuff to do daily lol?" [0222]; "Will it get easier to entertain my baby?" [0164]; "Idk what do with her. I just need a solution to make my life easier." [0071]; "need some activities to let him forget about tv" (a father) [0038]. What they want from the answer: "simple diy or things she can do independently" [0142], not more toys ("I'm truly not a big toy person" [0143]), because "I'm not a super creative person" [0144]. The age link is explicit once: "this is just our first baby and we are constantly learning about age appropriate activities" [0145].

**3. They already have the list (confirms wave 1 §10 "Against").** "We have oodles of toys, we read books, we sing and dance […] I feel like I'm exhausting every option I can think of" [0050]; "all kinds of activities and games […] i build him a climbing playground" [0057]; "tummy time and floor play and a bouncer and a swing and books and singing" [0140]; "play, color, make sensory activities and do pretend play. It's. Never. Enough." [0226]; toy rotation already done [0094]. The gap is minutes and stamina, not ideas.

**4. Won't play alone / can't be put down: realistic minutes, in numbers.** 3 minutes [0011], 5 minutes [0010], "even for 5 mins while I eat" [0190], 10 minutes in a pack and play [0122], 10–15 minutes on the play mat [0136], 20 minutes [0029], "maybe 2 minutes" with a room full of toys [0227], 45 seconds in a playroom [0219], "play for a minute" [0150]. Scenes: 3 hours to fold one basket of laundry [0228]; "Velcro", "barnacle", "FOMO" baby [0082], [0149], [0054]. Fears attached: being kicked out of daycare [0106], [0173]. The partner hands the baby back [0172].
Contrary: a 13-month-old who plays alone most of the day, and the mother still feels guilty for not "playing with her" [0014]; a 7-month-old whose independent play has arrived [0223]; a baby who plays alone on the nanny camera but not with the parent [0230].

**5. Mobility frustration, 4–9 months.** Babies furious that they can't crawl yet: "shouting whilst he is rocking […] burying his face in the floor and crying" [0076]; "four hours straight of whining" [0078]; "her dreams of crawling but the screeching is nonstop" [0175]; a 9-month-old who only wants to "walk" holding fingers, "I can't do this 10 hours a day" [0093]. Grandmother: "she'd drop as at a babybox if we were this frustrated" [0208]. "It's so discouraging when people tell you it'll get easier once they can roll/crawl/walk" [0186]. The parents' ask is "not rushing things, just want to support her" [0209], with one wanting to "accelerate the learning process" [0048].

**6. Tummy time.** Hate [0047], [0166], [0196]; "how long am I supposed to let him cry" [0147]; a paediatrician's "10-15 min goal a day" that a 4-week-old cannot meet [0188]; a midwife saying shoulder-carrying "isn't tummy time […] there is so much conflicting information" [0200]; regret after weeks of survival with a crying baby [0198].

**7. Container worry is information-driven.** Parents want a seat for 10 minutes and a coffee but have "read a lot of stuff online saying floor seats aren't good for babies" [0174]; a gifted bug seat: "would that hinder his development?" [0169]; "is it even worth it to get a chair at this point?" [0073]; a bouncer bought before birth was "a mistake as it has no source of entertainment" [0124]; the toy bar matters because otherwise "I have to sit with baby and dangle toys" [0126]. An activity centre that worked [0210]; a playpen planned [0170].

**8. Screens under 2: guilt, rules, gaps, relatives.** Guilt: 30 minutes twice a week while 38 weeks pregnant, "Social media has led me to believe it is the devil" [0027]; Ms Rachel twice at 3 months [0155]; a 1-year-old who eats only with Ms Rachel on [0157]–[0158]; Teletubbies creeping from one episode to four [0176]. The rule they cite: paediatricians "zero screen time until 18 months" [0111], NHS "no screen time before two but how is that actually viable for modern mothers?" [0177], "research and our pediatrician" [0087]. Their own rules: "Always on the TV, never ever on a phone" [0028]; a list of "nothing too over stimulating" shows [0032]. The gaps screens fill: cooking and eating [0155], admin and deadlines [0176]–[0178], meals [0157], a parent's own need for TV [0112]–[0113]. Flights: a father wants an emergency Fire tablet, the mother says 18 months is too young [0024]–[0026]. Relatives' TVs and phones: in-laws' week-long TV holiday [0085]–[0089], an aunt giving a 3-month-old Cocomelon [0099], "use the phones as a pacifier" [0100], a grandmother's Cocomelon at 8 months [0037], an uncle's phone [0108].
