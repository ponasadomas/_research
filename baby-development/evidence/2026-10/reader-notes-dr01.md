## Patterns

1. **"What do I do with the baby all day" is a 2–6-month problem and a 12–18-month problem, in different words.** At 3–6 months it is the parent's own exhaustion of performing: "constantly just come up with things to talk about with her while watching her swing at her toys" (0089), "fake smile and parentese voice" (0090), "the baby cruise director all day long" (0103), "every day is the same" (0114), "awkward stage where he can't really do much" (0116), toys hold for "1-4 minutes" (0180–0182). At 12–24 months it is filling the hours: "the things to do with her at home I kinda blank on" (0016), "How are we entertaining kids all day?!" (0153), "buy endless amounts of toys and still not fill up an entire day" (0106), "harder and harder to find things to keep her occupied that aren't outside activities" (0172), "I'm the constant entertainment" (0169). The 6–9-month version is a baby who wants to move before they can (0101, 0144–0145, 0156, 0189, 0208).
2. **The parent has usually already done the long list; the post is about it not working.** Tummy time, toy rotation, library, songs, TikTok activities (0102); "Montessori toys … Nothing entertains them" (0154); rotation and fewer toys (0167); every chew toy (0184); toys out of reach (0209). What they ask for is what fits *this* week, not more ideas.
3. **Structure is the stated desire behind kits.** "structured and you don't have to worry about findjng the supplies" (0017), "which one and was it worth it?" (0018); Lovevery used as "a good guide" without buying (0033); "Is there a list somewhere? I don't love to just blindly google" (0118); "there's no rulebook on how to do this" (0098); "struggling to find good resources" (0032). This is the closest the corpus comes to the guide's promise.
4. **Toy buying is a guilt and clutter loop.** "I'm a bad mom who doesn't get them anything" (0009); "play room exploded" (0041); grandparents' toys she "loses interest in within a few minutes" (0074–0076); "does better when he has less options" (0038); "old toys are a bit too young and everything else seems a bit too old" (0047) — an age-band complaint. Partners are the brake (0001, 0010).
5. **Screens under 2: guilt is the default, defence is loud and minority.** Guilt: 0090, 0173–0176 ("have I ruined him for life?"), 0179, 0203–0204, 0069 (spouse's phone at 4 months, 0068). Defence: 0082–0085 ("an ideal but not a reality"). Ms Rachel is used or considered as a speech aid by worried parents (0111, 0218) and rejected as a replacement for reading by others (0007, 0187).
6. **Tummy time: hate plus a professional telling them to do more.** Doctor "said he needs more tummy time" (0148); grandmother "im hurting his development" (0149); "sick of trying to enforce something that's low-key agonizing for us both" (0165); "Tummy time? We don't know her." (0131); tried list (0151, 0164); head shape and guilt (0192–0193, 0205–0207, restricted). Contrary: a daycare that does not do tummy time at all (0166) and commenters who skipped it (0152).
7. **What professionals said decides how the parent feels for weeks.** Harsh: the 8–12-month review nurse (0053–0057, 0061) — "learning more through this Reddit post than at the review" (0059). Reassuring: "she can see that he understands and wants to talk" (0129), "everything looks normal" (0191), "good because most babies say no first" (0143), nursery naming the child "intense" felt good (0200). Thin: "Our ped just talks about super generic milestones" (0117), "her doctor didn't say much" (0178), HV "wait and watch until his 12 months review" (0111).
8. **Comparison comes from classes, antenatal friends, twins and feeds.** Baby group (0195–0197), antenatal course meet-up (0135), maternity-leave friends (0096–0097), daycare peers (0122), twin sibling (0112), friend's same-due-date baby (0119), "comparisons online everywhere" (0142). Counter-voice: milestones "literally don't mean a single thing" (0071–0073).
9. **Relatives and partners mostly comment, rarely help.** "talker not a walker" (0133), household relatives' babies "scream less" (0140), "too clingy and too spoilt" (0170), mother-in-law "difficult baby compared to the other ones" (0027), grandmother doing no activities (0080–0081), partner on dolls (0062, 0093), on daycare (0065), on toys (0001, 0010). Fathers' own voice: 0074–0076, 0120–0121, 0177–0178.
10. **Classes can backfire.** Story time ends in tears (0077–0079); 0–5 stay-and-plays have "nothing for her to do" (0210); holding the baby the whole class (0196). Relief came from permission to skip (0099).

## Contrary evidence

- Some parents explicitly do *not* need ideas: "I have no problem entertaining baby" (0004); "not particularly worried … babies all learn skills at their own pace" (0208, 0211); "She'll walk when she feels inclined to" (0092).
- Household objects beat toys repeatedly (0003, 0046, 0120, 0184) — undercuts paid kits and supports a guide built on what is already at home.
- Kit buyers can be happy: Lovevery "one of the baby purchases I'm happiest with", resale £50 of £80 (0019–0021; that post carries a referral code).
