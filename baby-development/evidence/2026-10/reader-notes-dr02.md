## Patterns

1. **"What do I do with the baby all day" is a time problem, not an idea problem.** Parents already list what they do (mat, tummy time, books, singing, walks, sensory bags) and then say it fills 20 minutes or one hour of an 8–11 hour day [0002, 0004, 0045, 0126, 0216, 0306, 0369]. The scene is the clock: "Today it got to 1pm and I realised I had 5 hours to fill" [0322]; "now what do I do with you for 7 more hours until bedtime?" [0126, a father]. It peaks at three moments: 3–5 months, when naps shorten and wake windows lengthen [0320, 0368, 0307]; the drop to one nap at 11–15 months [0117, 0336]; and long solo days with no car, heat, rain or no village [0144, 0336, 0049]. Expectant parents ask it before birth [0156, 0158].
2. **The ask is for structure and age-fit, in their words.** "Are any of you SAHM that use a sort of “system” for each day? Almost like at home daycare" [0289, then "Please do share an outline" 0291]; "The more specific the better. What do your days look like?!" [0324]; "Do you have a routine of playing and napping?" [0370]; "What are yall doing all day? What’s your routines look like?" [0201]. Age-fit matters: ideas meant for older babies landed as worry ("But do they do those things at 3 months? … now I’m worried she’s behind 😂" [0327]); online activity lists made a parent fear the child "will suffer" without them [0181]; the toy-rescue idea "lasts approximately 10 seconds" at 10 months [0009]; set-up takes longer than the baby's interest [0200]. This is the strongest support in my chunks for an age-selected plan; it is a handful of posts, not a census.
3. **Awake-time guilt runs in both directions.** Guilt for setting the baby down (bouncer, mat, playpen) [0016, 0108, 0109, 0130, 0068, 0214] and, in the same post, guilt for never letting them play alone [0134]. "I basically feel guilty whenever I’m not full on interacting with him" [0133]; "How on earth do I just chill?!" [0135]; "reassurance that i don’t need to optimize my baby’s every waking moment" [0145]. The reverse self-blame also appears: "it’s my fault because I’ve entertained her too much and now she can’t entertain herself" [0299]. What reassured them: hearing that floor time is not doing less [0018]; seeing their own baby meet milestones after screens [0279]. What did not: "Be thankful you have a good baby" from family and paediatrician [0070].
4. **Won't play alone / can't be put down is its own cluster, 3–12 months, with a time unit.** Parents measure independence in minutes: "Max 5 minutes" [0001], "5-10 minutes? Or enough for me to pee?" [0051], "an extra 10/15 minutes of independent playtime would go a long way" [0207], "10 mins" [0083]. Several had a baby who played alone at 3–6 months and stopped at 7–8 months [0317, 0165]. Fathers report the same, plus the baby settling for the mother but not for them [0127, 0177, 0180].
5. **Comparison comes from named people and feeds.** A friend's partner measuring both babies' legs [0013, 0014]; a same-age cousin [0098, 0280]; a sister's baby [0257]; the antenatal group doing swimming, sensory and flights [0293]; a neighbour with WhatsApp groups (same unit); mothers' groups "very milestone-centered … “Is he doing xyz? …Oh.”" [0356] and a mother who avoids them as "dick-measuring contests over developmental milestones" [0352]. Instagram "come spend the day with me and my 6-month-old" reels [0025], TikToks of other moms [0027], and Facebook videos of babies self-feeding at six months [0056].
6. **Screens under 2: guilt, rules, and defence side by side.** Ms Rachel as the emergency tool at 4 months ("next time I’m in a pinch … for less then 30min") [0042, 0043], at meals ("I became that mom") [0153], for a toddler while a newborn is sore [0235]; a partner pushing Ms Rachel because a reel claimed 10–15 words [0350, 0351]; a lactation consultant putting on a high-contrast video to stop crying, which then became the best tummy time [0361, 0362]. Defence voices: background TV for an isolated mother [0297], "I gave up on being 100% screen free" [0198], Bluey for breakfast [0276]. No-screen families and their cost: "I’ve put this pressure on myself to keep her entertained without it" [0337, 0120].
7. **Tummy time.** Hate from 7 weeks to 7 months [0345, 0033, 0094 "forced tummy time"]; "I usually just give in and stop tummy time" [0346]; "tummy time boot camp … because I've been so negligent" [0167]; a doctor saying "more tummy time" to a baby already on the floor 3–5 hours [0189]; conflicting surface advice [0030]; head-shape guilt that drives more carrying and less setting down [0131, 0334, 0335]. A success: 11 minutes once tolerated [0114].
8. **Toys and kits: abundance, not lack.** Hand-me-downs of "all the popular toys for this age group" [0076]; noisy gifted VTech from the partner's family [0091, 0093]; viral products that disappointed: Baby Einstein aquarium "does nottt entertain her as much as the Amazon reviews/Instagram says" [0084], the "aquarium thing that everyone raves about" for 3–5 minutes [0065], the talking cactus in a drawer [0333], a vibrating rocker [0089], a  Amazon climber "really flimsy" [0192]. Running out of ideas "without spending heaps of money" [0170].
9. **Professionals' words, quoted by parents.** Health visitor 9-month review and referral [0341, 0342, 0343]; paediatrician milestone survey as the trigger of worry [0123, 0124]; a 2-month check "a little behind on muscle tone" [0373]; doctors reassuring and parents not reassured ("Like, how does that help me?" [0230, 0231]); a daycare educator who made a mother feel "uneasy that hes behind" [0184]; a physio saying "she is just taking her time" [0055]. All restricted.

## Contrary evidence

- Parents of content, early-moving babies exist and also post: a content 5.5-month-old left in her playpen or swing while her mother does chores [0298]; an 11-month-old walking at 9 months [0223]; "Every milestone … came really early" [0223]; a calm eldest who "never really did much" at 6 months and is fine at 4 [0171]. Comparison runs both ways.
- Some parents reject structured enrichment: the homebody mother [0352, 0353], the parent who "normally just let her play with her toys how she wants" [0181].
- Reassurance does land sometimes: the strangers on the walk [0295], the comment thread that made a mother "trust my gut" [0284], relief at a first body-part game [0151].
