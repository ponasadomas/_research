### The parent wants to get past fear around offering solid food.

12 of 154 posts.

Babies are roughly 6–12 months old, and fear of choking, gagging or vomiting makes offering food difficult even when the baby wants it. Some parents limit texture, delay practice, seek first-aid reassurance or ask for a few foods they can manage; one wants a worried partner to trust the baby. One connects the fear to an older child turning blue while choking, and another names emetophobia.

- "I am so damn scared my baby will choke just like my first. I never seemed to get over that fear. I am not sure how to get him to transition." [sr041-0080]
- "But it gives me CRIPPLING ANXIETY. I just have a fear of him choking." [sr006-0164]
- "I've tried some egg strips and now pasta exactly like I'm supposed to serve it, but she always just sucks the whole thing in her mouth and it scares the bejesus." [sr030-0026]
- "I have emetophobia ( fear of vomit ) and I just can’t do it.." [sr131-0047]
- "I think because I’m alone and she’s already scared me with the choking." [sr122-0025]

**But:** Fear is not shared by every parent involved: "But i can’t be afraid, i just have to watch him and teach him." [sr032-0007] Another parent is already sharing tiny pieces: "We have definitely shared a tiny bit of food! Just not full portions yet since I was spooked lol." [sr020-0042]

### The parent wants to move beyond purées but the child gags, vomits or rejects the texture.

13 of 154 posts.

At about 6.5–16 months, textured or thicker food can end an attempt with gagging, coughing or vomiting. The range includes grittier meat purée, tiny lumps, bread, eggs and finger foods; parents ask whether this is taste, texture, normal practice or a problem needing help. Some retain purées to get food in, and others describe cleanup and work making repeated trials harder.

- "My baby is 9 months old and refuses to eat anything other than purees. Any time I try mashed fruits or anything with the tiniest bit of texture, he gags and pukes. Did anyone else have this problem and how do I get past it?" [sr058-0009]
- "But any time I give her anything even remotely thicker she gags until she projectile vomits. (i.e. pureed turkey in turkey broth. Still a puree, but a grittier texture)." [sr116-0067]
- "Now she dont know how to chew solid food. She can eat yogurt bites, crackers, cookies etc." [sr039-0088]
- "The couple of times I have given them to him, he chokes and ends up puking everything in his stomach." [sr091-0149]
- "I know gagging is developmentally appropriate, but the vomiting makes it so much harder since I work from home full time and can't consistently stop to bathe her, change her whole outfit, wash the entire high chair, etc." [sr116-0069]

**But:** Tolerance is selective rather than absent across all textures: "She will happily chew on baby rusks and cucumbers, but struggles with pancakes and omelettes (will either shred them without eating or gag)." [sr068-0055]

### The parent wants the child to chew and pace bites instead of swallowing too much at once.

4 of 154 posts.

These posts describe babies around 7 months and 9–12 months, and toddlers of 17 and 23 months. Parents report swallowing before chewing, big bites, rapid shovelling, gagging or choking scares, including back slaps and three reported baby Heimlich interventions. They try modelling chewing, placing food to the side, feeding the child themselves or pausing between spoonfuls, but the child may resist.

- "He loves his food but just will not chew and we’re having near-choking incidents almost every day now. It’s terrifying. I’m still shaking from the last one almost half an hour ago." [sr055-0030]
- "I‘ve modelled chewing at every meal, tried to place the food to the side of his mouth, eaten same time as him, encouraged him to chew on toys (which he does) but he just won’t chew the food." [sr055-0034]
- "She will shovel food in her mouth, take big bites, etc. And when she gets too much in there, she will gag and spit it out, on herself, the carpet, etc." [sr011-0072]
- "We tried this morning with breakfast to wait a bit between each spoonful but she got more and more angry and kept crying." [sr118-0081]
- "He DOES it, just not super effectively and sometimes he does just outright forget to chew before swallowing his food, so I'd prefer to keep food on the softer/moister side" [sr055-0060]

**But:** The concern can coexist with a strong appetite and growing independence: "My daughter (23 months) is generally a good eater - independent, likes to feed herself, learning fork and spoon well, etc." [sr011-0072]

### The parent keeps offering foods but the child eats very little or mostly plays with them.

From 5 months to about 15 months, parents report mouthing, licking, smashing, dropping or throwing food instead of consistently eating it. They offer purées, finger foods, different flavours and textures, shared meals or preloaded spoons, and some say the child mouths every non-food object.
