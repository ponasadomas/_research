### She feels rejected despite doing the daily care and wants to feel loved by her child.

4 of 40 posts.

These parents describe two- and three-year-olds who choose dad, relatives or almost anyone else while refusing their comfort, play or affection. They contrast the rejection with patience, daily care, appointments and activities, and ask whether the child loves them or whether the relationship will stay this way. One pregnant mother describes raising her voice, then leaving the room crying; another mother worries about a future estrangement.

- "I can’t have special moments with him because he’s always looking for dad. I can’t hold him when we go out to eat, I can’t walk with him holding my hand, I can’t go down the slide with him, only dad gets to do these things." [ar027-0069]
- "It would be one thing if she preferred just her dad, but she prefers EVERYONE over me. Her aunts, her grandma and nana, her uncles, literally if anyone else is around, she immediately ignores me and goes to them." [ar015-0026]
- "I think it sucks even more because I’m the default parent. I arrange all the appointments, make sure we have everything we need, go through the wardrobe and make sure the clothes still fit, buy the new season—appropriate clothes, pack the old clothes away, check milestones are being hit, set up activities and plan days out for them." [ar060-0113]
- "Does she just need a break from me?? I’m losing my mind.   
It feels like she doesn’t love me or wants to be around me which is also heartbreaking." [ar052-0026]

**But:** One mother recalls being specifically wanted during illness, so her account includes an exception to never being preferred: "The only time I can think she requested me above anyone else is when she was hospitalized for RSV at the beginning of this year." [ar015-0024]
