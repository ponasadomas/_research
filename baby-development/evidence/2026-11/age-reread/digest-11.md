## Pattern 9, share 11: yes posts

### The parent weighs more time with other children against keeping their child at home.

11 of 140 posts.

Parents of two- and three-year-olds ask whether peers, language exposure, stimulation, structure or practice being away from home justify starting preschool or increasing its hours. Some already provide daily outings, classes or grandparent care; others describe little peer contact, two parents working at home, a new baby or their own need for respite. They ask about the age and amount of attendance that helps, sometimes under pressure from a partner or professional.

- "going from 100% at home to 5 full days of preschool seems like a radical shift for my 2yr old, but i feel like we're simply not getting him the level of socialization and active engagement he needs in our current setup" [ar080-0061]
- "She said that he needs a lot more exposure otherwise he will fall behind once he enters school at 6 and that I can’t expect kindergarten to do the work for me in a year." [ar081-0019]
- "we just had our second baby, and juggling two kids hasn’t been easy (I’m a SAHM) Having a 3 hour break even 2 times a week would be great for me, and also let him get some time out of the house." [ar018-0110]
- "Anyway, I am PRAYING to the Universe to please, please allow the social time and structure and play based nature of this preschool to please give her space from her brother, stimulation (she is very smart), and then when she gets home, her brother will need a nap, and I can provide quiet time activties." [ar012-0157]
- "I'm wondering if there is an age where the benefits of going to school and being among peers 5 days a week would outweigh the benefits we are getting now from the part-time arrangement?" [ar079-0051]
- "I've heard it said here that pre-k/daycare is beneficial starting at 3 but is there any research on how much is beneficial?" [ar082-0030]

**But:** The parents do not all see an unmet social need or feel ready for another transition. "I have no concerns about his “academics”, I think the only real benefit from school for him would be the social-emotional aspect, \*but\* I don’t know if he is socially and emotionally ready for it." [ar003-0072]

### The parent checks whether a school's approach, staffing and surroundings suit their child.

14 of 140 posts.

Parents choosing care or preschool for children roughly two to almost four compare Montessori, play-based, nature, Waldorf, conventional and gymnastics-based settings. They look for movement, suitable expectations, warm adults, safe ratios and support for suspected or diagnosed developmental differences, while weighing cost, travel, political commitments or religious teaching. Some ask how to assess a teacher or observe a placement before another difficult year.

- "We just want him to have a socially positive year surrounded by people who don’t label him as a “problem child” and who genuinely enjoy him and value his strengths and abilities (he’s a wonderful kid)." [ar055-0056]
- "I am wondering if Montessori could help him develop much needed independence and social skills, or whether he and his teachers would really just be butting heads all year? Other option would be more of an outdoor nature school." [ar058-0055]
- "\-even if there is 1 educator per 11 children should we be looking for smaller groups of children no matter how many educators? for eg our closest kinder had over 30 children, is that too big (again even if there are 3 educators)?" [ar060-0014]
- "Did they get enough physical outlet and outdoor time? How was their development with extra-curriculars like singing, dancing, and sports?" [ar010-0130]
- "Mais je suis très dérangée, politiquement, par l'idée de quitter le public (même si c'est pour y revenir), de ne pas être dans une école totalement mixe socialement et aussi, plus étrangement, de ne pas offrir une expérience "normale" à ma fille." [ar017-0005]
- "One place genuinely told me ‘our primary focus is spreading the gospel to the children’. 🤦‍♀️" [ar048-0133]
- "How can I be sure that she won’t get abused at daycare? (Considering licensed home daycare so they’re less kids and the teacher can be more attentive)." [ar005-0079]

**But:** The concern is not always that the current approach fails; one parent says the child enjoys a much-questioned schedule. "My toddler loves it and learns a lot from circle time." [ar011-0132]

### The parent weighs an older or younger classroom against friendships, maturity and the teacher.

4 of 140 posts.

Parents of children about two years seven months to three and a half weigh moving up, remaining in a younger group or choosing between parallel classrooms. Toileting independence, stimulation from older children, a better teacher and keeping friends can point in different directions. One parent who chose longer-term class stability now describes a child missing her best friend and blaming herself for the separation.

- "They said that she was independent, never had any potty accident and should be ready for the next class. They also said that she could meet some of her old friends there( who had already turned three)" [ar077-0081]
- "The primary 3s room has a much better teacher in it. The upper 3s has a teacher who I don't like for multiple reasons." [ar019-0124]
- "She’s very social and attached to them, so part of me worries she’ll notice that they moved up while she stayed with the younger group. I also wonder whether being one of the oldest will be less stimulating for her." [ar079-0118]
- "She repeated in the car to herself “it’s all my fault” and when I asked her what she meant she said it was all her fault she wasn’t with her friend anymore." [ar036-0075]

**But:** Keeping a particular friendship is not presented as an unqualified benefit. "They said she only copied her and followed her around and her friend bosses her around. They said she wasn’t gaining independence at school." [ar036-0074]

### The parent wants separation at daycare or school to feel easier for their child and themselves.

21 of 140 posts.

Parents of children aged two to almost four describe crying, clinging, anticipatory dread or chasing the departing adult, sometimes after months or years of previously happy attendance. They try quick handoffs, repeated goodbye words, reassurance, familiar teachers, comfort objects or another parent doing drop-off, and ask how long adjustment takes or whether to withdraw. The range includes children who settle quickly, those reported to cry or ask for the parent well into the day, food refusal, disrupted nights and a nonwalking twin whose father wonders about isolation.

- "He can walk into the building by himself, excitedly exclaim “friends!” while walking towards his classroom, but when it comes to crossing the threshold, he won’t do it." [ar037-0054]
- "Parents always say "oh it will get better in a few weeks" i turn and say "its been going on 2 years"." [ar012-0086]
- "I’m not sure what to do now that my toddler is literally chasing after us making the drop off period very stressful and prolonged." [ar053-0104]
- "He went back to daycare this past Monday and we just wrapped up day 4, with him still crying/whining all day, following the teacher around, and NOT eating." [ar024-0038]
- "My son does have a mild form of cerebral palsy and cannot walk yet. I think this may be contributing to his feeling of isolation there as his sister and all the other 3.5 year olds are running around." [ar029-0074]
- "and wakes up at night alot to come to our bed. and i just feel like shes regressed alot... i dont know if i should withdraw her and start prek at 3.7 instead or i would be harming her i really dont know.. the clingy part worries me alot.." [ar079-0135]
- "he told me last night that it feels like it’s a long time before Mama comes back, and it broke my heart." [ar018-0171]

**But:** One update reports substantial improvement after a consistent goodbye, a bracelet and an arrival job. "Where we are now: not magically tear-free, but the crying has gone from full-body panic to a quick whine and then he walks in. Twice this week he ran ahead of me." [ar012-0060]
