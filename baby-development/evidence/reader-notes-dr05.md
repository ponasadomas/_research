## Patterns

**1. The first quotable ask for a paid, age-staged plan.** A parent keeping a 17-month-old home instead of Montessori school: "Does anyone have recommendations for paid programs, guides, or structured weekly plans designed for toddlers at this stage? Ideally something flexible, easy to follow" [0003], "a gentle, age-appropriate framework we can follow" [0002]. A 7-month-old's parent asks for a book on "what to do from 7 months onwards and which skills they build on each month(s)" [0115], "what's relevant for which month is what I need help with" [0120], "I'd like to have sort of guide when I feel like lost" [0121], and in the same breath says "I'm not by any means trying to buy every book and toy under the sun" [0122]. Kit guides are named as part of a kit's value: "The kit guides are also very helpful for someone who has basically no experience" [0012]; "I do like the concept of included parents guide" [0037]. Still no one in this batch buys a guide, course or app; these are asks, not purchases.

**2. The design rule a parent wrote for us: milestones without weeks.** Thanking a commenter: "Just a bunch of milestones we can keep an eye out for without assigning them to defined weeks" [0190]. The other side of the same coin: a kit app's milestone notification ("baby might be hitting this milestone!") that left the parent fearing it was her fault [0015] (restricted); "my day revolves around all the items I should be using" [0013]; a day without flashcards or tummy time = guilt [0014]; Wonder Weeks "easily creates the same anxious feelings" [0017]; "these play kits are creating more pressure than helping" [0254]; deleting the app "feel[s] so revolutionary" [0018], [0022]. A CDC checklist overriding the doctor [0245] (restricted); a paediatrician's worksheet the parent found she was already doing [0222] (restricted). This strengthens §10 "lists can hurt" and §16 (b1) with unrestricted quotes [0013], [0014], [0017], [0254].

- Parents who reject buying and structure: "Detangling myself as a parent from capitalism" [0006]; "do I need to just chill out?" [0004]; toddlers "can play with the same toy every single day" [0059]; "I'm more than happy to make my own and find random objects" [0122].
- Parents who want an app for fun, not tracking: "not a very anxious mom […] rather than tracking or comparing" [0045].
- Reassurance that came from outside any product: "have you ever met an adult that can't hold their head up?" (read online, in [0018]'s thread; not quoted); "development can happen slowly and then all at once" [0063]; "It's all different for every baby just easy to get sucked into milestones and set times!" [0292].
- The reverse belief Mamivio may not promise: early effort made the first child "incredibly advanced" [0170]; daycare "developed more milestones" [0288].
- Babies who do play alone: 30–40 minutes [0058]; 15–20 minutes [0154]; a 17-month-old whose independent play worries the parent the other way [0299].
