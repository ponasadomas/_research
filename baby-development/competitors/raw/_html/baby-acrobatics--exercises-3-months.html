Baby reaching for toys at 3 months: what a miss tells you that a toy in the fist can't

A hand moves toward a toy. It misses. Then it comes back but by a slightly different route. If you're watching the toy, nothing happened. If you're watching the movement, that's new.

Written by Andri Peetso

Creator of Baby Acrobatics

Updated Sep 13, 20264 min read

Three reaches you'll see this month

The stare and the flapping arms
The toy's right there. Both arms go, and nothing gets any closer to it. It looks like excitement, and it is, but it's also the first part of a reach.
The hand that goes past
One arm moves toward it, misses, and comes back another way. If you only look at the toy, you'll miss this one completely.
The toy in the fist
You put it in their hand, the fingers close around it, and they hold on for a while. It looks like the finished version of the other two. It isn't.

What our son was doing before his hand connected

For our son, reaching became a favorite activity almost as soon as he started noticing the toys we'd dangle in front of him. Watching his little hand finally connect was a moment of pure pride for all of us.

There were a lot of misses before that, and the obvious thing to do with a miss is to help. Put the toy in the hand. The fingers close around it and everybody's happy.

Why the toy in the fist tells you nothing

Here's why that tells you nothing. Right now their hand closes on whatever touches the palm on its own, and it will for another month or so. Nobody decided anything. The people who score infant movement for a living don't count a toy pushed into the fist. Neither should you.

What they do count is the reach that misses. Before about four months, grabbing on purpose mostly fails, because the hand closes on its own before they've decided anything, and the shoulder isn't steady enough to aim yet. Nothing is lost in those months. And a baby who stares at the toy and flaps both arms at it without getting anywhere is doing something with a name, prereaching, and they count that too.

Three things inside a reach

So there are three separate things inside a reach, and they don't arrive together. Noticing the toy comes first, and the flapping is what it looks like. Aiming an arm at it comes next, and it needs a steady shoulder, which is why the hand goes past for a while. Closing the hand on purpose comes last, once the hand stops closing by itself. The miss you saw this morning is the middle one turning up, and a toy in the fist skips all three.

What to do with the toy

So that's mostly what there is to do. Put the toy where they can see it and where the arm can get to it, and then don't finish the reach for them.

And that's the change this month. With a younger baby you do all the moving. Now they start doing the part they can do. Let it. Do less.

If you remember one thing

A toy you put in their hand tells you nothing, because the hand closes on its own. A reach that misses and a hand that flaps at the toy both count. Wait. Don't put it in their fist.

One side first is normal

One arm first, one side only, a knee that resists today and gives tomorrow. That's variation. What's worth noticing is a pattern that stays the same however you change the position, the side or the moment, or a clear change from what they could do before.
