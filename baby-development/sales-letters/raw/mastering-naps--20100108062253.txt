 The Nap / Night Connection 

 For most parents, eliminating or reducing baby and toddler naps seems like a good idea. After all, it logically follows that the less sleep they get during the day, the more they’ll need at night…and the more they need at night, the better your chances of a deep, uninterrupted night’s rest. Right?

 Wrong. Unfortunately, this is one of the few cases in parenthood when your instincts aren’t reliable. According to most parenting experts, babies as young as four months old and toddlers well into their toddling years all need a good, solid foundation of sleep during the day in order to get the sleep they need at night.

 The truth is that the better rested babies and toddlers are during the day, the better they’ll fall asleep – and stay asleep . Overtired children are much more prone to struggle with their bedtime routines and typically have a more difficult time soothing themselves during those late-night wakings. They also tend to be fussier throughout the day, often losing out on those vital playing and learning opportunities that are so important during these early years . The end result is not only less rest for them and less rest for you, but a bit more of that crankiness we can all do without.

 It’s also been proven beneficial for babies and toddlers to know what to expect during their daytime routines. If you make it a habit to enforce napping at the same time every day and using the same techniques, your child will learn to look forward to the structure of their lives – including the obligatory nap.
