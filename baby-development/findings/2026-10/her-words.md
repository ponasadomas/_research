## Her vocabulary

Taken from `~/GitHub/_research/new-and-expecting-mothers/evidence/jargon-glossary.md`, the general glossary for mothers; this niche has no glossary of its own yet. Only the glossary's terms that also appear in this niche's quotes are listed, each with the number of the 2,970 quotes that contain it, counted by a word search. The glossary was written for another product, so its remarks about that product are left out.

- **tummy time**: supervised time on the front to build strength. Cheerful nursery-poster vocabulary with an edge of obligation: babies often hate it, so it also appears in guilt. Warm and safe. (found in 143 quotes)
- **wake windows**: how long a baby can comfortably stay awake between sleeps; days get planned around them. The language of tracking apps, used in earnest by some and mocked by others as optimising gone too far. Safe, but any hint that she is getting them wrong lands as pressure. (found in 50 quotes)
- **independent play**: a child amusing himself without an adult. Aspirational, and bitterly joked about by those whose baby will not do it. Warning: promising it sounds like an over-claim. (found in 71 quotes)
- **Ms Rachel**: the YouTube presenter who is the default acceptable screen time. Affectionate and guilty at once; naming her is a small confession. Warm to nod to. Warning: do not moralise about screen time near it. (found in 47 quotes)
- **early intervention**: assessment and therapy for a suspected delay in development or speech. Reassuring from another parent, devastating when it lands. Warning: never imply that a product stands in for it. (found in 37 quotes)
- **PT / OT**: physical and occupational therapy. Warning: the glossary notes that PT often means pelvic-floor physiotherapy among mothers; in this niche's quotes it is the child's therapist, so write the words out. (found in 40 quotes)
- **put down / goes down**: of a baby: laid in the cot, or settles to sleep. "Won't be put down" is the usual way to say a baby settles only while held. Practical and weary. Warning: both phrases have other readings, so use them only with the baby plainly as the subject. (found in 26 quotes)
- **LO**: little one, her own baby. Warning: forum shorthand; in a headline it reads as an outsider dressing up as a member. Safe only when quoting real parents. (found in 26 quotes)
- **little one**: the spoken form of LO. Warm, soft and slightly old-fashioned. Not insider-only, so it can be used, but it is close to sugary: body text, not headlines. (found in 13 quotes)
- **SAHM**: stay-at-home mother. A live argument about whether it counts as real work. (found in 15 quotes)
- **FTM**: first-time mother. Warning: say "first-time mum" or "first-time mom" in our own voice; FTM also reads as "female to male". (found in 11 quotes)
- **mom guilt / mum guilt**: the guilt attached to every choice. Self-aware and half ironic; naming it is how mothers defuse it, and others reliably answer that she has nothing to feel guilty about. Warning: safe only when absolving. Stirring it up is the fastest way to be hated here. (found in 14 quotes)
- **bad mom / bad mum**: the charge she brings against herself, sometimes as a defiant joke and sometimes truly dark. One may gently reject the label for her; never imply she might be one. (found in 13 quotes)
- **HV / health visitor**: the British community nurse who does weigh-ins and development checks. There is no American equivalent; there it is the pediatrician. (found in 10 quotes)
- **baby wearing**: carrying the baby in a sling or carrier to get through the day. Practical and slightly evangelical; the stock answer to "how do I get anything done". (found in 8 quotes)
- **nursery**: a false friend across the Atlantic. In Britain it is paid childcare, with waiting lists, cost and guilt; in America it is the baby's decorated bedroom. Warning: never write one line meant to work in both markets. Americans say daycare (60 quotes here). (found in 8 quotes)
- **mat leave**: British for maternity leave. Casual, and often used to correct people who treat it as a holiday. (found in 6 quotes)
- **mom friends**: friends made through motherhood, often through apps or baby groups. Earnest and a little exposed; it names loneliness without the word. Never imply she should already have them. (found in 5 quotes)
- **soft play**: British for an indoor play centre. There is no direct American word. (found in 5 quotes)
- **self soothe**: a baby settling without feeding, rocking or holding. Loaded: an aspiration for parents and a weapon for relatives. Implying a baby should do it draws anger. (found in 5 quotes)
- **MIL**: mother-in-law. Warning: the in-law abbreviations live almost wholly in threads about grievances and boundaries. Using them takes a side in a family quarrel. (found in 5 quotes)
- **PPD / PPA**: postpartum depression and anxiety. Warning: serious diagnoses. Acknowledge that they exist; never imply that a product touches them. (found in 4 quotes)
- **weaning**: Warning: in Britain it means starting solid food; to many Americans it means stopping breastfeeding. A dangerous ambiguity. (found in 4 quotes)
- **contact nap**: a nap the baby will take only while held. Double-edged and warm: pinned to the sofa and the cuddles she will miss, in the same breath. Framing it as a problem to fix reads as judgement. (found in 3 quotes)
- **the trenches**: the hard first months, spoken of as a war zone one is in and later comes out of. Weary but bonding. Use it only standing beside her. (found in 2 quotes)
- **mental load**: the unseen work of remembering, planning and foreseeing everything. Said with quiet, controlled anger, and it nearly always points at a partner. Use it seriously, never as a wink. (found in 2 quotes)
- **NICU**: the neonatal intensive care unit. It stays part of a family's identity. Warning: never a hook. (found in 2 quotes)
- **gentle parenting**: discipline by acknowledging feelings instead of punishing. Deeply contested: a badge for some, a joke or a source of guilt for others. Avoid claiming the label. (found in 1 quote)
- **big feelings**: the gentle-parenting phrase for a toddler's outsized emotions. Sincere from a parent, and the most parodied phrase in this register. Cringe-making from a seller. (found in 1 quote)
- **velcro baby**: a baby who cannot be put down at all. Fondly exasperated, worn as a badge. Warm and safe. (found in 1 quote)
- **high needs**: a settled label for a baby who needs far more holding and soothing than most. It lets a mother describe a hard baby without saying "difficult". (found in 1 quote)
- **BLW**: baby-led weaning: finger foods in place of purées. A mild tribal signal. (found in 1 quote)

The glossary's warnings that reach beyond single terms: do not use mom guilt as a lever; do not write "enjoy every moment", the platitude mothers quote in order to mock it; write ages in weeks and months, as she does. Three of its warmest terms, "survival mode", "touched out" and "positive stories", do not appear in this niche's quotes at all.
