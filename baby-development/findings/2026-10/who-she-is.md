# baby-development: who is she, and what is her life like?

**Written:** 2026-11-18 from `~/GitHub/_research/baby-development/evidence/who-she-is.md`.
**Reviewed by the owner:** not yet

## Limits

The quotes are 2,970 records: 2,806 from Reddit posts written between April and October
2026, 159 from app reviews and 5 from book reviews (the reviews run from 2014 to 2026).
They were collected to learn what hurts, what parents want and what they say about
products, not to describe a typical buyer. A count is a count of quotes, never of people,
and never shows how common something is; one event can sit behind several quotes. The
counts are wide topic indexes that overlap, so they cannot be added together, and a count
can include a few quotes from people other than her.

What the quotes cannot tell us:

- Her typical age: 5 quotes give an age or an age band (two are the same 27-year-old
  mother); 2,965 give none.
- Her usual work or leave arrangement: 240 quotes carry some work cue, counted in three
  different ways by the three readers; 2,730 carry none. No employment rate, typical job,
  working hours or usual leave follows from them.
- Her income: 0 quotes state what she or her household earns. Prices, budgets, benefits
  and a single income give no bracket.
- Her education: 0 quotes name a finished qualification. 5 mention studying or exams, and
  one of those is a father.
- Her country: at least 12 quotes name a place, four of them from one account in Wales.
  Health-service words, currencies and subreddits do not settle where she lives.
- Her family size and relationship status as a profile: the 284 first-baby quotes and the
  292 sibling-or-pregnancy quotes overlap and are not a census; 0 household surveys were
  collected. A partner in the story says nothing about marriage, living together or help.
- Whether she is the buyer, and what she normally spends: the quotes come from people who
  post or review, and nothing shows which of them buy. The 164 review records are not 164
  verified buyers. 0 maps of what
  she spends on development were made before the reviews were chosen.
- A typical day, a media habit, or how common any difficulty is: 0 time-use diaries and 0
  sampled respondents. How common distress, disability, regret or harm is cannot be
  estimated, and no quote is a diagnosis.

Other standing limits:

- Unread material: about 4,300 candidate Reddit posts have not been read.
- Who is counted as her: mothers, and parents who do not state their gender. Parents of
  unstated gender are not all women. 93 quotes say nothing more about the adult who wrote
  them.
- Reviews: ids that start with `dvr1` are app and book reviews. A reviewer is not always
  a mother or a paying customer.
- Provenance kept, not judged: six quotes (dv02-0013 to dv02-0018) were worded with
  ChatGPT's help, by the mother's own account. The playroom picture behind dv03-0016 to
  dv03-0018 was an AI mockup, not a finished room. Whether the review clusters for Bloomie,
  Pathways and Lovevery are independent is unknown; nothing shows they were coordinated.
- Fiction: one record is a story its author marks as fiction (dv25-0179). It is kept in
  the quotes and describes no real life.
- The product brief's proposed mother, price ladder and age-selected guide are
  assumptions, not findings.

## Her situation

### When it is her first baby, she has little to judge normal by

284 quotes.

- "I’m a first time Mum and it’s difficult to not think that I’m doing things wrong sometimes, so this is really helpful to hear ❤️" [dv01-0137]
- "I’m an only child that didn’t grow up around younger relatives, so the early life stages are brand new to me. On top of that, I have few people in my life with babies right now" [dv05-0023]

**But:** experienced mothers post too, and one was still treated as a beginner. "I was repeatedly reassured that I was just an anxious first-time mom—even though this is my second child and I’m an occupational therapist." [dv21-0080]

### When she has other children or is pregnant again, her attention is split and she is still unsure

292 quotes.

- "he’s been having such a hard time with the transition to big brother that I’ve been focusing on him so much I guess I haven’t had the 1x1 time to focus and practice milestones with her like I did with my first." [dv05-0151]
- "Now with my second? Energy is gone […] But I just have no mental capacity to plan and execute fun things anymore." [dv03-0267]

**But:** a home with two small children can also be calm. "I am a SAHM to a 3yr girl and 18mo boy. My son is a very agreeable, easygoing kid" [dv25-0124]

### She may be on leave, back at work, studying or at home full time, and each one changes who cares for the baby

342 quotes.

**But:** some can hand over to a partner who is also at home. This is a first-time mother writing about her wife. "my wife is on mat leave for a year, I work part time and from home, we’re both very hands on and can switch in and out as the other one needs a break." [dv06-0156]

## Her day

### Her day is a round of feeding, changing and short bursts of play, with few minutes of her own

1,226 quotes.

- "Some days I have the energy for entertainment (passing him things on the playmat, rolling him, sitting him up on me, carrying him around, talking, singing, reading etc.) But nothing is holding his focus for very long so it just feels like a never ending conveyor belt" [dv02-0306]
- "Success: got a 1and half hour nap, got to eat a sandwich, got the floor swept" [dv04-0013]

**But:** some children keep themselves busy and nap well, which makes a different day. "otherwise does his own thing, is very sweet to me, naps great" [dv25-0124]

### Naps and her own tiredness decide how much of the day she can use

530 quotes.

- "I feel like my day always consists of pumping, baby care, playing with baby, and I’m so tired that when she naps I will just nap, and I’ll do stuff around the house. I haven’t gotten any me time lately." [dv04-0165]
- "my baby was doing really well at sleeping through the night and independent play and was a fairly happy baby. all of a sudden he’s screaming and crying non stop." [dv22-0044]

**But:** nights that got better are reported too. "We're back down to 1 feed a night and sometimes 2 but we're getting a good 6-8 hours now!" [dv22-0137]

### Outings give her day a shape, but being among people does not always make her less alone

410 quotes.

- "She goes to gymnastics with dad every week, I take her swimming, usually once a week soft play and she’s outside at least twice a day no matter what." [dv01-0172]
- "I feel isolated despite piling my weeks high with social plans, outings and appointments. […] I feel like I have to constantly entertain her or haul her out of the house. I feel exhausted and completely depleted." [dv24-0096]

**But:** one mother is settled at home and wants no more mother groups. "mommy groups frankly sound like awful dick-measuring contests over developmental milestones." [dv02-0352]

### A worry sends her searching, posting and tracking

422 quotes. The trigger can be an appointment, a form, a hard day, a relative or a video.

- "I didn't really think about it until they had me fill out his questionaire, but now I'm worried that his eye contact has decreased, he's not cooing much anymore, and he seems behind on motor skills." [dv04-0021]
- "TikTok (of course) popped up with the scariest video of infantile spasms. Had never heard of it before. It’s wrecked me. Every move my son makes I analyze and worry about." [dv22-0015]

**But:** not every search for a tracker comes from fear. "Tbh I’m not a very anxious mom and was looking for apps that would more so be fun to use rather than tracking or comparing" [dv05-0045]

## Who else has a say

168 quotes.

**But:** a mother who disagrees with her partner about sleep training still praises what he does. "He is always playing with baby and doing exercises making sure he’s getting plenty of tummy time and all of that. I mean he is truly great." [dv23-0090]

### Having a partner or family nearby does not mean she has help she can use

174 quotes.

### Doctors and therapists can reassure her, contradict her, refer her on or blame her

465 quotes. The professional's words are as the parent reports them.

141 quotes.

### People and content online both help her and judge her

658 quotes. Google, Reddit, Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube, ads and reviews all appear; appearing is not the same as being trusted.

**But:** she also pushes back against what she is shown. "Your child will not fall behind if you don’t buy them a fancy toy. That is manipulative marketing and you shouldn’t fall for it." [dv03-0071]

## What she already follows and believes

### She reads her baby through named methods and checklists, such as Montessori, wake windows, leaps and CDC milestones

829 quotes. These are different frameworks, not one shared philosophy.

- "I’ve been listening to the podcast Montessori way and have been interested by following this lifestyle but on a budget. It’s my first child and so far I try my best to implicate some of the self-regulation, independent play, screen free, independent feeding" [dv24-0080]
- "If she doesn’t nap when the app says she should, I worry. If she wants another feed sooner than expected, I worry. It feels like there’s a “right” way to do everything, and I’m scared I’m getting it wrong." [dv04-0019]
- "We are pulling out all the stops - medication, chiropractic care, had tongue and lip tues removed, hypoallergenic formula nutrimegen, biogaia probiotics, holding upright 30 mins after feeding, gas drops, gripe water, tummy time, baths, bicycle kicks." [dv22-0074]

**But:** some reject the idea that a parent controls development, "The truth is that we don't control our children's development, we support it." [dv24-0061], and one weighs a method (Doman) without taking its side: "Others say it is expensive, lacks scientific evidence, and is essentially a scam." [dv22-0112]

### She holds rules about screens and constant stimulation, and bends them to get through the actual day

1,016 quotes.

- "HOW DO YOU DO IT ALL? I am finding it hard to figure out what to sacrifice and just want to be an amazing mom (I am a perfectionist st heart)." [dv22-0177]
- "We are full-time working parents and maintain screen-free environment at home." [dv05-0158]

**But:** others make room for a child who is ordinary and left to explore. "Let him discover things at his own pace." [dv24-0073] "Would I rather she play with her 400 toys than sort spices? Yes. Is it hurting anyone? No. So I just let her." [dv24-0005]

## Experiences that need their own labels

720 quotes. Depression, PPD and PPA, OCD, medication, trouble bonding, anger and suicidal thoughts are each reported at the speaker's own level of certainty.

[dv22-0008]

## Public figures

These are the figures as filed in `~/GitHub/_research/baby-development/statistics/us-first-time-mothers.md`;
each Source line names the saved page the figure was copied from. Each number here appears in the saved page its Source line names (checked 2026-11-18); the sources themselves were not fetched again. All are
American. Only the first two are about first births; none measures first-time mothers of
babies 0 to 24 months, and none describes who buys. The quotes cannot test any of
them. The statistics page's own readings ("mid-career", leave "mostly
unpaid") are not carried over, because the evidence file does not accept them.

### The average American woman is 27.6 years old when her first child is born

Source: NCHS, births data for 2024; `statistics/raw/nchs-births-final-data-2024.txt`.
Covers all US first births in 2024. It was 27.5 in 2023 and 25.4 in 2010.

### 1,427,020 of the 3,628,934 babies born in the US in 2024 were first children

Source: NCHS, births data for 2024; `statistics/raw/nchs-births-final-data-2024.txt`.
Covers all US births by birth order. The report prints no percentage.

### 39.5% of US births in 2024 were to unmarried mothers

Source: NCHS, births data for 2024; `statistics/raw/nchs-births-final-data-2024.txt`.
Covers all births, not first births only.

### 88.6% of US women giving birth in 2024 had finished high school, and 36.7% had a bachelor's degree or more

Source: NCHS, births data for 2024; `statistics/raw/nchs-births-final-data-2024.txt`.
Covers all births, not first births only.

### The median household income of American women with a recent birth was $85,320 in 2021

Source: US Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2021; `statistics/raw/census-recent-births-acs-2021.txt`.
Covers women aged 15 to 50 who reported a birth in the past 12 months, any birth order.

### 65.4% of American mothers with a child under 3 were in the labour force in 2024

Source: BLS, Employment Characteristics of Families 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-employment-characteristics-of-families-2024.txt`.
Covers mothers living with their own children under 3.

### 61.9% of American mothers whose youngest child is under 1 were in the labour force in 2024

Source: BLS, Employment Characteristics of Families 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-employment-characteristics-of-families-2024.txt`.
Covers mothers whose youngest child is under 1, first child or not.

### 68.3% of American mothers with children under 6 were in the labour force in 2024, against 78.0% of those whose youngest is 6 to 17

Source: BLS, Employment Characteristics of Families 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-employment-characteristics-of-families-2024.txt`.
Covers all mothers with children under 6, well past 24 months.

### 76% of employed American mothers with children under 6 worked full time in 2024

Source: BLS, Employment Characteristics of Families 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-employment-characteristics-of-families-2024.txt`.
Covers employed mothers with children under 6. It was 81% for those with children 6 to 17, and 79% of all employed mothers against 95% of employed fathers.

### American mothers took a median of 11 weeks of leave after a birth or adoption, and less when household income was lower

Source: Pew Research Center, 2017 (survey of about 2016); `statistics/raw/pew-family-medical-leave-2017.txt`.
Covers mothers who took leave in the previous two years, any child. The median was 12 weeks at household incomes of $75,000 or more, 10 weeks at $30,000 to $74,999 and 6 weeks under $30,000; 47% took 12 weeks or more.

### In a 2026 survey, mothers who went back to work had taken a median of 10 weeks of leave

Source: New America, National Parent Survey 2026; `statistics/raw/new-america-parental-leave-2026.txt`.
Covers nearly 5,500 parents of children under six. 6% of mothers took two weeks or less, 88% returned to work after leave, and 15% of parents employed at the birth took no time off.

### 27% of private-industry employees in the US had paid family leave from their employer in March 2023

Source: Congressional Research Service, 2025, citing BLS; `statistics/raw/crs-paid-family-medical-leave-2025.txt`.
Covers private-sector workers of any family type, not mothers.

### 53% of American children under 3 were in regular care by someone other than a parent in 2024

Source: ASPE, 2026, citing the 2024 National Survey of Early Care and Education; `statistics/raw/aspe-child-care-parent-preferences-2026.txt`.
Covers all US children under 3. Unpaid care by an individual was the most common kind (24%), then centres (18%).

### Nearly half of American children under 5 spent some time in a grandparent's care

Source: Roosevelt Institute, 2026, using Census Bureau survey data of an unstated year; `statistics/raw/roosevelt-whos-minding-the-kids-sipp.txt`.
Covers children under 5, for any amount of time, which is wider than regular childcare.

### Adults living with a child under 6 spent 2.5 hours a day on childcare as their main activity in 2024: women 3.0, men 2.0

Source: BLS, American Time Use Survey 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-american-time-use-survey-2024.txt`.
Covers adults of either sex with a child under 6 in the household.

### Employed adults with a child under 6 had 3.0 hours of leisure and sport a day in 2024, against 4.5 for those with no child under 18

Source: BLS, American Time Use Survey 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-american-time-use-survey-2024.txt`.
Covers employed adults of either sex.

### Women whose youngest child is under 6 were recorded as sleeping 9.08 hours a day in 2024

Source: BLS, American Time Use Survey 2024; `statistics/raw/bls-american-time-use-survey-2024.txt`.
Covers women with a youngest child under 6. The figure counts naps and spells of sleeplessness; it does not measure broken nights.

### Four in five American parents of a child aged 0 to 4 use social media for parenting advice or to share experiences

Source: C.S. Mott Children's Hospital National Poll, 2023; `statistics/raw/mott-poll-social-media-parenting-advice-2023.txt`.
Covers 614 parents with a child aged 0 to 4; most mothers do. Two in five find it hard to tell good advice from bad there, and nearly half find it very useful for new ideas.

### 91% of American women and 97% of American adults aged 30 to 49 own a smartphone

Source: Pew Research Center, 2026; `statistics/raw/pew-mobile-fact-sheet-2026.txt`.
Covers all US adults, not mothers.

### 82% of American children under 2 ever watch TV, and 62% watch YouTube

Source: Pew Research Center, 2025; `statistics/raw/pew-how-parents-manage-screen-time-2025.txt`.
Covers what parents report about a child under 2. About four in ten say the child ever uses a smartphone; the YouTube figure was 45% in 2020.

### Nearly a quarter of American parents of a child five or under have suspected a delay in milestones

Source: C.S. Mott Children's Hospital National Poll, 2021; `statistics/raw/mott-poll-milestone-worry-2021.txt`.
Covers 779 parents with a child five or under. Of those who suspected a delay, 63% asked a healthcare provider, 24% a childcare provider, and 18% only looked online or asked family, friends or social media.

### An average of 55% of American households with a child under 3 had some concern about the child's learning, development or behaviour in 2025

Source: ZERO TO THREE, 2026, from the Stanford RAPID survey of 2025; `statistics/raw/zerotothree-rapid-baby-brief-2025.txt`.
Covers an online panel, not a random sample: 5,816 responses. About a quarter were very concerned; 64% were very or somewhat concerned in November 2025.

### 36.5% of American children aged 9 to 35 months had a standardized developmental screening in the past year

Source: America's Health Rankings, National Survey of Children's Health 2023-2024; `statistics/raw/ahr-developmental-screening-nsch-2023-2024.txt`.
Covers children aged 9 to 35 months whose parent completed a screening tool.

### 79.6% of American children aged 0 to 17 had at least one preventive doctor's visit in the past year

Source: America's Health Rankings, National Survey of Children's Health 2023-2024; `statistics/raw/ahr-well-child-visit-nsch-2023-2024.txt`.
Covers all children 0 to 17. The source says it is higher for ages 0 to 2 but gives no figure.

### American married couples on a middle income spent about $12,680 a year on a child aged 0 to 2, in 2015 dollars

Source: USDA, Expenditures on Children by Families 2015; `statistics/raw/usda-expenditures-on-children-2015.txt`.
Covers married-couple families with a before-tax income of $59,200 to $107,400; $2,870 of it was childcare and education. It was $9,690 below that income band and $19,770 above it.
