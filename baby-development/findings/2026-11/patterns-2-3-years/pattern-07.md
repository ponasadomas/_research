# baby-development: pattern 7, Learning to eat and drink as a skill (children of 2 and 3 years)

**Written:** 2026-11-20 from baby-development/evidence/2026-11/age-reread/digest-09.md
**Reviewed by the owner:** not yet

## Limits

The starting material was a random sample of 20,000 Reddit posts from twelve parenting subreddits, dated 1 April to 24 October 2026 and chosen without keywords; 3,811 were on-niche, including 1,340 assigned to the children-of-2-and-3-years group. This pattern's supplied digest contains 18 on-niche and 4 near posts, but describes five of those on-niche posts and three near posts as concerning young infants, so the age label is inconsistent with its contents and the supplied classifications are retained here. Each count is a number of posts tagged by one reader, rather than people or market prevalence; forms from different digest readers would be merged by judgement, but this pattern has only one digest and its forms remain separate.

## On-niche posts

### The parent wants to end comfort bottles even though the child can already drink from cups.

3 of 18 posts.

Parents describe children who already use cups but want bottles for milk, sleep or soothing. Changing the drink or container has not removed the attachment; parents consider stopping abruptly or changing bedtime with a bottle-fairy ritual.

- "I’ve started watering down his milk in the morning and sometimes I offer it to him in a straw cup but he gets so pissed. Should I just cold turkey cut him off bottles? He drinks from open cup and straw cup just fine through the day." [ar061-0033]
- "Tonight, we even did an experiment where he poured the milk directly from his bottle into his favorite sippy cup. He took one sip and made a completely disgusted face. He genuinely hated it, even though the milk tasted fine. It seems like a pure sensory/comfort association with the bottle itself." [ar006-0129]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent wants to replace bottles with cups but the child refuses the alternatives.

3 of 18 posts.

Parents want to move away from bottles used for milk, water or sleep, but cups provoke rejection or distress. One compares advice to wean with reassurance that older bottle use is ordinary.

- "Yes I've rode out the tantrum he goes until he throws up it's terrible. I've given him just water in his bottles (tip from my mom) and he didn't care, he just wants his bottle." [ar051-0062]
- "My almost three year old daughter refuses to give up her Dr. Browns bottle. She only drinks water out of it, but refuses to use a sippy cup. She also likes to go to bed with it at night and drinks water out of it while sleeping." [ar027-0144]

**But:** Cup refusal is not absolute in every post: "He will accept drinking water from either an open cup or from my water bottle, but only a sip or two and then he'll ask for his baba and throw a fit until he gets it." [ar051-0062]

### The parent wants to teach straw drinking after attempts have failed.

2 of 18 posts.

Parents of children aged 2 and 2.6 years want practical ways to teach straw drinking. Bottle or open-cup use does not mean the straw skill has followed.

- "I already tried the yogurt method, the one where you put water in the straw and putting your finger over the top of the straw to trap a small amount of liquid inside. But I still cant teach my son to use straw." [ar029-0054]
- "So far our attempts to teach him have left him frustrated and we have better success with open cups than straw cups but we dont want to skip straws entirely." [ar069-0003]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent wants independent eating after hospital stays made feeding the child a habit.

2 of 18 posts.

Parents describe feeding capable two-year-olds after hospital stays made intake a priority. They want independence but fear poor intake or difficulty regulating emotions.

- "Then he was hospitalised twice over a span of 5 months and regressed to being spoon-fed. We were super stressed about his overall weight and development, so we did whatever we could to feed him whilst working full-time jobs. What was survival mode turned into a regular thing." [ar079-0033]
- "He’s totally capable of feeding himself, but I don’t know how to break the cycle. I know I could just put the food down and let him eat if he’s hungry. But then he’ll turn into a moody mess. I think he has a harder time regulating his emotions due to his history with his brain surgeries. I’m just at a loss" [ar031-0045]

**But:** The children do show independent eating in some circumstances: "He will feed himself if it’s a preferred food like pizza, pickles, anything like that." [ar031-0043] The other parent also says: "Sometimes he'll feed himself, but he was eating quite well until the molars started coming." [ar079-0031]

### The parent needs a breastfed baby to accept bottles and asks what else to try.

2 of 18 posts.

Parents of nine- and ten-week-olds want bottle feeding to become possible, either to stop breastfeeding or to share feeds and have time away. One baby previously took bottles; another refuses despite changes in bottles, flow, temperature, position and caregiver.

- "Breastfeeding is slowly destroying me and I can't keep doing it but I feel like I have no choice with the bottle refusal." [ar030-0111]
- "This week, baby suddenly started refusing the bottle. We've never had this issue before, he'd always taken it without fuss." [ar021-0154]
- "We've tried various brands (Philips Avent, MAM, lanisoh and nuk) as well as different teat flows. Experimented with different temperatures. I've attempted to feed, as has my husband and my mother. We've tried sitting up position, lie-down feeding, I've tried nursing and then doing the ol'switcheroo with the bottle and she will just not take it." [ar030-0109]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent wonders whether feeding a formerly independent child provides connection.

1 of 18 posts.

The parent has begun feeding a previously independent two-year-old and wonders whether the request is for connection. This is the parent’s interpretation, not an established motive.

- "I’ve refused to feed her most times because she used to be super independent but recently I’ve been feeling mom guilt and maybe she’s just wanting connection so I been feeding her." [ar062-0105]
- "Anyone else?" [ar062-0105]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent wants direct breastfeeding to work because pumping is wearing them down.

1 of 18 posts.

The parent of an 11-week-old wants to establish breastfeeding after roughly two months of exclusive pumping. Early latch attempts worsened hunger and frustration, while the feeding-and-pumping routine now takes a toll on mental health.

- "Early on I tried to get him to latch before feeding him expressed milk but it wasn't really working out - he'd go from 0 to 60 so quick with his hunger cues, and his frustration from not latching made it so much worse." [ar049-0051]
- "The thing is, it's really starting to take a toll on my mental health - if I'm not feeding him from bottle, I'm pumping." [ar049-0052]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent shares a later move from bottles to breastfeeding to challenge early pressure.

1 of 18 posts.

Writing at 12 weeks postpartum, the parent describes moving from mostly pumped-milk bottles to mostly breastfeeding by six weeks. They want parents to receive help with later transitions rather than early pressure.

- "I absolutely hated the pressure to breastfeed at the beginning, and all of the judgement when you tell people you’re not doing it." [ar059-0008]
- "So I got some nipple shields to help him latch, had him in a BF position with the bottle by my boob when he was being fed with pumped milk, and then by 6 weeks he was mostly breastfeeding (his dad still gives him a bottle every day because it’s nice for them to have that time together)." [ar059-0007]

**But:** The parent explicitly limits the example’s reach: "I understand that doing things this way around may not work for everyone" [ar059-0008]

### The parent wants a bottle flow that lets the baby feed without falling asleep or choking.

1 of 18 posts.

After eight weeks of breastfeeding and a return to work, this parent finds extra-slow flow too slow but slow flow upsetting. They want an intermediate option that allows the baby to take enough milk.

- "She did okay last week with the Lansinoh XS flow nipple but now she is falling asleep while feeding after only 0.5 oz." [ar074-0112]
- "I upped to the S flow nipple and it seems to be choking her up and she gets upset." [ar074-0112]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent fears choking when a capable eater stuffs too much food into the mouth.

1 of 18 posts.

The parent of a two-year-old wants to teach slower, safer eating when excitement or play leads to mouth-stuffing. Removing food has not resolved the fear.

- "Thankfully, he has not choked yet, but I’m so terrified he will." [ar032-0027]
- "Like today, he had avocado toast pieces and he decided he wanted to make a sandwich out of then and stacked them together and then shoved all the pieces (so basically a whole piece of toast ) in his mouth." [ar032-0029]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent questions choking warnings after seeing other parents offer whole meat sticks.

1 of 18 posts.

A parent who quarters foods such as hot dogs wonders why other parents offer whole meat sticks to children aged two or younger. They ask whether warnings to wait until four are excessively cautious; the post does not establish safety.

- "Everywhere I look, it explicitly says that it's a HUGE choking risk and you should never give a whole Chomps to a 2yo, and shouldn't until they're 4yo." [ar068-0091]
- "My question is.. are all these sources erring on the major side of caution, or is it actually safe to give them whole meat sticks?" [ar068-0092]

**But:** nothing found against it.

## Near posts

### The parent wants more food accepted but worries about withholding alternatives.

1 of 4 posts.

The parent of a two-year-old offers alternatives when family food is refused, while the husband favors letting hunger teach acceptance. Low weight, sleep disruption and wasted food make that approach worrying.

- "I would love to just give him what we are having, but when I have tried, he just throws it and then won't eat it, and then I cave and make him something else." [ar021-0143]
- "Our little guy is healthy and happy, but he is tiny (5th percentile for weight), so I get worried about putting him to bed with no food - but maybe the learning curve would be quick, and I should get over it?" [ar021-0145]

**But:** Some food is reliably accepted: "He loves breakfast and eats it every day" [ar021-0139] The digest also reports that a feeding assessment found no mouth-movement problem [ar021-0138].

### The parent wants possible allergy investigated after feeding improved and then became uncomfortable.

1 of 4 posts.

After improved feeding following a tongue-tie release, the parent of a six-week-old reports distressed feeds and other symptoms. They want possible cow’s milk protein allergy investigated despite the GP’s reassurance about settling and weight gain.

- "His feeding has also changed, he can seem very hungry but then within 30 seconds–1 minute of latching he becomes frantic, arches/straightens his body, cries and comes off, so feeds are often much shorter and more frequent and I’m struggling to get him satisfied as he’s just too uncomfortable." [ar063-0097]
- "Does this sound like symptoms worth pushing to investigate, even though he is generally able to be settled eventually and gaining weight? And is a dairy elimination trial something I should be asking about?" [ar063-0099]

**But:** The account also reports substantial improvement after release and good weight gain: "After that, breastfeeding improved massively. He started latching well, feeding efficiently, coming off satisfied and gaining weight really well." [ar063-0095]

### The parent wants direct breastfeeding to work because pumping is wearing them down.

1 of 4 posts.

At ten weeks postpartum, this parent wants to nurse but describes a painful, shallow latch and exhaustion with pumping and washing. They anticipate hating themselves for stopping and feel unable to make time for repeated consultant visits.

- "I hate pumping. I want to nurse my baby so bad but her latch is so bad and so shallow and it hurts so much." [ar081-0112]
- "I’m so fucjing tired and so upset. I’m so sick of washing the fcking dishes all the time and all the other bullshit that comes along with being an exclusive pumper." [ar081-0113]
- "I hate this so much but I think I would hate myself for quitting even more." [ar081-0115]

**But:** Direct nursing is possible occasionally, despite the painful latch: "I nurse her every once in a while in a pinch or if I really don’t want to pump." [ar081-0113]

### The parent asks whether painful newborn breastfeeding means the latch needs help or feeding should change.

1 of 4 posts.

The mother of a two-day-old describes raw, painful nipples and little sleep during cluster feeding. She has ordered more supplies and asks whether to endure the pain, seek latch help, supplement or stop.

- "Left nipple has some cracking, right looks fine. Both are pretty okay looking but feel so very raw afterwards and deeply painful when he feeds." [ar081-0123]
- "I’ve ordered all the things for same day delivery (shields, heat/cooling pads, etc.) but I’m worried it’s my latch or something. Has anyone had anything similar? Is this just normal? Do I need to just get through it? Is it a sign I should stop or add formula to the mix? Any reassurance or advice would be amazing." [ar081-0124]

**But:** The digest also reports a successful latch at the first home visit [ar081-0122]; it supplies only a sentence fragment for that counterexample.

## Across the on-niche posts

### Parents have already changed feeding techniques, equipment or routines.

18 of 18 posts.

Parents describe attempts to change feeding equipment, technique or routines, including repeated unsuccessful experiments. The tag records an attempt, not proof that it worked.

- "I already tried the yogurt method, the one where you put water in the straw and putting your finger over the top of the straw to trap a small amount of liquid inside. But I still cant teach my son to use straw." [ar029-0054]
- "We've tried various brands (Philips Avent, MAM, lanisoh and nuk) as well as different teat flows. Experimented with different temperatures. I've attempted to feed, as has my husband and my mother. We've tried sitting up position, lie-down feeding, I've tried nursing and then doing the ol'switcheroo with the bottle and she will just not take it." [ar030-0109]

**But:** Some attempts accompanied success: "So I got some nipple shields to help him latch, had him in a BF position with the bottle by my boob when he was being fed with pumped milk, and then by 6 weeks he was mostly breastfeeding (his dad still gives him a bottle every day because it’s nice for them to have that time together)." [ar059-0007]

### Parents receive feeding advice and pressure, and ask family to help with feeds.

5 of 18 posts.

Parents report advice, pressure and feeding help from professionals, family and others. This tag includes named co-caregivers’ actions; an unspecified “we” alone does not establish another person’s separate action.

- "I've gone to the pediatrician for this and at least in my country (Mexico) while they do tell me he should've been weaned off of it by now the societal norm is for them to keep taking at least night bottles until they decide to stop." [ar051-0060]
- "I absolutely hated the pressure to breastfeed at the beginning, and all of the judgement when you tell people you’re not doing it." [ar059-0008]

**But:** Others also provide practical help: "I've attempted to feed, as has my husband and my mother." [ar030-0109]

### One parent has bought more than fifty kinds of cup without displacing the bottle.

1 of 18 posts.

One parent explicitly reports buying more than fifty cup types without replacing the bottle; the amount paid is unknown. This is the only purchase quote supplied, so one is reproduced; equipment use or “got some nipple shields” does not establish payment, and the husband’s adult dental spending is not a purchase for the child’s feeding problem.

- "I've bought easily over 50 different types of cups, even smaller versions of my water bottle but he just. wants. the. freaking. bottle." [ar051-0062]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### Parents describe fear and strain around feeding, alongside curiosity and hope.

12 of 18 posts.

Parents explicitly name panic, fear, frustration, guilt, pressure and harm to mental health. The tag also includes curiosity and hope; a request for advice alone does not count as a feeling.

- "Thankfully, he has not choked yet, but I’m so terrified he will." [ar032-0027]
- "Every other no I can take. A no for meals makes me panic." [ar079-0037]
- "Breastfeeding is slowly destroying me and I can't keep doing it but I feel like I have no choice with the bottle refusal." [ar030-0111]

**But:** Feelings are not uniformly negative: "I’m hopeful it will be easier to just change it all up rather than try to figure out how to do our normal bedtime with no water bottle and then later try to change it on her again." [ar046-0126]
