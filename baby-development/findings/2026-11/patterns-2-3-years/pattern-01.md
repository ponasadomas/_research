# baby-development: pattern 1, Is this normal, or is something wrong with my child? (children of 2 and 3 years)

**Written:** 2026-11-20 from baby-development/evidence/2026-11/age-reread/digest-02.md
**Reviewed by the owner:** not yet

## Limits

These posts come from a random, keyword-free sample of 20,000 posts in twelve parenting subreddits, 1 April–24 October 2026: 3,811 were on-niche, including 1,340 about children of two and three years. This digest contains 171 on-niche and 23 near posts; counts are posts tagged by one reader, not people or market prevalence. Forms from different digest readers are merged by judgement; this single digest’s forms remain intact.

## On-niche posts

### The parent asks whether intense tantrums exceed the usual toddler stage.

15 of 171 posts.

Screaming, reversals and head-banging prompt evaluation questions; one parent reports repeated pinching and asks how to stop.

- "If I give them the "wrong" cup, cut their food differently than they expected, tell them it's time to leave the playground, or even help them with something they wanted to do themselves, it can turn into 20–30 minutes of crying, screaming, and refusing to calm down." [ar070-0068]
- "Não é a primeira vez que belisco ela." [ar018-0163]

**But:** Head-banging is not always frequent. "She’s done it twice now, once when she was 2, and now today." [ar029-0068]

### The parent wonders whether a repeated habit or intense interest will pass.

14 of 171 posts.

Habits include mouthing, rocking, demanded responses and fixations; insurance blocks one family’s pediatrician access.

- "I have twin boy toddlers who are obsessed with System of a Down." [ar038-0077]
- "I have tried giving him a fidget toy to hold instead, redirecting, saying “hands down” or “hands out of mouth” and it works for a minute and then he’s back to putting his hands in his mouth." [ar041-0107]

**But:** Concern can be confined to one habit. "It’s really just this, besides things that I can guarantee are typical." [ar056-0153]

### The parent asks whether a bodily change is developmental or needs care.

14 of 171 posts.

Vomiting, tiredness, teeth, eyes, staring, falls and a reported seizure prompt checks; supplied ages include sixteen and twenty-one months.

- "My 2.5 year old daughter eats until she throws up and I genuinely don’t know what to do anymore." [ar034-0025]
- "Does sucking his thumb only to help him fall asleep and at night will still negatively impact his teeth and palate development?" [ar064-0039]

**But:** Distress need not be general. "I would describe him as a happy child overall, and our home situation is stable and positive." [ar072-0081]

### The parent worries about what the child’s emotional responses mean.

13 of 171 posts.

Concerns span people-pleasing, sensitivity, discipline, cry-singing, quiet reunions and apparent indifference to others’ suffering, including killing live minnows.

- "My daughter does this thing where she cry-sings." [ar035-0127]
- "I’m worried that my bf’s son seemed to show no regard for life or feeling in his conscious for killing like that." [ar062-0060]

**But:** The child wanting others sad also seeks company. "He also asks to see other kids he’s familiar with like his cousins." [ar020-0063]

### The parent struggles to reconcile strengths and developmental concerns.

12 of 171 posts.

Parents consider autism, ADHD or assessment; two supplied posts appear cross-posted, and one writer says AI arranged their own thoughts.

- "It seems like there is a huge discrepancy between the things he can and can't do." [ar048-0118]
- "We fully intend to take him to his appointments and want to schedule an eval." [ar025-0055]

**But:** Settings disagree. "It almost feels like they are describing a different child." [ar007-0095]

### The parent worries about joining in and speaking socially.

12 of 171 posts.

Watching, solitary play and silence prompt shyness, adjustment or selective-mutism questions.

- "I know every kid is different, but my son is no where near that level of pretend play." [ar056-0169]
- "When she's expected to interact or we gently prompt her, she says "I'm not ready" or "I'm uncomfy."" [ar028-0183]

**But:** School is still enjoyed. "The part that makes this confusing is that he actually loves school." [ar056-0092]

### The parent asks whether communication meets expectations.

11 of 171 posts.

Concerns include single words, repetitive speech, few questions and recounting the past.

- "But she doesn’t talk in sentences. She still speaks in gibberish baby talk." [ar075-0114]
- "He knows hundreds of words but uses them mostly to label or repeat." [ar008-0068]

**But:** Other language is strong. "She asks for help, can name our entire massive family, expresses emotions etc." [ar036-0058]

### The parent asks how to handle distress over sensations or routine changes.

11 of 171 posts.

Sound, clothing, grooming, mess and unexpected changes restrict activities; some children seek strong movement instead.

- "The mere suggestion of shorts upsets her." [ar061-0038]
- "I bought her the sound proof ear muffs but she refuses to wear them." [ar006-0108]

**But:** Messy food is tolerated. "He’s oddly enough much better with messy food- he still asks for a napkin to clean himself but doesn’t avoid messy food what so ever." [ar005-0048]

### The parent questions the child’s need for closeness.

9 of 171 posts.

Constant contact, separation distress or unexpected independence raise questions about security.

- "If he didn’t see me leave the room and realizes I’m gone, he will starting running around the house screaming and crying." [ar058-0045]
- "but my (almost) 4 year-old tells me almost daily that she doesn't like when I leave her and it CRUSHES me." [ar039-0152]

**But:** Separation can be easy elsewhere. "The strange thing is that before summer break, he was totally fine at preschool." [ar058-0046]

### The parent asks whether activity or inattention needs investigation.

8 of 171 posts.

Parents question temperament, screens, ADHD and supporting learning.

- "My point is that this age is an exhausting line between "Let them do it, they're learning" and "I want him to be entertained in a way that's not stressful for me or dangerous for him"." [ar015-0087]
- "I guess my biggest question is, how do I know if he is simply a very active and advanced 3-year-old, or if there is something else I should be paying attention to?" [ar007-0128]

**But:** Attention can improve with engagement. "He can’t really sit still unless he’s being actively engaged (like reading together or doing something hands-on)." [ar078-0004]

### The parent wants help stopping injury or damage.

7 of 171 posts.

Injury and preschool concerns include one parent’s unacted-on urge to hit.

- "My arms are usually covered in scabs from him gouging me with his nails, and bruises from his bites." [ar033-0069]
- "The teacher has said he can’t “safely participate” in any activity without 1 on 1 supervision." [ar081-0132]

**But:** Hair-pulling can be absent at nursery. "Il va 3 jours par semaine dans une micro-crèche, et il ne tire pas les cheveux et ne présente pas ce comportement." [ar081-0147]

### The parent questions another adult’s expectations of the child.

7 of 171 posts.

Teachers, relatives, sitters or doctors unsettle a previously reassuring view of ability or behaviour.

- "But she’s 2 and according to her doc at her yearly checkup, this is all emotion regulation and she’s not showing signs of autism and it’s far too early for adhd." [ar032-0016]
- "To be told it might need more investigation was a gut punch to say the least and now we’re questioning everything." [ar032-0099]

**But:** Parents also see difficulties. "My wife and I have admittedly been seeing similar things at home and any full days with him are trying" [ar032-0099]

### The parent worries that growth measurements signal a problem.

7 of 171 posts.

Smallness and exceptional size prompt comparison; supplied ages include nearly six and seventeen months.

- "My son is 3 years and 3 months old and he's 3 foot 7." [ar024-0035]
- "Anyone ever had a high percentile (90s) 2 year old that ended up leaning out and becoming a very average kid later?" [ar049-0082]

**But:** The infant’s doctor is unconcerned. "He just got diagnosed that he has CMPA but the doctor is not worried about his growth at all." [ar031-0054]

### The parent asks whether movement and self-care should be easier.

7 of 171 posts.

Falls, inward feet, dressing, socks or demands for help prompt comparison, demonstrations and medical consultation.

- "My preschooler is almost 4 and he struggles with getting dressed (ie looking at the writing and label for shirt and pants) and is still peeing his pants at night frequently." [ar038-0019]
- "Has anyone else had a similar situation and was there anything you did you help your child walk with straight feet?" [ar035-0131]

**But:** An orthopaedist recommends time. "I took her back today because it hasn’t corrected itself but he mentioned that her hips looks good and that the bones are fine they just need a little more time to adjust and that he wouldn’t recommend braces or even special shoes." [ar035-0129]

### The parent wants help understanding new fears.

6 of 171 posts.

Darkness, showers, familiar people, challenges or growing up become frightening; parents try reassurance and gentle exposure.

- "But as soon as I start singing it she whines and says she's scared." [ar002-0097]
- "As soon as I turn off the main light, he starts clinging to me, crying, and pointing at the corners saying "scared."" [ar045-0010]

**But:** One family reports improvement. "He only cries in the beginning of showers and will be fine towards the middle and at the end." [ar067-0025]

### The parent wants body boundaries without shame.

6 of 171 posts.

Touching, exposure, apparent self-stimulation, jokes, requests and drawings prompt normality and privacy questions; some fear inappropriate touching.

- "She says she's doing yoga, but I suspect it's self-stimulatory in some way." [ar020-0082]
- "One night while I was kissing her face/head, she said “kiss my \[vulva\],” then “kiss my butt,” and laughed." [ar073-0130]

**But:** One parent reports no disclosure. "She hasn’t said anyone touched/kissed/hurt her there, hasn’t identified a person or talked about secrets, doesn’t seem distressed when talking about it, and is easily redirected." [ar073-0131]

### The parent asks whether changed or choppy speech will settle.

4 of 171 posts.

Stuttering, mood-specific lisping or separated syllables prompt questions about waiting or therapy.

- "Any word that ends in a T, D, as well as some other letters, has become T-T-T or D-D-D." [ar022-0066]
- "My toddler 2.3y has been speaking in 3-4 word sentences for around 6 months now, but it still sounds very choppy and like he’s new to speaking?" [ar006-0120]

**But:** Assessment reassured; fluency stayed choppy. "We had him evaluated by E.I. and they stopped the evaluation about an hour in and said that they didn’t need to continue because he was well past the “passing grade” and would not get services and that his speech pattern and fluency was still in the realm of normal and would even out the more he talks and as he gets older, but that was 2-3 months ago and despite him adding more and more words to his vocabulary, the speech fluency hasn’t improved." [ar006-0123]

### The parent fears a new baby has made the toddler’s behaviour worse.

4 of 171 posts.

Parents report or anticipate regression and ask how to manage.

- "My 2 1/2 year old (3 in July) has been having SEVERE behavior issues since our 3rd baby was born a few weeks ago… but he also struggled with this behavior a bit before baby came too, and I’m wondering if it’s worth an evaluation." [ar033-0001]
- "This will be a lot of changes and I’m nervous." [ar046-0052]

**But:** Affection for the baby can remain. "She is affectionate towards her little sister, wants to hold her and snuggle all the time." [ar014-0029]

### The parent wants safe independence around strangers.

3 of 171 posts.

Running away, escaping supervision and approaching unfamiliar adults make ordinary outings difficult.

- "I totally understand but it's not always safe and she tends to drop my hand and run." [ar070-0044]
- "We had some guys working at the house and apparently she went to them after the bathroom with her pants down to show them the toilet paper with poop." [ar028-0161]

**But:** Most encounters are uneventful. "Most people just smile and walk on, but some tend to stop and start chatting to her or encourage her to give them a hug (i dont want her hugging strangers)." [ar070-0045]

### The parent asks whether disrupted sleep reflects a developmental change.

12 of 23 posts.

Sleep changes prompt reassurance, schedule changes and possible medical checks.

- "She's either really cranky because she doesn't get a nap or she struggles to fall asleep at night because she naps so close to bed time." [ar075-0186]
- "Every night we put him to bed, and he’ll chill for a bit but then get up to come see us." [ar034-0130]

**But:** Difficulty can depend on the setting. "She’s also totally fine going to sleep for naps and at nursery." [ar038-0143]

### The parent asks whether physical signs reflect ordinary growth.

6 of 23 posts.

Screening results, appetite, leg pain, clothing proportions, teething and constipation prompt questions; some ages are unknown.

- "I don't know if it's a growth spurt or what but lately my 3 year old has a ravenous appetite." [ar073-0119]
- "I assumed it was growing pains, but it's been going on quite a while and it's only ever the left side." [ar047-0074]

**But:** Screening raised new concerns. "I’ve NEVER had any concerns about his hearing or vision." [ar049-0001]

## Across the on-niche posts

### The parent has tried strategies, practice or medical checks.

127 of 171 posts.

Attempts include consequences, routines, exposure, activities, language correction and medical testing.

- "We've demonstrated and have also had him demonstrate gentle behavior in these instances, and then he gets the toy back." [ar066-0032]
- "We later learned of the reading at 21 months and insisted on a fresh blood draw." [ar078-0024]

**But:** Trying does not guarantee relief. "We do try to give her a warning and then the tears just start at that time instead of when the noise actually starts." [ar006-0111]

### The parent feels guilt, overwhelm or helplessness.

104 of 171 posts.

Parents also describe embarrassment, anger, anxiety and exhaustion.

- "Feel like I’m going completely insane with guilt over this and so upset I didn’t notice this faster." [ar051-0037]
- "I feel awful for even thinking this, but a small part of me is almost regretting having a second." [ar014-0031]

**But:** Peer reassurance can bring relief. "*Thanks to everyone for your input, I'm feeling way better now, actually." [ar038-0060]

### The parent hears conflicting judgments and advice from other adults.

94 of 171 posts.

Relatives, teachers and clinicians offer warnings, referrals, support or reassurance.

- "She told me “my kids didn’t act this way!”" [ar032-0017]
- "The teacher said that she has trouble focusing sometimes." [ar006-0261]

**But:** Some advice is reassuring. "Her pediatrician reassured us that it's a phase and advised us to keep exposing her to social situations without too much pressure, which we've been doing." [ar028-0186]

### The parent reports buying practical items for everyday activities.

3 of 171 posts.

Three posts name purchases: ear defenders and train tickets, special markers, or Vinted clothes; prices are unknown.
