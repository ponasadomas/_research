# baby-development: pattern 6, The child prefers someone else (children of 2 and 3 years)

**Written:** 2026-11-20 from baby-development/evidence/2026-11/age-reread/digest-08.md
**Reviewed by the owner:** not yet

## Limits

A random, keyword-free sample of 20,000 Reddit posts from twelve parenting subreddits, 1 April–24 October 2026, yielded 3,811 on-niche posts, including 1,340 about children of 2 and 3 years. This pattern contains 40 on-niche posts and 1 near post, all read; each count is posts tagged by one reader, not people or market prevalence. Forms from different digest readers would be merged by judgement; this single digest’s distinct forms stay separate.

## On-niche posts

### The parent feels unloved despite doing the daily care.

4 of 40 posts.

They want affection and special moments together.

- "Does she just need a break from me?? I’m losing my mind. It feels like she doesn’t love me or wants to be around me which is also heartbreaking." [ar052-0026]
- "It would be one thing if she preferred just her dad, but she prefers EVERYONE over me. Her aunts, her grandma and nana, her uncles, literally if anyone else is around, she immediately ignores me and goes to them." [ar015-0026]

**But:** Illness brought an exception. "The only time I can think she requested me above anyone else is when she was hospitalized for RSV at the beginning of this year." [ar015-0024]

### The parent thinks fewer limits make another adult more appealing.

4 of 40 posts.

Discipline and household responsibilities feel unequally shared.

- "Whats different? Papa lets her do whatever . He tells us when she’s bad but leaves us to discipline her. He just an easy going non confrontational person. I on the other hand im playful but im more structured than papa and my partner." [ar029-0010]
- "Sometime she's bad cop, I'm usually good cop or at least indifferent cop, I believe most of it stems from that. I've told her we can try to switch things up and I can be more stern but she declines." [ar032-0088]

**But:** Reconnection can be quick. "Which to a 2 year old was back as besties by dinner time nuggets." [ar032-0088]

### The parent struggles to share care or separate briefly.

4 of 40 posts.

Distress interrupts daily care and drop-offs.

- "As long as she is out of sights, he shows major distress. Does not stay peacefully even with me." [ar022-0054]
- "He is going through phases, both during bedtime, daycare drop offs, and other seemingly random times during the day when he cries desperately out for a parent, usually the one that is not in the room at the moment." [ar049-0040]

**But:** One child settles after the protest. "He does usually calm down once the initial anxiety is over (e.g. once he gets to school he's fine, once I've started doing his bath and bedtime routine he's fine)." [ar060-0155]

### The parent needs help coping with the child’s rejecting words.

3 of 40 posts.

Parents seek solidarity or guidance on their response.

- "Im around him way more than mom since my schedule is very flexible, so we do everything together, but this constant yelling "no dad" with everything I do is extremely frustrating and annoying. Anyone else deal with it?" [ar082-0023]
- "I’m not sure how to recover from this. Obviously I know she’s going through a phase but man it really made me wonder why the fuck I stayed home for the past 2 years instead of sending her to daycare. Hope you’re having a better Monday." [ar038-0009]

**But:** One father also acknowledges yelling and apologizing. "And I'll admit, I do lose my patience and temper, and unfortunately I've yelled at her multiple times. And I feel so bad about it, I always apologize and talk to her afterwards." [ar064-0081]

### The preferred parent wants the rejected partner to feel wanted.

3 of 40 posts.

Reassurance and encouraging more care do not necessarily help.

- "I feel bad for him because he does a lot for them but then they ask for me at bedtime or for snuggles etc. And will sometimes downright refuse his help or affection." [ar073-0044]
- "Unfortunately and as you would expect just saying he does love her is useless, even reminding her of the times he does ask for her." [ar028-0155]

**But:** Preference can switch. "My son (6) has always preferred me, but my younger daughter (2) was preferring my husband for awhile but lately wants nothing to do with him." [ar073-0044]

### The father or new caregiver wants to build a bond.

3 of 40 posts.

They want acceptance; regular outings remain a plan.

- "I have a 2-year-old daughter and correct me if I'm wrong, I think it's common for young toddlers to bond with their mothers more than dads. But from your experience, when did your daughter begin to "feel" or "know" or "appreciate" you as a father? A first-time father is asking..." [ar052-0040]
- "I know I shouldn’t feel this way and it takes time. It just feels like rejection in the moment." [ar003-0064]

**But:** The new caregiver also sees improvement. "I’d say overall, son is trending to be more comfortable and playful around me vs. 6 months ago." [ar003-0064]

### The father enjoys time alone together but is rejected around mother.

3 of 40 posts.

Giving mother a break at home proves harder.

- "The reason I usually take him out is that if we're hanging around the house, the yard, or the basement, he eventually decides he's done with dad and wants mom. Then it turns into a struggle to keep him engaged, there are tears, and my wife usually ends up giving in." [ar072-0044]
- "He’s constantly asking his Mum to do stuff and he tells me to “go away, I don’t want you only Mummy”." [ar038-0161]

**But:** Alone, the child can be affectionate. "He wants to be my friend, he tells me he loves me and he wants to do all the things we would normally do (play in the garden, play tractors, go to the driving range etc.)." [ar038-0162]

### The mother fears managing another baby while being the only acceptable caregiver.

2 of 40 posts.

Pregnant mothers describe intensified demands for their presence.

- "Now she refuses to let my husband do bedtime and when I do bedtime will not let me leave." [ar045-0027]
- "I’m already exhausted by this and can’t imagine doing it with a newborn." [ar009-0107]

**But:** One also calls the attachment sweet. "It’s very sweet but I just found out I’m pregnant with #2 and panicking about how things will be when I literally can’t always be with her." [ar009-0107]

### The mother fears pregnancy or a new baby is weakening their closeness.

2 of 40 posts.

Mothers want to preserve or regain closeness.

- "I'm a few days away from my second baby's due date - and instead of excitement I'm just feeling guilty and sad for my 2yo son, especially when he's at nursery. He's starting pushing more around my DH and mum, and runs to give me a cuddle and then stops when he sees the bump and doesn't want me anymore." [ar043-0087]
- "But boy I miss her soooo much and feel so jealous." [ar081-0118]

**But:** One still has happy time alone together. "I'm trying to spend quality time before baby is born, he's happy when just us but wants nothing to do with me if husband/granny/grandad is around." [ar043-0088]

### The mother worries illness or demanding work has damaged the bond.

2 of 40 posts.

Limited availability raises fears about their future relationship.

- "He kicked me in my gut before and I had a miscarriage. So this age is hard. I guess what I'm wondering is, if you were absent from your child's life for the first years, were you still able to have a good relationship with them when they grew up?" [ar021-0122]
- "We just found out I'm pregnant with #2 and I can't help but feel like this is just going to be another baby I carry who loves dad more and puts up with me. I just feel so unwanted and left out." [ar069-0234]

**But:** The resident also considers energy for play rather than hours alone. "It could be more due to that than the hours because of medical training… at least I hope" [ar069-0237]

### The parent feels hurt when the child prefers grandparents to home.

2 of 40 posts.

Parents feel unmissed or fear losing their bond.

- "When she came back she didnt even wanna go into her bed or fall asleep without grandma because there they slept together in one bed ... and fell asleep watching cartoons, which again I said it should NOT be like this." [ar014-0043]
- "After she left, he immediately asked to go back." [ar042-0089]

**But:** One values the closeness and childcare. "I love how close my toddler (age 2.5) is to them, from the get go they have always been very active and involved grandparents, my toddler actually stays overnight 2-3x a week due to my night shifts and I understand he loves being there." [ar042-0088]

### The parent needs to prepare the child for overnight separation.

2 of 40 posts.

Renewed attachment complicates an upcoming absence.

- "What advice do I give the grandparents for this situation? How can we prepare him for it? Is there anything I can do in advance or in the event?" [ar048-0069]
- "I'm talking tantrums and screaming levels of anger if she has to be away from me. She's been a menace to her own father and loves her aunts as long as I'm in the room." [ar004-0148]

**But:** Sleepovers with grandma can go well, but grandma is also travelling. "She does very well on the sleepovers, the problem is that grandma is the one I'm going on the trip with." [ar004-0147]

### The parent wants the partner to trust their care.

2 of 40 posts.

Parents dispute interventions or explanations that undermine their bond.

- "I also just want my husband to trust that I can handle it. Being a mom is really all I have right now since I don’t have a “real job” and I just want to feel like I’m good at it with no help." [ar013-0014]
- "I’ve respectfully told my wife I disagree with her assessment but how do I SHOW her that? Or is my son really afraid of me just at bedtime?" [ar062-0078]

**But:** The father already gives accepted care. "He will do his bath time routine with me without a problem." [ar062-0077]

### The mother repeats care her husband already gave to calm their child.

1 of 40 posts.

Exact repetition extends to bathing and diaper placement.

- "But, for my son to calm down, I have to reenact everything my husband did to the T." [ar030-0077]
- "I just do wonder if there is some neurodivergence going on because it seems like total anxiety and not the kind of tantrum he has over other things." [ar030-0080]

**But:** Father and son otherwise enjoy each other. "My husband and son have a great relationship outside of this issue and have the best time together and he’s super loving and everything of course so nothing to do with him personally." [ar030-0078]

### The caregiver is exhausted by the child wanting a largely absent parent.

1 of 40 posts.

The caregiver encourages contact while explaining away the absence.

- "Here lately EVERY TIME there is an inconvenience, son bumps his head, etc, son asks for Parent A. It’s driving me fucking crazy. I’m at my breaking point for it." [ar066-0067]
- "I’ve tried explaining to son that Parent A is working or busy so they don’t grow up knowing how little parent A is actually trying but I’m on the verge of giving up because I’m exhausted by the favoritism." [ar066-0068]

**But:** Some contact continues. "Now son sees Parent A maybe 2 days a week for 4 hours or less (of their own accord, we don’t have anything in writing and I aim to never say no when they ask for hours.)" [ar066-0066]

### The parent wants grandparents to become familiar enough to care for the child.

1 of 40 posts.

Infrequent contact makes care distressing for an autistic, nonverbal child.

- "Without realizing it, my MIL hadn’t seen my son in over 2 months so he basically forgot who she was. We gave it an hour or so but my son never calmed in the presence of MIL so I took the rest of the day off work to care for my son." [ar058-0039]
- "I would love a world where my son can spend time with his grandparents but currently that would not be possible without severely stressing him out." [ar058-0040]

**But:** The child is generally happy. "I should also mention my son is autistic - he is nonverbal and has other limitations but is generally a very happy kid." [ar058-0038]

### The mother accepts dad’s appeal but questions the tantrums when he returns.

1 of 40 posts.

She understands wanting dad’s attention, but questions the screaming.

- "When she's with me, everything is basically okay. Everything flows naturally, she eats, play, shower, sleeps without fighting and without crying." [ar013-0109]
- "I'm okay with it and I understand because sometimes she doesn't see him in all day but why she has to scream and cry so much?" [ar013-0109]

**But:** The change is not absolute. "Sometimes she's "fine" but mostly dad comes home and she shift to a little rebel with a lot of tantrums." [ar013-0109]

## Near posts

### The father seeks help regulating his hurt at the child’s rejecting words.

1 of 1 posts.

A father with depression wants to avoid resenting his son.

- "I feel so alone, my wife just went to work and dropped him off at daycare. Today's my day off and I just spent the first hour after they left crying." [ar037-0040]
- "I myself realize from this post that I also do need to learn how to regulate my feelings better." [ar037-0052]

**But:** He sees a child still learning to communicate. "He's still learning how to communicate his feelings. I was very caught off guard and never thought about how kids say stuff like that when they're young and don't know entirely what effect it has or what they're really trying to communicate." [ar037-0051]

## Across the on-niche posts

### The parent tries shared care, special time and explanations.

37 of 40 posts.

Attempts include preparing for absence and apologizing after yelling; future-only plans do not count.

- "And I'll admit, I do lose my patience and temper, and unfortunately I've yelled at her multiple times. And I feel so bad about it, I always apologize and talk to her afterwards." [ar064-0081]
- "I have tried to make special time just for him and me on Friday mornings, like going to get donuts and doing something fun (like going to the playground or library)." [ar060-0157]

**But:** Switching bedtime can bring only temporary relief. "At times we have switched who has put him to sleep and it might stabilize for a few days until he starts calling for mama." [ar049-0041]

### The parent gets help, conflicting explanations or resistance from other adults.

31 of 40 posts.

Other adults comfort the child, dispute choices or resist sharing responsibility.

- "Through tears I told my husband to stop coming down and helping, and he said I made him feel bad for wanting to check on our son to make sure he was okay." [ar013-0013]
- "Mom tells me it’s because he feels unsafe with me because it was hard for us to bond when he was first born." [ar062-0075]

**But:** Some caregivers work through distress supportively. "At daycare, he has a solid meltdown every time we drop him off. Our daycare providers understand this and are able to hug him and eventually get him out of it. When it’s friends who are watching him for the day it’s harder but they work through it as well and are understanding." [ar049-0042]

### The parent reports ordinary purchases and relatives’ spending.

3 of 40 posts.

Reports cover clothes, grandparents’ things and a cruise paid for by someone else; prices are unknown. None is a parenting solution.

- "Now we are back and better but Ive noticed they really just trying to buy off my toddler with all sorts of better things that a family of 4 (me,dad and her little sister) living in apartment cant afford like trampoline, sandbox, dogs cats etc all sorts of funsies there. When I show them pictures of our family events her first question is that hasnt she wanted to come to them like shes not even happy we went anywhere as family." [ar014-0042]
- "I can't cancel this trip. It's already fully paid for, and I desperately need it as I'm so burned out." [ar004-0152]

**But:** The doll purchase is still a plan; payment for named care services or evaluation is unknown. Nothing found against the reported purchases.
