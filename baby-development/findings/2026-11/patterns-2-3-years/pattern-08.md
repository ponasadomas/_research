# baby-development: pattern 8, Delight or pride in what the child now does (children of 2 and 3 years)

**Written:** 2026-11-20 from baby-development/evidence/2026-11/age-reread/digest-10.md
**Reviewed by the owner:** not yet

## Limits

These come from a keyword-free random sample of 20,000 Reddit posts in twelve parenting subreddits, 1 April–24 October 2026: 3,811 on-niche, including 1,340 about ages 2 and 3. This pattern’s 81 on-niche and 17 near posts were all read; counts are posts tagged by one reader, not prevalence among parents. Forms would be merged across readers by judgement; this single digest’s forms stay separate, retaining its infant posts and unknown ages despite the age label.

## On-niche posts

### The parent enjoys the surprising explanations their child gives the world.

9 of 81 posts.

Body questions, literal meanings and unexpected descriptions make parents laugh.

- "When I told him "No, I did not eat a baby," he asked how did the baby go into my stomach!" [ar023-0064]
- "Yesterday, I get home from work and my son immediately runs up to me and says, "DADDY! Today I had a really long fart! It was longer than when they said "GOOOAAAAALLLLLL!"" [ar053-0100]

**But:** Language can still be difficult.

- "Currently have a 2.5yo and we’re struggling with “me” and “you” words." [ar021-0107]

### The parent enjoys games, music and interests they can share with their child.

9 of 81 posts.

Children join games, hobbies and elaborate bedtime play.

- "For a good 10-15 minutes, the four of us were just running around the basement and laughing together." [ar040-0052]
- "We build Thomas the Tank Engine tracks together every day, roleplay characters from his favourite shows, and lately he’s gotten really into board games and charades." [ar045-0050]

**But:** Music can overwhelm the parent.

- "My husband saw I was overstimulated and took our son outside for them to practice" [ar080-0028]

### The parent enjoys the imaginary worlds their child builds from ordinary things.

8 of 81 posts.

Invented animals, future offices, nests and games bring amusement and sometimes concern.

- "“look! My lizard came out of my butt!”, & now he’s running around pulling lizards out of everyone’s butts. It’s an epidemic truly." [ar058-0019]
- "Her office has lots of toys, gummy bears, strawberry ice cream and multiple play areas." [ar040-0008]

**But:** One parent cannot explain the game’s origin.

- "It’s adorable, but also concerning. Very unclear where he learned about crucifixion." [ar055-0125]

### The parent celebrates speech progress after worrying about how little their child could say.

7 of 81 posts.

Small combinations and fluent conversations both matter after earlier worries or delay.

- "When I woke up and came downstairs, my son runs up to me, hands me a card that he made, and says “happy mommy day!”" [ar033-0048]
- "He started saying “mama tété” (“tété” is the French word we use for nursing) — his first 2-word combination and he says it each time he wants to nurse!" [ar036-0004]

**But:** Progress does not mean the delay has resolved.

- "He’s still quite behind with expressive speech, but we’ll finally start speech therapy in 2 weeks, and I’m really looking forward to it." [ar036-0006]

### The parent marvels at what their child remembers, understands or creates.

6 of 81 posts.

Parents marvel at memory, drawing, writing and translation.

- "His mothe said something in Bulgarian, he then looked at me and said the same thing in West Frisian and we were both quite shocked." [ar011-0104]
- "This morning was the first time he wrote his ABC’s without tracing. I’m super proud of him." [ar061-0022]

**But:** Advanced speech still belongs to a toddler.

- "I agree with him not receiving grace, mostly by older kids because he’s acting like them verbally, but social/emotionally, still very much a toddler" [ar064-0006]

### The parent laughs when their child talks back or gets around an instruction.

6 of 81 posts.

Clever replies amuse parents while leaving the problem unresolved.

- "She kept arguing back at me so I said "we're not arguing" and she immediately said "yes we are"" [ar060-0105]
- "In the middle of me talking, she covered my mouth and said “Mami, don’t breathe on me please.”" [ar024-0001]

**But:** The behaviour can hurt.

- "When did kids get this smart? My 3 year old just slapped me really hard across the face, when I asked him why he slapped me, his response was " I didn't slap you daddy, I just high fived your face"" [ar022-0138]

### The parent feels loved when their child chooses or trusts them.

6 of 81 posts.

Thanks and requests for company or help feel significant.

- "There's no real reason, he doesn't expect a toy or a treat or anything.  He just likes coming with me.  And it's the best" [ar069-0145]
- "There is something special in being picked by your own tiny little human to be the one who can help her." [ar012-0161]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent hears familiar people and phrases coming back through their child.

4 of 81 posts.

Children copy siblings, books and adult habits, then give the words unexpected uses.

- "[…] Five minutes later he dropped a spoon and whispered "sorry spoon" like it had feelings and a mortgage." [ar045-0058]
- "I’m glad he’s been repeating some positive phrases but confused why he thanks himself for stuff his dad and I do for him lol" [ar016-0058]

**But:** Imitation can unsettle them.

- "Cute, yes. Also mildly alarming, because now I am hearing myself through this very small echo." [ar045-0059]

### The parent is moved when their child notices distress and offers comfort.

4 of 81 posts.

One father describes empathy despite limited speech and movement.

- "[…] Then she laid her little head on me and said "it's ok Mommy."" [ar038-0034]
- "His empathy and worry for his baby sister just makes me so proud of him, of his mum, and fuck it, of me." [ar049-0080]

**But:** Concern can need soothing.

- "he didnt stop sobbing until she did, despite me hugging him tightly and explaining she was fine, she just doesnt know how to tell us what she wants yet." [ar049-0079]

### The parent celebrates their child doing more to care for themselves or the home.

4 of 81 posts.

Parents celebrate laundry, cleanup, accessible storage and potty use, including for a child born blind.

- "My 3 year old heard the washing machine finish and I found him turning the laundry over into the dryer. […]" [ar070-0098]
- "I decided to bite the bullet and try the Porky Pig approach — i.e. no pants or diaper — and yeah! *Four pees on the potty!!*" [ar022-0145]

**But:** Independence varies by activity.

- "Then he refused anything other than cheese on his taco and would only try the chicken if I fed it to him." [ar070-0099]

### The parent feels sad as their child leaves babyhood behind.

4 of 81 posts.

New names, a haircut, a birthday or abandoning a sleep sack can feel like losses.

- "Kid hit me with Dad today and it hit me hard. […]" [ar075-0066]
- "I'm Tearing Up - my 2.5 yo daughter just got her first haircut." [ar004-0125]

**But:** Enjoyment remains.

- "[…] She's a hilarious, vivacious toddler that keeps me laughing all day long, but I suddenly got sad about her not being a "baby" anymore." [ar054-0119]

### The parent laughs at the odd and messy things their child does.

4 of 81 posts.

Two posts concern infants; others describe a toddler’s nose treasure and candy corn.

- "Today my 3 year old told me she loves candy corn as she blithely shoved them in her mouth, ignoring the horror on my face." [ar073-0139]
- "[…] Since she arrived earth side, she has proven to be a highly pressurized system capable of launching poop and pee a distance of up to 51 inches (yes, she’s a girl, and yes, I’ve measured). […]" [ar027-0147]

**But:** Amusement can accompany an unpleasant cleanup.

- "No worries we got it out after a quick blow (and about 30min of me dry heaving) and no tears were shed until I threw her snot treasure in the trash." [ar041-0150]

### The parent sees their child learning to manage feelings through their example.

3 of 81 posts.

Reassurance, taking breaks and apologising give children ways to calm down or explain themselves.

- "[…] Now when my son is upset he walks away from me and closes the door to "take a break", he opens the door when he's ready to talk about it." [ar038-0046]
- "She looked at me and said, “And I’m still learning how to be me.”" [ar066-0140]

**But:** Comfort does not always get through.

- "Not every time I try to comfort my kid do I actually get through to him. That would be impossible." [ar069-0048]

### The parent is proud when their child shows kindness or speaks up for themselves.

3 of 81 posts.

Parents value resilience, friendship and insisting that bodily boundaries be heard.

- "I was so impressed and proud of my son and how resilient he was. He kept choosing kindness and it paid off for him. […]" [ar077-0086]
- "He said “stop being mean to me, I’m not poopy pants, say sorry!”" [ar010-0106]

**But:** Adults may not listen.

- "He waved it off and said “what, that didn’t hurt you! You’re crazy!” Like she was being silly. I saw her get quiet and shrivel." [ar017-0071]

### The parent is delighted to discover their child can enjoy playing without them.

2 of 81 posts.

Independent play or book time brings surprise, relief or a sense of shared temperament.

- "I was shocked and so incredibly proud of her and honestly relieved. I didn't know she was capable of that." [ar032-0073]
- "But it just makes me giggle that she has her quiet time and I do too." [ar042-0094]

**But:** Quiet time is only part of the child’s behaviour.

- "Please note though that this child is also an absolute terror who demands "hot cakes" every Saturday morning" [ar042-0095]

### The parent deliberately looks for good in a toddler stage they find hard.

2 of 81 posts.

Small treasures and calling it the “terrific twos” help parents reframe continuing difficulty.

- "My favorite part of this 3 year old is what you find lying around the house. […]" [ar027-0048]
- "It’s definitely helped me reframe it to more growth and brain development and getting the two year molars is a lot and it’s amazing and terrific to see." [ar051-0042]

**But:** Reframing does not remove the strain.

- "Every single day feels like a battle, uphill, downhill, sideways hill, every hill between my 3 year old, 1 year old, and 2 working parents." [ar027-0045]

## Near posts

### The parent is proud of their child’s effort and progress in learning.

4 of 17 posts.

Ages are unknown; achievements include goals, school and performance.

- "What made the difference: THEY picked the goals, not me. […]" [ar075-0044]
- "As a reward for his hard efforts, I bought him Skyrim for his Switch." [ar010-0080]

**But:** A performance need not be flawless.

- "She was a little stiff, but she had a good time doing the rehearsals with her friends and being part of the show so I'm happy!" [ar024-0028]

### The parent celebrates a bedtime change that helps their child settle.

3 of 17 posts.

Pacifier removal, sleeping alone and a sleepy history lesson bring relief or humour.

- "Second night, he whined for 5 minutes, then laid there and eventually fell asleep." [ar029-0051]
- "Come to find out she thinks I smell and she’s no longer afraid to sleep alone." [ar080-0091]

**But:** The first night was hard.

- "He was so upset for about 2 hours (we came and checked in and gave him the spiel)." [ar029-0050]

### The parent loves raising this child but is uncertain about having another.

2 of 17 posts.

Birth distress or disagreement with a partner makes the decision difficult despite delight in the existing child.

- "I was told it would all leave my mind after she was born- but I did not." [ar035-0094]
- "[…] I love watching my daughter grow and become her own person. […]" [ar081-0100]

**But:** Fulfilment can mean stopping.

- "He feels he's getting everything out of the parent experience with the one." [ar081-0099]

- "[…] It is cool to see his progression and makes for a great keepsake. […]" [ar033-0095]

- "The closing of one chapter but the beginning of another, very exciting chapter." [ar010-0024]

### The parent finds that reading with their child brings back grief.

1 of 17 posts.

Reading recalls a late father and missed milestones; the child’s age is unknown.

- "At the end my daughter could see I was a bit teary eyed and gave me a huge hug." [ar076-0049]
- "It's always hurt not having him there for those milestones." [ar076-0049]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent celebrates their child following a repeated safety lesson.

1 of 17 posts.

A preschooler of unknown exact age brings a found heart pill to the parent in exchange for candy.

- "When you find a pill, bring it to me and I'll give you candy!" [ar054-0050]
- "It was a heart pill Granny left, and LO knew just what to do." [ar054-0051]

**But:** Success followed years of repetition.

- "My LO is a preschooler now, but it took 2.5 years for this advice to work, so now I pass it on:" [ar054-0049]

### The parent is overwhelmed when their child begins saying “I love you.”

1 of 17 posts.

A two-and-a-half-year-old’s affection prompts tears the parent struggles to manage.

- "My son (2.5 yrs) just started saying 'I love you' to me from this weekend." [ar018-0099]
- "And it has turned me into an emotional wreck!" [ar018-0100]

**But:** The parent worries about discouraging him.

- "I don't want to worry him or discourage him." [ar018-0103]

### The parent celebrates feeding progress after a difficult beginning.

1 of 17 posts.

Doubled birth weight follows early feeding difficulties.

- "[…] My ebf LO is currently 10 weeks and has somehow doubled his birth weight! […]" [ar009-0057]
- "So proud of my LO but also so proud of myself." [ar009-0058]

**But:** A personal feeding goal remains.

- "But I have a goal for me and my babe and I’m sticking with it." [ar009-0058]

### The parent enjoys their child’s growing skills but struggles to care for themselves.

1 of 17 posts.

Toddler independence coexists with exhaustion and disrupted self-care.

- "He’s potty trained and speaks in full sentences now so it feels like we have a whole kid these days." [ar042-0066]
- "The rest of the time I just feel exhausted and like I’m just surviving and on autopilot." [ar042-0067]

**But:** Some days feel manageable.

- "I feel like I have a few good days out of the month where I can balance taking care of myself, feel like a super mom and feel very harmonious with my husband." [ar042-0067]

## Across the on-niche posts

### The parent has tried teaching, practice, routines or therapy.

62 of 81 posts.

This tag includes activities, conversations and arrangements already made.

- "[…] He’s been in speech therapy for 9 months now and has made amazing progress." [ar033-0046]
- "I kissed her head and said “mommy will talk to papa for you, but next time, you just tell him ‘That’s too rough, papa!’” And we practiced." [ar017-0072]

**But:** Attempts can create complications.

- "We tried random things sometimes to make bedtime more fun so he wouldn’t fight it, but now it’s resulted in some pretty ridiculous practices. […]" [ar067-0117]

### The parent encounters other people’s concern, praise, help or dismissal.

32 of 81 posts.

Other adults can change how a parent experiences the child’s progress.

- "The older boy kept kind of picking on him and taunting him, but we let them go." [ar077-0084]
- "My husband saw I was overstimulated and took our son outside for them to practice" [ar080-0028]

**But:** Adults can dismiss hurt.

- "He waved it off and said “what, that didn’t hurt you! You’re crazy!” Like she was being silly." [ar017-0071]

### The parent reports buying learning books or supplies to organise the home.

2 of 81 posts.

Explicit purchases include tracing books, two roughly $6 packs of photo paper and a roughly $20 drawer-organising set; other costs are unknown.

- "We started buying the learning books so he loves to trace. That is really what got him going." [ar061-0025]
- "I ordered a bulk drawer organizing set off of Amazon for like $20 and it did my whole house." [ar079-0066]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### The parent expresses pride, love or relief alongside harder feelings.

60 of 81 posts.

Stated feelings include joy and gratitude as well as grief, guilt and sadness about growing up.

- "Ive never been more proud and more in love with him. What a fucking guy." [ar049-0080]
- "And that was the greatest sense of relief I’ve ever felt in my life." [ar007-0146]

**But:** Pride does not remove guilt about time and support.

- "[…] I’ve carried *so much guilt* over the fact that I don’t get to spend enough time with her. […]" [ar022-0144]
