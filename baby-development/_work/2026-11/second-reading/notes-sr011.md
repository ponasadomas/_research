Read all 150 assigned post files in share order, including every archived reply by the original author. None were unavailable or left unread. The supplied share belongs to the supervisor's random, non-keyword sample; I did no further selection. These posts were published 14–21 April 2026, with some later replies. Results: **21 yes, 43 near, 86 no**. Kept **233 quote records**, from all 21 yes posts and 19 near posts explicitly tied to development or skills. Counts below are posts, not quotes or people, and describe this share rather than market prevalence.

Among yes posts, concern that parental choices might harm development or the relationship appeared in 6; delight in abilities or growth in 5; unexplained milestone/normality concerns in 4. Activities, age-appropriate purchases, eating skills, and groups/daycare each appeared in 3; known delay/therapy, difficult behaviour, and caregiver preference each in 2. Patterns overlap. What stood out was the range within a concern. A parent describing limited words also described affection, running, gestures and good eye contact; a later reply confirmed speech/physical therapy approval and a proposed autism assessment. Another parent's worry about absent babbling ended with a later report of babbling and a possible first word. Practical pleasure also appeared: longer independent play, helping with laundry and rubbish, music, and responding to conversation. Severe distress, rage, parenting regret, partner belittling and thoughts about not being here were retained where quote-eligible, alongside attempts to cope and moments of affection.

Age uncertainty stays visible; explicitly older children remain no even with a younger sibling.
