# Reader 121 — second reading

Read all 150 supplied post files, whole and in share order: **27 yes, 45 near, 78 no**. Extracted **122 quotes from 40 posts**: all 27 yes posts and 13 near posts with an explicit developmental, skill-learning or regression link. No files were unavailable or left unread. This is the assigned share of the random sample, not a keyword selection. Replies were read wherever included; missing or unfetched replies were not reconstructed.

Among the 27 yes posts, reassurance about a specific behaviour or ability was the largest pattern (1: 8 posts). Hard behaviour and fear about the parent's own choices each appeared in 5; stage-specific purchases in 4; daily activities and delight in growth each in 3; daycare/enrichment and potty learning each in 2. Preference for another caregiver, interference from others, eating readiness and an existing diagnosis/therapy referral each appeared once. One post sought practical rolling instruction without asking whether the baby was normal, so received 0. These are overlapping post counts, not quote counts.

Again and again, parents wanted to know whether something was normal, how long a difficult phase would last, or whether their response was making things worse. Comparisons came from other babies, social videos, relatives and professionals. One mother feared that unscheduled feeding, late bedtime and little floor tummy time meant daycare would develop her baby better. Another parent wanted to teach rolling partly because daycare staff might not respond immediately. Growth also brought amusement and sadness: potty learning, a first birthday approaching, and funny nursing requests.

The 45 near posts comprise 10 sleep, 9 parent-state, 8 age-unknown, 7 feeding, 6 age-over-24, 4 logistics and 1 other. Borderline decisions: “two years” and “just turned two” stayed near; a clearly 25-month-old stayed no. Sleep-only schedules stayed no; explicit self-settling learning, milestone practice, regression, daytime behaviour or independent quiet time qualified as near. Bottle amounts alone stayed no; gag exercises and alternative feeding skills qualified as near. The custody post was marked yes for concern about infant security during separation; this is an attachment interpretation, with no explicit development wording. No applicable author explicitly stated paying for something for the concern; using products or having a nanny was not treated as proof of payment.

Every quote was checked against its raw file, including punctuation and whitespace. IDs, share membership, row coverage and quote totals were checked by script.
