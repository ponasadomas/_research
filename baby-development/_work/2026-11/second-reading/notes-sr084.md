# Reader 084 — second reading

Read all 150 assigned post files in full, including every archived author reply present. This is one assigned share of the supervisor’s random sample of text posts from twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; I did not draw or independently verify that sample. These files run from 30 July to 7 August. No files were unavailable and none remain unread.

There are 25 yes, 42 near and 83 no posts. The 132 quotes come from all 25 yes posts and the 12 near posts explicitly tied to development. Counts below count posts once per pattern, across yes and near together; patterns overlap. Hard behaviour appears in 14, fear about the parent’s own choices in 9, interference in 8, normality or suspected problems in 7, delight in 6, feeding skills in 4, and known delays or professional intervention in 3. Day-filling, gear, preference and groups each appear in 2; potty readiness in 1. Another 21 fit none of the numbered patterns.

Sleep repeatedly overlaps with the niche: 12 near posts have sleep as their border. Some link it to walking, rolling, regression or development outright; others describe screaming, crib play or settling preferences without such a link. Ten near posts primarily concern parental distress or relationships but describe the child’s behaviour, play or day. Nine have the two-year age ambiguity, six an unstated age, four another main subject, and one feeding as the main subject. Pure night waking, feeding quantities, illness without relevant behaviour, and clearly older children were marked no.

What struck me was the range alongside worry: a first reciprocal affectionate gesture, a child guiding a parent’s hands into a sign, unusual interests and playful airplane feeding. Worry included vaccine-related family blame, a sibling caregiver fearing a newborn’s possible disability, and exhaustion around extensive therapy. These passages were retained in their own words. Brand mentions did not automatically mean payment: four included posts explicitly report buying or hiring; obtaining or using a product alone was insufficient.

Border decisions remain reviewable in the ledger. “Just turned two” and the two-to-three toy range were near because exact months are missing; an explicit older age or birth date overrode a vague “two.” Speech evaluation and an early-intervention referral were assigned 12, with notes that therapy had not yet begun; an unevaluated suspected delay stayed in 1. General behaviour advice and a developmental medication question lack a stated caregiving role and remain near with that uncertainty noted. Month boundaries use the later stage. Some authors identify as mothers without stating previous parenting experience; that uncertainty is noted. Repeated equivalent sleep schedules were quoted once; social acknowledgements without substantive information were not cut.
