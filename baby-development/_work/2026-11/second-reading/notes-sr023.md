# Reader 023 — second reading

Read all 150 assigned post files, including the original posters’ archived replies, in share order. This is one assigned share of the supervisor’s random sample; I made no keyword selection or additional sampling. No posts are unread or unavailable. Marks: **17 yes, 31 near, 102 no**. Pulled **120 quotes** from every yes post and all 11 near posts marked as explicitly tied to development.

Across yes and near posts, choosing age-suitable things appeared in 10 posts: toys, a walker, travel sleeping equipment, carriers and a swaddle transition. Everyday activities appeared in 9 posts through outings, gardening, songs, pretend play and attempts to expend toddler energy. These counts include borderline posts and must not be treated as rates among parents or buyers. Sleep alone, feeding amounts alone, health alone, pregnancy, relationship disputes without child-development content, and clearly older children account for much of the 102 no posts.

The concrete developmental concerns included absent babbling, fascination with hands, and not holding a bottle. Grandparents’ questions or confident comparisons sometimes intensified worry. Pride appeared in standing, crawling and expressive emotional language. One particularly clear contrast: one parent celebrated rapid standing and crawling, while another found constant supported standing physically painful. Another parent’s later reply reported that hand fascination had faded and the baby was doing well.

Borderline reasons: 11 age_unknown, 5 parent_state, 4 logistics, 5 sleep, 3 feeding, 2 age_over_24 and 1 other. “Two years old” without months stays near; one title saying “2y” became yes because a reply clarified “about to be.” A baby described only as a baby was treated as within the age limit, with stage unknown. Unbounded “toddler” ages stayed near. References to rolling, standing, anticipated crawling, regression, cup drinking, potty awareness and emotional expression were treated as explicit skill links; temperament or crying alone was not.

Second-hand vaccine/autism claims are preserved as the author reported them, with provenance in quote context; they are not verified causal findings. The sample contains repeated cross-posted text about butter and sleep, and about parental insomnia; each assigned post retains its own row. Only the three requested files were written.
