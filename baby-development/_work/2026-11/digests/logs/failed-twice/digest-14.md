### I feel rejected when my child chooses the other parent despite all the care I give.

13 of 129 posts.

These parents describe being pushed away, refused hugs, ignored on coming home, or unable to comfort a child who reaches for the other parent. Children range from seven to 22 months, with one post also discussing an almost-three-year-old sibling. Some say the preference has lasted since early infancy and doubt assurances that it is a phase; others describe jealousy, resentment, embarrassment or fear that the child will grow up disliking them.

- "But I am literally STARVED for my baby to show me the tiniest crumb of affection." [sr046-0061]
- "I love her so much and keep trying to build a strong connection with her and I feel like if he’s around she hates me." [sr007-0090]
- "If we go shopping with dad, my son will completely meltdown if I am near him and will constantly say ‘mommy go away’ so I have to walk behind him and his dad and not be in his sight otherwise he will have a meltdown in the shop." [sr047-0013]
- "But when we are home, we both play with him and spend time with him, so I don’t understand why it feels like he prefers his dad so much." [sr130-0044]

**But:** The same posts include affection when the other parent is absent, days favouring either parent, and a later reversal of a long preference: "I wanted to let you know that this was a phase that ultimately has passed for us now." [sr053-0030]
