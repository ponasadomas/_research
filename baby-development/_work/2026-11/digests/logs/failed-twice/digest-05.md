## Pattern 1, share 05: yes posts

### They ask when their child will stand or walk independently and how to encourage it.

9 of 198 posts.

These parents ask about standing and walking from 6 months to 17 months: some babies want supported walking very early, while others cruise, crawl or climb but resist independent steps. They try walkers, handholding, toys and games, or seek physical therapy stories; comparisons at daycare and the park can make the delay feel more serious. One post describes new leg buckling after walking had already begun.

- "She will walk barely holding onto one of our fingers, but she refuses to go from sitting to standing independently or walking independently." [sr134-0021]
- "I’ve tried using a push walker and even a walking harness, but she bends her knees within seconds and refuses." [sr003-0024]
- "I feel so guilty because I've been dreading going. I get a lot of stares from people with his size and the fact he isn't walking independently yet." [sr043-0016]
- "Joined daycare and all other babies are confidently walking expect mine and doesn’t seem excited to practice walking." [sr113-0203]
- "My 6 month old is showing all the readiness signs of wanting to walk, but of course she is so young I really don't think she'll be able to for some time." [sr079-0094]
- "He will be walking fine and then he just stumbles. It’s like his legs give out for a couple seconds then he’s fine." [sr083-0001]

**But:** The worry does not always mean walking is late or stays absent: one parent explicitly accepts 13 months as normal, and another reports first steps just after posting. "Ok, I know it's normal for 13mo not to walk, but I'm really curious to know how any of you encouraged your babies to walk!" [sr029-0042] "You won’t believe it, she took her first independent steps just 2 hours after I made that post! 😂 (Honestly, not exaggerating)" [sr003-0034]

### They ask why their baby does not roll, rolls only one way or stops doing it, and how to help.

11 of 198 posts.

At roughly 3.5 to 6 months, parents describe no rolling, occasional rolling that does not repeat, or back-to-belly rolling without a way back. Some practise daily and compare with younger babies; others want the return roll because being stuck leads to crying, reflux or fears of suffocation. They ask whether to keep helping, wait, or seek physiotherapy despite a doctor's reassurance.

- "Six month old still not rolling over and I am so beyond worried" [sr017-0001]
- "anyone here with a chunky baby? did they have a little bit of a harder time rolling and grabbing their feet? my girl is 4 months and she can only make it to her side so far 😭😭" [sr005-0096]
- "I do lots of floor time, practice rolls with him, tummy time, other core activities." [sr059-0130]
- "The result is constant rolling onto front, being happy for about 2 minutes (if that) then spitting up, fussing and eventually crying. I roll him back, 2 seconds later he's back on his front." [sr061-0159]
- "I’m just scared to death of her throwing up in her sleep and suffocating in it idk what to do to calm that worry." [sr117-0027]
- "Especially because the doctor keeps saying more tummy time but he's on the floor for three-five hours a day depending on what I can get him to tolerate." [sr123-0004]

**But:** Some babies show other motor skills or prefer the direction they already know; absence of the second roll is not described as absence of all movement. "Still can’t roll belly to back though and tbh shows zero interest in it even when being guided and encouraged to, she’s jumping straight to learning how to pivot on the belly." [sr024-0073]

### They ask why their baby is not sitting, crawling or getting into positions without help.

9 of 198 posts.

Mostly at 7 to 11 months, parents ask about sitting only when placed, not moving into sitting, absent crawling or pull-up, and army crawling instead of hands-and-knees crawling. They offer floor time and distant toys, wonder whether weight or motivation explains it, and want something to do before a visit. One aunt describes her nephew's motor delay and disagreement with his mother; another parent reports Gemini's explanation and questions whether the AI is merely reassuring them.

- "Often times we feel she has the desire to move but is not able to physically." [sr002-0223]
- "My 9 month old is army crawling, and he gets on his hands and knees and rocks back and forth. His 9 month WCC is soon and I’m not sure how to answer the question of “Is he crawling?”" [sr016-0027]
- "He'd rather plop and roll to the toys. If I move the toys when he rolls, he starts crying." [sr085-0123]
- "Yo he intentado alejar un poco sus juguetes de él pero por supuesto se frustra, llora... y el tummy time nada, no quiere hacer nada y llora." [sr041-0039]
- "I am talking to Gemini (the google chatgpt) and they say its normal because of heavy weight and because my baby seems so much ahead talking milestone. I can’t say if AI is just people pleasing, if my baby is really ahead or if that can explain the delay." [sr058-0035]
- "I know all babies progress at their own pace, but I can’t help but feel like I’m missing something or failing her." [sr123-0123]

**But:** The reported difficulty can coexist with effective movement or reassuring assessments; those reports do not establish a diagnosis. [sr005-0194] "I also had her alevaluayed by an occupational therapist at 6 months and was told that baby didn’t have any tension or physicality holding her back, she just thought she was not confident enough to become mobile." ### They ask whether their child's sounds, words or conversations are developing as they should.

From 13 weeks to almost 2 years, these posts ask about missing coos or consonant babbling, limited first words, no mama or dada, unclear word approximations, lost or less-used sounds, and speech that labels things without making requests or conversation. Parents narrate, read, sing, prompt and imitate; they want both timelines and a reason to seek speech support. One parent fears a congenital tongue tie will affect later speech and expose the child to teasing.

### They weigh missing gestures and other developmental signs and wonder whether their child needs assessment.

20 of 198 posts.

At about 5.5 to 20 months, parents and one babysitting relative assemble profiles of missing pointing, waving, imitation, speech or name response alongside movements, sensory preferences and social strengths. Some explicitly fear autism or ask about early intervention; others ask whether they are expecting too much or whether good eye contact and motor skills are reassuring. They describe dismissed concerns, disagreement with a partner, fear for the child's future and, in one case, hearing and genetic tests already completed.

- "Now he has several red flags for autism. The biggest one is that he doesn't point. He also doesn't consistently respond to his name and has a few other concerning behaviors." [sr050-0029]
- "But if I'm being completely honest, I'm grieving the future I imagined for my son, and I'm desperately hoping we're wrong." [sr050-0030]
- "Our Pediatrician has brushed off my concerns multiple times, so I am searching for a new one." [sr002-0299]
- "She used to be super social and smile at everyone and for the past month or so even I have a hard time getting her to smile or laugh." [sr107-0014]
- "I know babies develop at different rates, and because she was born 4 weeks early, I’m also wondering whether I should be considering her corrected age (around 6 months)." [sr114-0008]
- "Chromosomal microarray and Fragile X testing came back negative." [sr126-0077]
- "I'm no doctor so when my friend told me to look into development delays and autism I did as much research as I could and used tiktok and youtube as a visual." [sr075-0139]

**But:** These are concerns and informal interpretations, including the relative's account, rather than diagnoses. One later update still reports absent distant pointing and toe walking but substantial language and interaction. "Besides that everything else seems typical. He doesn't have any sensory issues, has great eye contact and has well over 200 words, sings songs, etc." [sr002-0304]
