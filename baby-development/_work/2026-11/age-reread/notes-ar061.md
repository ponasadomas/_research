# Reader 061 — age reread

All 150 supplied files were read whole in share order, including the archived author replies provided. Nothing remains unread. This share belongs to the supervisor’s random sample, not a keyword-selected set. Results: 24 yes, 42 near, 84 no; 149 quote passages. Counts are posts, not quotes or people; patterns overlap.

Among yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 11, groups/daycare in 7, delight in abilities in 6, concern about parental choices in 5, and questions about normality in 4. Toy/gear choices and potty learning each appeared in 3. Recurring situations included hitting or pinching despite several attempted responses, wanting cooperation without yelling, sudden daycare reluctance, and choosing learning tools. Pleasure was concrete too: alphabet writing, comforting speech, clever questions and growing restraint.

The near posts comprise 20 older-age cases, 7 unknown-age cases, 5 mainly about the parent’s state, 3 feeding, 3 sleep, 3 other and 1 logistics. Established child diagnosis/therapy appeared in 2 near posts and no yes posts. Explicit expenditure was recorded in 5 posts across yes and near. Ownership or programme attendance alone did not establish payment.

The contrast between competence and difficulty surprised me: a clever child thriving at preschool could be difficult at home; a child could toilet reliably naked but immediately wet clothing. Yelling, hand slaps and parental regret were retained alongside successes.

Borderlines include custody refusal (1sfudlm), vague “easy” temperament (1to4pi2), a hypothetical temperament poll (1xd20gx), adult retrospective developmental expectations (1vmyi6p), hypothetical safety examples mentioning sitting ability (1wk3amf), and future sports for a current infant (1wzbzu8). Four-year-olds past the fourth birthday were near. Generic worry about harm did not automatically establish a developmental link. “Preschool age” did not establish a numeric age.

Checks passed: one ledger row per share ID, unique sequential quote IDs, matching post IDs and metadata, and every quotation word for word in its raw file.
