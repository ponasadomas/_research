# Reader 069 — random-sample reading

Read all 150 assigned posts, including the authors’ archived replies, in share order. Nothing remains unread or unavailable. Results: **21 yes, 54 near, 75 no**. Extracted **248 quotes from 43 posts**: all 21 yes posts and 22 near posts with an explicit link to development, skills, learning, or regression. These are counts within the supplied random share, not estimates of parents or the market.

Among the 21 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 10, concern about the parent’s own effect in 4, potty learning in 3, and delight in the child in 3. Social participation and known difficulties/therapy each appeared in 2; activity ideas, age-related toys, drinking skills, parental preference, and interference each appeared in 1. Tags overlap. All yes posts concerned two-year-olds (15) or three-year-olds (6). Infant posts in this share were largely about sleep, feeding amounts or milk, health, equipment, and parental recovery rather than the defined subject.

The repeated difficulty was responding to behaviour without exhausting or frightening everyone: biting, food-spitting, screaming, contradictory demands, and transitions. Several accounts described extensive attempts already made, including warnings, choices, emotion naming, sticker charts, and consequences. One author reported improvement after changing boundaries; another still found the usual suggestions ineffective. Both accounts and their replies are retained. Joy also came from ordinary companionship, affectionate toddler language, and successfully comforting a meltdown. Social confidence ranged from a cautious child avoiding physical challenges to a verbally advanced toddler bluntly rejecting playmates.

The 54 near posts comprise 32 over-age, 13 age-unknown, 4 mainly sleep, 2 other, and one each mainly parent state, logistics, and feeding. Explicit “four-year-old” descriptions without exact months were placed near, since they do not establish the birth-to-fourth-birthday window. Past age at potty training did not establish current age. Mixed-age families and the uncertain focal son in the forced-hugs account are flagged individually. The contact-nap account mainly concerns the two-month-old, with a four-year-old sibling’s behaviour also described.

Other judgment calls include illness with an explicit wish to play, party-theme comprehension, unsafe gifts with a toddler mouthing a bead, and generic recruitment or commercial posts. Pure symptom reports stayed no. Commercial memory-app copy is labelled in quote contexts; suspected promotional product anecdotes and disclosed prior AI formatting are labelled in the ledger. Emotional distress, aggression, identity conflicts, and other sensitive material were retained under the same rules. “Got” birthday equipment and therapy attendance were not treated as explicit payment claims.
