# Reader 027 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned posts in share order, including every archived author reply supplied in the post files. These are one share of the stated random, non-keyword sample from twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026. No assigned material remains unread. Counts describe these sampled posts, not parents or the wider market.

Marked 17 yes, 44 near and 89 no. Extracted 176 quotes from 33 posts: all 17 yes posts and the 16 near posts with an explicit link to development, skills, learning or physical growth. Paragraphs and sentence spans were selected after reading; no script classified the posts. Near reasons: 25 age_over_48, five age_unknown, three each feeding, parent_state, sleep and logistics, and two other.

Hard behaviour was the most repeated yes pattern (nine posts): hitting, running away, screaming, resisting care and sibling conflict. Potty learning appeared in four yes posts, often as apparent readiness or demonstrated ability followed by refusal, anxiety or accidents. Peer play/daycare and interference from others each appeared in three yes posts. Counts overlap. Two yes posts concerned fear about the parent's own response or teaching; one concerned rejection in favour of dad. There was one yes post each about cup use and established delay/early intervention. Most sampled posts instead concerned adult health, pregnancy, relationships, work, equipment or sleep without additional developmental content.

What surprised me was how little straightforward infant-milestone discussion appeared. Much of the useful material concerned toddlers' social behaviour and the parent's day. Joy also appeared: a three-year-old's imagined arrangements of belongings, and an infant's funny, parent-specific bodily-function habit. Older-child material included swimming ability, autism, gender identity, peer stereotypes, resilience at camp and understanding dental instructions.

Boundary calls are recorded per row. Four-year-olds were near because the brief ends at the fourth birthday. A generic preschool cohort described around three to four was yes, but has no single focal child. Toddler-only ages stayed unknown; nursing for over two years did not establish an exact age. Stimming suspicion without an explicit developmental link stayed near without quotes. Physical catch-up growth and losing teeth were treated as explicit growth links. Humorous elimination was treated as a funny habit rather than a medical post. Where motherhood was explicit but first/subsequent-child status unstated, I used the generic parent category. One child's reported statements were explicitly the author's paraphrases; the quote context preserves that provenance.

Checks passed: exact share coverage, unique sequential quote IDs, valid fields and limits, post/source-path membership, per-post quote totals, and every quote segment occurring verbatim in its raw file. Only the three requested output files were written; no network or git was used.
