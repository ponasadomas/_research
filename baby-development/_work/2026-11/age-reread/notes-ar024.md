# Reader 024 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned post files, including every author's reply present. No assigned post remains unread. Results: **14 yes, 36 near, 100 no; 88 quotes from 24 posts**. This is one share of the supplied random sample, not a measure of parents or buyers. In 115 files, replies were explicitly marked “not fetched”; those missing replies were unavailable, not silently skipped.

Among the 14 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 5, delight in 4, socialisation/daycare in 3, normality concerns in 2, age-related purchases in 2, and potty learning in 2. These are overlapping post counts. Screaming, refusing, controlling play and toilet refusal repeatedly left parents asking what else they could try. Practical attempts included choices, calm explanations, individual attention, playdates, coaching invitations and repeated toilet visits. Humour and pleasure also mattered: funny speech, an improvised song, building together and successful toilet learning.

What surprised me was how much fell outside the subject: 100 no posts, with many solely about sleep, illness, feeding quantities, adult health, relationships or equipment. All 14 yes posts concerned two- or three-year-olds. The near posts were not uniformly weak matches: 21 were over the age limit, including serious distress and skill-learning questions. Other borders were age unknown (8), parent state (3), logistics (2), sleep (1), and other (1). Only 3 yes/near authors explicitly reported buying or paying for something; merely using products, attending daycare or reading books did not establish payment.

Marking uncertainties: four years was placed beyond the fourth-birthday cutoff, even without months. In 1u2jhp2, “Terrible Threes” conflicts with “third year”; I used child_3_years and flagged it. 1wufd1y was yes because behavioural refusal was central and the author explicitly linked it to brain development; 1usmtat was near/other because vomiting was mainly medical but included pretend gagging. 1tksmau was near/sleep despite hitting and screaming. Incidental homeschooling, classes and play warranted some near marks; equipment alone did not. I used ties_to_development only for an author's stated learning, skills, milestones, diagnosis-related explanation or developmental connection; the boundary with ordinary emotional learning remains interpretive. In 1uuu1uv both children exceed the cutoff, but the daughter's exact age is unassigned.

All quote parts were checked verbatim against their assigned raw files; IDs, sample membership, one-row coverage and quote counts were checked by script. Comment quotes retain their comment date and permalink.
