# Reader 034 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned post files, including the original posters' archived replies, in share order. Nothing remains unread or unavailable. This is one assigned share of the supplied random sample of text posts from twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; I did not select posts by topic or keywords. The ledger contains 14 yes, 46 near and 90 no decisions. There are 152 quote records from 31 posts. Pattern counts are posts, not quote totals, and these are neither counts of people nor estimates of the wider market.

Among the 14 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in five, normality concerns in four, and potty learning in three. These overlap. Parents described climbing, whining, hitting, refusal, strategic behaviour and trouble completing familiar tasks independently. They wanted workable responses, reassurance about what is typical, and approaches that fit around a newborn or limited energy. Teaching guilt, recognised late speech, music lessons, restrictive Montessori corrections and suitable outdoor shoes also appeared. The tension between helping a child learn and making daily life manageable stood out.

The near posts often concerned older children's friendships, school participation, interests and behaviour. Other near posts placed concrete child behaviour or daily care within parents' exhaustion, feeding problems or practical arrangements. Many no posts were about pregnancy, postpartum health, relationships, illness, sleep alone or equipment without developmental content. An advertised financial-literacy app and general mental-health advocacy were retained with their provenance distinguished from personal child reports.

Age was the main uncertainty. I treated four-year-olds as outside the before-fourth-birthday scope; used older-age borders where puberty or school stage clearly established that boundary; and retained unknown stages when no numerical age was given. Mixed-age posts have explanatory notes. “6m and 9m” was read as boys aged six and nine from the school-age activities described; another “6M” was contrasted with a 13-month-old brother. Explicit age-related explanations, puberty, named skills and self-regulation counted as developmental links; ordinary guilt or a care routine alone did not. Purchased items were marked only where spending was stated.
