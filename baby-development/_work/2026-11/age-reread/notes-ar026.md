# Reader 026 — reading notes

Read all 150 assigned post files in full, including every archived author reply supplied. This is one share of the random sample described in the brief; these counts describe posts in this share, not people or market prevalence. No assigned post remains unread. Replies marked as not fetched were unavailable in the supplied files; image contents were not supplied.

Marked **13 yes, 33 near, 104 no**. Extracted **84 quotes from 26 posts**: all 13 yes posts and 13 near posts with an explicit connection to development or skills. All quote passages match their assigned raw files exactly; quote IDs are unique and every quoted post belongs to this share.

Among the 13 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in eight, other children/daycare in four, interference from others in four, normality concerns in three, and potty learning in two. These overlap. Potty posts contrasted success with prompting or at school against difficulty initiating, pooping, or cooperating at home. Behaviour posts included aggression toward animals and a parent, unwanted touching of peers, running, sibling jealousy, and sudden hitting or throwing. One mother described spanking, resentment, isolation and failed attempts at shared activities; those passages are retained in full. One post concerned a child with known autism and an impending sibling.

What surprised me was how few in-range posts directly concerned milestone timing, compared with conflicts about everyday conduct and independence. The normality questions included button fears, imagined wetness, and a babysitter’s expectations for writing, puzzles and attention. Two yes posts fit none of the numbered substantive patterns: access to snacks and pacifier weaning.

The 33 near posts comprise 24 older-child posts, four mainly about sleep, two mainly about the parent’s circumstances, two with unstated ages, and one mainly about feeding. Fourth-birthday cases were treated as reaching the end of the included range; the “just turned four” case is explicitly flagged. School grades establish older childhood without establishing an exact age, so those stages remain unknown. Mixed-age posts name the main child or explain the ambiguity.

Other borderline decisions: independent snack access is yes because autonomy is the main question; meal refusal with an underweight concern is near/feeding, despite an explicit decision-making link. Detailed crib climbing, distressed settling, sibling interaction and nighttime reading justify near/sleep; waking alone does not. A generic discipline maxim is near/age_unknown and labelled as such. Payment is not inferred merely from an app, babysitter, school or cinema visit. Much of the no material concerned pregnancy, adult health, relationships, sleep alone, equipment or acute child illness.
