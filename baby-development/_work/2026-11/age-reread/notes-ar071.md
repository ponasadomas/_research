# Reader 071 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned post files, including every supplied author's reply, in share order. Marks: **15 yes, 37 near, 98 no**. Pulled **112 quotes from 23 posts**. These are posts from the supervisor's random sample, not counts of people or estimates of the market. No assigned file remains unread. In 112 files, replies were explicitly not fetched; the other 38 give archived reply counts, sometimes partial. Those unavailable replies cannot be assessed.

Among the 15 yes posts, **11 show hard behaviour (3)**, **4 potty learning (11)**, **3 concern the parent's own impact (5)**, and **3 involve groups or other children (9)**. Known delay (12), delight (8), and interference (10) each occur in 2; normality concerns (1), daily activities (2), and eating skills (7) each occur in 1. Counts overlap. Parents repeatedly describe trying several disciplinary responses without lasting change, comparing children, and doubting their own competence. Four potty posts describe refusal, withholding, accidents, or lost progress. Two posts describe criticism from other adults about age-appropriate behaviour or discipline.

A surprise: all 15 yes posts principally concern children aged two or three. Infant posts in this share largely concern sleep, health, feeding amounts, equipment, or the parent's experience. The small wins mattered alongside difficulties: one parent celebrated a language-lagging child's account of stay-and-play; another described substantial early skills while struggling with hitting. One mother wanted speech-delay support but also lacked activity ideas and transport.

The 37 near posts comprise **25 age_over_48, 4 age_unknown, 3 parent_state, 2 feeding, 2 sleep, and 1 logistics**. I treated “four years old,” including “freshly 4,” as beyond the fourth birthday. Kindergarten alone did not establish age. First teeth with no age was a borderline developmental mention, retained as near. Ordinary waking and settling alone were no; learned thunder fear and explicit concern about naps and growth were near. The adult's retrospective early-puberty account was near/age_over_48 and quoted without omitting sensitive passages. Teaching, memory, organisation, independence and explicit age-related abilities counted as developmental links even without the word “development.” The potty “milestone” reference concerns another child. One author calls herself FTM but describes a baby brother; I used experienced_mother. A reported language lag was coded 12 without assuming a formal diagnosis.
