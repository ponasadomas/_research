# Reader 053: reading notes

Read all 150 assigned posts, including each file's archived replies by the original poster. This is one share of the supervisor's random sample of posts with text in twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; I made no keyword selection. No posts remain unread or unavailable.

**Results:** 14 yes, 36 near, 100 no. Cut 130 quotes from 23 posts: all 14 yes posts and the 9 near posts whose authors explicitly linked the subject to development, skills, learning, milestones or regression. Counts below count posts, not quotes; patterns overlap.

Among the 14 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 6, potty learning in 5, daycare/groups in 4, and normality or possible-condition questions in 2. Daily activities, choosing something for the age, delight, and established developmental support each appeared in 1. Potty problems often involved partial competence: knowing what to do without consistently doing it, different responses at school and home, or not wanting to interrupt play. Three posts centred on daycare treatment or separation distress. Morning independent play was a useful quieter counterpart to requests about conflict and distress.

The developmental-delay post was especially mixed: love, recognition of strengths, encouraging teacher reports and progress coexisted with comparison, avoidance of new challenges, self-blame and difficulty enjoying one-to-one time. These passages were retained. The funny speech post also shows delight without asking for reassurance or intervention. Many of the 100 no posts concerned adult health, relationships, employment, pregnancy, sleep alone, or medical care without developmental content.

**Boundaries:** 20 near posts were over the age limit; 4 centred on the parent's state, 2 logistics, 2 feeding, 2 sleep, 2 unstated ages and 4 other subjects. I treated an unqualified four-year-old as beyond the fourth birthday. School descriptions establish older children, but exact ages remain unknown. The work-regret post's only explicit ability detail concerns an older child's independence; the main child is the newborn. Pool supervision linked to future swimming competence, the milestone-planning spreadsheet, and teaching sharing within food etiquette were close calls retained as near. The spreadsheet and coding account describe self-created resources; commercial intent is unconfirmed.
