# Reader 049 — age reread

Read all 150 post files in the supplied share, including every archived author reply. This is one share of the random April–October 2026 sample of posts with text, not a keyword-selected set. No files were unavailable; none remain unread. Result: **17 yes, 38 near, 95 no; 107 quotes from 29 posts** (17 yes and 12 development-linked near).

Among the 17 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 8: nail-cutting resistance, screaming instead of speaking, whining, separation distress, class refusal and daycare disruption. Three posts involved potty learning, three classes/daycare, three relatives’ or staff interference, and three delight/pride. These overlap. Two concerned preference for a parent; one concerned a known condition. The warmest contrast to distress was a father describing his cerebral-palsy toddler’s empathy for his newborn sister. Another parent celebrated a three-year-old proudly showing visitors her accessible closet. Neither was discarded for being outside a problem-focused story.

The 38 near posts break down as 12 age_over_48, 12 age_unknown, 6 sleep, 4 logistics, 3 parent_state and 1 other. Pregnancy, adult relationships, medical symptoms and sleep-only questions accounted for much of the off-topic material. A commercial app-concept survey and a general behaviour-recording question were kept as near, with their non-parent-research provenance labelled; the survey’s proposed benefits are not buyer testimony.

Judgment calls: I treated “four years old” as outside the birth-to-fourth-birthday range; “about to turn four” remains yes. School/first-grade descriptions establish older children without establishing an exact stage. A carrier post states only the future travel age, so its present age is unknown. The BMI/leaning-out question is yes as a physical-growth question, though its overlap with health makes that boundary uncertain. Screening concerns are near/other despite explicit milestone discussion. Sleep-only waking and medical pain are no; sleep posts that describe independent behaviour, routines, or an explicit regression can be near. General “future issues” after a fall did not establish a developmental concern. References to an old regression still count as explicit links. Product possession alone was not treated as payment.

Counts are posts showing a pattern, never quote counts, and describe this share rather than market prevalence. Every quoted passage was checked against its assigned raw file; author replies use their own comment dates and links.
