# Reader 036 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned post files in full, including every author's reply present. No post was unavailable or left unread. Classification: **8 yes, 38 near, 104 no**. Cut **104 quotes from 21 posts**: all eight yes posts and thirteen near posts with an explicit developmental or skills connection. The supplied files omit fetching replies for 109 posts; their unseen replies are outside this reading.

All eight yes posts concern two- or three-year-olds (five aged two, three aged three). Three show hard behaviour, three social experiences or preschool, two normality questions, two activities or teaching at home, and two pride in skills; these overlap. Two fit none of the numbered substantive patterns: thumb-sucking with dental consequences and incessant why questions. One post concerns a known expressive speech delay and approaching therapy. Parents describe attempts before asking: reminders and bitter nail treatment, rewards and removing books, a calming corner, narration, reassurance, and outings. No yes post centres on learning to eat or potty learning.

Speech produced both delight and uncertainty: one parent celebrates a first two-word combination with a known delay; another wonders what remains to teach a child the doctor calls advanced. Another parent finds normal questioning intolerably overstimulating. Safety and independence also pull against each other: a friendly runner approaches strangers; another toddler rejects restraints to explore; a mother fears separating preschool friends caused distress despite teachers recommending independence.

The near group includes 22 posts outside the age boundary, six chiefly about parental state, five sleep, four unstated ages, and one event-etiquette complaint. Sleep-only and medical-only accounts remain no; a language explosion, established sleep skill, daytime aggression or equipment choice creates a relevant boundary case. Only one relevant post explicitly describes buying something: books from the bookstore.

Uncertainties: an age given simply as four was treated as at or beyond the fourth-birthday boundary, not assumed under four. Several posts cover children of different ages; the ledger identifies the primary child or notes the ambiguity. One rejection post uses “4m” but speech and school establish a four-year-old male. The mental-health-signs inquiry offers generalized examples rather than an identified child's experience; its quotes retain that provenance. Broad independence and safety-learning language counted as explicit skill connections; guilt alone did not.
