# Reader 006 — age reread

Read all 150 post files, including every archived author reply, in share order. This share comes from the supplied random sample of posts with text in twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; I used no keyword selection. Nothing remains unread in this share. Marks: **18 yes, 43 near, 89 no**. Extracted **278 quotes from 34 posts**: all 18 yes posts and 16 near posts with an explicit developmental or skills connection.

Among the 18 yes posts, concerns about normality (pattern 1), hard behaviour (3), and groups/other children/daycare (9) each appeared in **7 posts**. Delight or pride (8) appeared in **4**; activities (2) and stage-related purchases (4) in **3 each**. These overlap and count posts, not quotes. Speech appeared in **3 yes posts**: delight in longer sentences, concern about choppy fluency after EI declined services, and pronunciation worries during speech therapy. Intense tantrums, hitting, boundary testing and adults' responses recurred. Two yes posts involved potty learning; one concerned milk being accepted only in a bottle.

What surprised me was the contrast between rich activities and the parent's own distress: one mother described crafts and museum trips while feeling she was failing her daughter's learning. Another long thread showed how travel, temperament and a grandfather's expectations amplified a toddler's distress. That thread supplied **66 quotes**, and an older child's martial-arts gift thread supplied **43**. Their volume must not be mistaken for frequency across sampled posts.

Near reasons: **26 age_over_48, 7 sleep, 6 age_unknown, 2 parent_state, 1 logistics, 1 other**. The main uncertainty was the fourth-birthday boundary: posts saying only “four years old” were marked near and noted, because they do not establish an age within 48 months. Kindergarten timing was not used to invent an age. Retrospective childhood without stated ages stayed near. “Normal for this age” alone did not establish an explicit developmental link; skills, delays, attachment, resilience or named regression did. Sleep-only questions, acute illness/injury, adult relationships and feeding amounts were excluded unless they contained a distinct relevant description. The airy newborn cry was treated as a medical symptom. Product ownership or using a service alone was not treated as proof of payment.

Validation: exact share coverage, sequential unique quote IDs, post-to-source mapping and verbatim source matches checked by script. No network, git or other agents used; only the three requested output files written.
