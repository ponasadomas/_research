# Reader 073 — age reread

Read all 150 assigned post files, whole and in share order, including every author reply present. No posts remain unread in this share. Totals: **22 yes, 41 near, 87 no**. Cut **148 quotes from 35 posts**: all 22 yes posts and 13 near posts whose authors linked the subject to development or skills. These are post counts within the supplied random sample, not people or market prevalence. Some files explicitly say replies were not fetched; I read everything available without treating missing replies as evidence.

Among the 22 yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 8; activities and filling the day in 6; classes, peers or daycare in 6; choosing things for the stage in 5; fears about the effect of parenting choices in 4; delight in the child in 4; and normality questions in 3. Parent preference and potty learning appeared once each. Patterns overlap. There were no yes posts about eating skills, other people's interference, or a known diagnosis/therapy. The behaviour posts ranged from repeated refusal and family power struggles to sharing toys, joining group activities and bodily boundaries. Dividing attention between a toddler and a baby recurred across both eligible and adjacent posts.

The accounts were not uniformly worried. A parent described dramatic speech and broader learning progress after preschool; another described rewarding seasonal play at a cabin. A literacy professional writing as a parent worried both about holding a child back and about early reading leading to school boredom. Sensitive body-curiosity and puberty accounts were retained under the same rules as other subjects. Product use, book reading and class enrolment were not treated as proof of payment by themselves.

The 41 near posts comprised 21 over-age, 7 unknown-age, 5 parent-state, 3 other, 2 sleep, 2 feeding and 1 logistics. I treated an unqualified four-year-old as having reached the fourth birthday; exact boundary ages remain uncertain. First grade and elementary school did not supply a numeric age. With mixed ages I followed the central child, noting shared focus where needed. Nursery-photo privacy, sunburn after an outing and medication refusal were difficult incidental-subject boundaries. Bedtime resistance with repeated requests was near/sleep; sleep-only settling or waking was no. Explicit growth-spurt suggestions qualified for developmental ties; diagnosis or therapy alone did not. Age-normality, learned imitation and long-term parenting effects were interpretive ties, recorded conservatively but quoted where present. Digital content recommendations were treated as stage-appropriate choices under pattern 4. A settlement promotion was labelled commercial rather than treated as a personal account.

Validation: every assigned post has exactly one ledger row and there are no extra posts. Quote IDs are unique and sequential. Every quote part occurs verbatim in its stated raw file, and all quote records point to an assigned post. No network, git or other agents were used.
