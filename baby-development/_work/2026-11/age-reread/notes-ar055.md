# Reader 055 — age reread notes

Read all 150 assigned post files, including the author's archived replies, in share order. This share belongs to the random sample of text posts from twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; I did not select posts by topic. No assigned file is unread or unavailable. Replies not captured in the supplied files remain unavailable.

There are **21 yes, 42 near and 87 no posts**, with **154 quotes from 31 posts**: every yes post and the 10 near posts with an explicit developmental link. Counts below are posts, never quote counts, and overlapping patterns do not add to the number of posts.

Among yes posts, hard behaviour appeared in 10, potty learning in 6, questions about normality in 4, delight in 3, and worries about the parent's own actions in 3. Social settings appeared in 2; activities, age-related equipment, interference and known difficulties/evaluation each appeared in 1. Potty accounts repeatedly contrasted success naked at home with accidents, distraction or resistance elsewhere. One detailed account described a successful “poop party,” rewards and an apology after parental frustration. Other parents described retaliation, pinning down during changes, physical car-seat restraint, anger and financial loss; those passages are retained.

The 42 near posts comprise 28 beyond the fourth birthday, 4 with age unstated, and 2 each mainly about logistics, parental state, sleep, feeding or other matters. Older-child posts extended the material into friendship rejection, ADHD support, sensory regulation, unusual memory and growing independence. This surprised me alongside the small number of yes posts in a parenting sample.

Judgment calls: “almost/soon four” stays yes when otherwise on subject; “four years old” has reached the fourth birthday and is near. Grade-only and broad age-range posts have unknown stage. Several children without a single focus required a noted stage choice. Brief activity details within parental distress, dog fear, medicine refusal and bottle refusal were kept near. Pregnancy, sleep-only, nutrition-only, medical-only and adult-only accounts were no. Explicit growth, learning, skills, self-regulation or regression supported developmental ties; ordinary “is this normal?” wording alone did not. Payment was not inferred merely from attending care, using medication or owning equipment.
