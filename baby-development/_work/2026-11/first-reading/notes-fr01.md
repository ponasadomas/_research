Reader 01 — first reading, 18 November 2026

Read all 300 assigned post files in full, including archived author replies: 46 yes (15.3%), 71 near (23.7%), 183 no (61.0%). Extracted 872 quote records from the 117 yes/near posts. Unread or unavailable assigned files: 0. These are this reader’s share of the supplied 1,500-post random sample of text posts in twelve parenting subreddits, April–October 2026; no keywords selected this share. Counts describe sampled posts, not people, buyers or the wider market. Replies belong to their parent post and never increase a pattern’s post count.

I used yes for a direct concern about infant development, activities, understanding or connection. Near keeps adjacent concerns visible, especially sleep routines, feeding transitions, equipment readiness and adult difficulties that affect activities. Thirty-five near posts center on sleep/feeding timing, settling or sleep equipment; this is a deliberately broad boundary to revisit during the merge. General illness, nutrition, household administration and parental distress without a developmental/activity concern were no.

What recurred among the 117 yes/near posts, counted once per post using my quote themes (groups overlap):

- Motor skills, posture or physical growth: 24 posts (15 yes, 9 near). These ranged from rolling, sitting and walking to head control, low tone and stalled head growth. Parents compared observed abilities with age expectations, siblings, other babies and medical reassurance.
- Play, interaction or activities: 25 (15 yes, 10 near). The question was sometimes which toy or outing suited the age, sometimes how to engage a baby at all. Household participation, music, books, sensory classes and free outings appeared alongside toys. Geography, heat, transport, money and caring for two children constrained the options.
- Language, communication or understanding: 11 (6 yes, 5 near). Examples included what to say to a five-month-old, whether a child understands a limit or goodbye, and questions about language/social delay.
- Feeding as learning or transition: 10 (6 yes, 4 near). Independence, chewing, textures, gagging, practice opportunities and readiness mattered. This differs from the many no posts about milk supply, bottle compatibility, quantities or gastrointestinal illness.
- Parental connection or affection: 18 (9 yes, 9 near). Preference for another caregiver, missing cuddles, enjoying contact and feeling connected through new skills all appeared. Competence, guilt, doing enough or perfection pressure appeared in 25 posts (11 yes, 14 near).

I was struck by how often a skill changed the parent’s emotional experience: one author felt maternal joy through cheering new abilities after newborn dread; another suspected sudden independent sitting explained a week of fussing. One mother worried that constant entertainment caused dependence; another worried that practical household learning was insufficient compared with toy learning online. New abilities were both welcome and disruptive. These are distinct accounts, not evidence that either explanation is correct.

Of the 183 no posts, 52 centered on children explicitly older than 24 months. Other frequent subjects were work and childcare logistics, couple conflict, parental physical/mental health and identity, lactation, acute child illness, equipment, clothing and money. Distress was not a reason to exclude: relevant self-directed dark thoughts remain in the quotes from 1v07vgl; the severe abuse/self-harm account 1wabg15 was no because its subject was the parent’s safety and mental health.

Uncertainties remain explicit in each ledger row. Fifteen near posts have unknown stage, including five describing only a two-year-old, who may exceed 24 months. Five yes posts clearly discuss a baby’s development/activity but give no numeric age; stage stays unknown. In 1u7fl9r and similar cases I did not treat changing sleep alone as proof of a developmental cause. In 1u87zpc the three-month-old is the sibling, not the potty-training child. Four posts state chronological and corrected ages; both are preserved and stage uses corrected age (1to7b9u, 1vdzecb, 1vo9c8s, 1x5mtr9).
