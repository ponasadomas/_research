## What do I do with the child all day?
@pattern 02

### One post in twelve asks what to do with the child all day
@count pattern

Under 2: {U2} posts ({U2P}). At 2 and 3: {A23} ({A23P}). Under 2 parents do not know how to fill a baby's day and fear it is not enough; at 2 and 3 the child demands play the parent cannot keep up.

- "I wake up thinking I've got a plan and then somehow it’s 10:30 and I'm out of ideas" [sr020-0071]
- "I am so tired of pretending to be Ursula or an evil step mother." [ar032-0071]

**But:** for some the days are easy.

@run u2

### Under 2, parents run out of things to do, fear the day is not enough for development, and want a plan that tells them what to do
@forms u2
Parents run out of ideas for ordinary days.
Parents exchange play ideas built around children’s interests.
Parents keep switching activities to prevent fussing.
Parents need alternatives when going outside is difficult.
Parents worry their activities are not enough for development.
Parents want a manageable daily routine.
Parents question how much attention and independence to allow.
Parents want music and screens they can enjoy too.
Parents want safe exploration without constant hovering.
Parents want activities that match their child’s energy.
Parents want reading to fit their child.
Parents question which toys and plans hold attention.
@end

Of the 133, 15 fear the day is not enough for development and 10 ask for a routine.

- "Is it possible to play with him yet or is he too young?" [sr016-0043]
- "I’d like to follow just one account or purchase one “program” or something that would just tell me what to do!" [sr036-0107]

**But:** ordinary things can do. "I give him the salad spinner on the kitchen floor." [sr008-0164]

@end

**But:** the energy for it can run out.

### Under 2, parents need the child occupied so they can work, wash or rest, and some say the days bore them
@forms u2

**But:** some manage it around naps.

@run a23

### At 2 and 3, familiar play no longer fills the day, and parents ask for fresh activities, shows and ways to teach
@forms a23
They need fresh activities because familiar play no longer fills the day.
They compare daily routines and ask whether they are doing enough.
They ask which shows, songs or background media fit life with their child.
They want to support the child’s learning and reading in the right way.
They want to replace screen time with activities they can sustain.
They want to involve the child in chores without making every task harder.
They ask whether pretend fighting should be joined in or redirected.
Other posts concern a promised outing, a free app and fictional toddler humor.
@end

One of the 33 is a creator promoting a free activity app, and one is written as fiction.

- "Should I be doing more activities? More structured activities?" [ar065-0118]
- "It's 92 degrees, so our playing outside window is closed." [ar030-0029]

**But:** old play can still absorb the child.

### At 2 and 3, the child wants the parent in every game, and parents want an hour to themselves
@forms a23
They need the child occupied during necessary tasks and rest.
They struggle with constant demands to join or direct play.
They struggle to sustain play and want time for themselves.
They need play that accommodates physical limits.
They need to keep the older child engaged while caring for a baby.
They question when to interrupt or stop offering quiet time.
@end

- "I want an hour or so of just silence to myself." [ar020-0088]
- "But playing with my toddler often really bores me." [ar007-0077]

**But:** the same parents say they love being with the child.

### At 2 and 3, parents look for outings, rituals and shared activities the child can manage
@forms a23

@end
