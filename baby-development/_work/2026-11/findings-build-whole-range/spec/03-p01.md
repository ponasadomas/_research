## Is this normal, or is something wrong with my child?
@pattern 01

### One post in five asks whether what the child does, or does not yet do, is normal
@count pattern

Under 2: {U2} posts ({U2P}). At 2 and 3: {A23} ({A23P}). Under 2 the question is mostly a milestone or the body; at 2 and 3 it is behaviour, and a named condition.

- "Every interaction feels like I’m doing an evaluation and I need to stop." [sr003-0184]
- "It seems like there is a huge discrepancy between the things he can and can't do." [ar048-0118]

**But:** some ask without alarm.

@run u2

### Under 2, parents ask whether rolling, walking or talking is late, what counts as a word, and what to expect at this age
@forms u2
Parents ask whether movement and practical skills are late.
Parents question what counts as enough speech.
Parents need clearer expectations of their child.
Parents worry when sounds or skills disappear.
Parents seek reassurance while movement skills emerge.
@end

- "I just don’t know what’s developmentally appropriate at this age." [sr072-0053]
- "I'm wondering if it counts as a word, or a gesture." [sr040-0019]

**But:** some know the range and are not worried.

### Under 2, parents question how the child responds to people: eye contact, answering sounds, too clingy or too content alone, odd play
@forms u2
Parents question their child’s attention and social responses.
Parents question too much or too little closeness.
Parents question unusual play, attention and habits.
@end

- "But I can’t make noises at her and then she makes noises back." [sr099-0110]
- "Perfectly ok being alone in her crib and she also gets mad if you hold her too long." [sr037-0006]

**But:** the same child often has other strengths.

### Under 2, parents ask whether a movement, a head shape, growth or late teeth is a sign of something
@forms u2
Parents question whether unusual movements signal problems.
Parents question growth, appearance and tooth development.
@end

- "Do these seem like infantile spasms?" [sr125-0020]
- "Then out of nowhere she started tip toe walking!!" [sr008-0081]

**But:** some take the doctor's word.

### Under 2, parents ask whether this much crying, or tantrums this early, is normal and will pass
@forms u2
Parents ask whether persistent crying will pass.
Parents question normal limits for tantrums and roughness.
@end

- "What troubles me the most is that I can’t figure out what’s wrong." [sr039-0085]
- "Isn't it too early for tantrums?" [sr086-0113]

**But:** it can pass, as a phase.

### Under 2, smaller forms of "is this normal": comfort habits, fears, crying at bath or nappy time, several signs added up
@small u2
Parents want comfort and exploration without injury. => thumb sucking, genital touching, comfort habits
Parents ask whether fears and sensitivities fade. => fears and sensitivities
Parents question crying during particular care tasks. => crying at baths or changes
Parents combine observations into wider developmental fears. => several signs added into one fear
Parents question loud or unfamiliar vocal sounds. => screeching and growling
A parent worries about food spitting and weight loss. => food spat out
@end

- "Could these things indicate a bigger problem?" [sr088-0135]

**But:** nothing found against it.

@run a23

### At 2 and 3, parents set the child's strengths against signs of autism, ADHD or a speech delay, often after a teacher, relative or doctor raised it
@forms a23
The parent struggles to reconcile strengths and developmental concerns.
The parent worries about joining in and speaking socially.
The parent asks whether communication meets expectations.
The parent questions another adult’s expectations of the child.
The parent asks whether movement and self-care should be easier.
The parent asks whether changed or choppy speech will settle.
@end

- "He knows hundreds of words but uses them mostly to label or repeat." [ar008-0068]
- "To be told it might need more investigation was a gut punch to say the least and now we’re questioning everything." [ar032-0099]

**But:** home and school can describe a different child.

### At 2 and 3, parents ask whether tantrums, aggression, restlessness or coldness go beyond the usual toddler stage
@forms a23
The parent asks whether intense tantrums exceed the usual toddler stage.
The parent worries about what the child’s emotional responses mean.
The parent asks whether activity or inattention needs investigation.
The parent wants help stopping injury or damage.
The parent fears a new baby has made the toddler’s behaviour worse.
@end

- "My arms are usually covered in scabs from him gouging me with his nails, and bruises from his bites." [ar033-0069]
- "The teacher has said he can’t “safely participate” in any activity without 1 on 1 supervision." [ar081-0132]

**But:** the worst of it can be rare.

### At 2 and 3, parents ask whether a habit, a fixed interest, a fear, a dislike of clothes or noise, or clinging will pass
@forms a23
The parent wonders whether a repeated habit or intense interest will pass.
The parent asks how to handle distress over sensations or routine changes.
The parent questions the child’s need for closeness.
The parent wants help understanding new fears.
The parent wants body boundaries without shame.
@end

- "The mere suggestion of shorts upsets her." [ar061-0038]
- "I have twin boy toddlers who are obsessed with System of a Down." [ar038-0077]

**But:** the worry can be one thing only.

### At 2 and 3, smaller forms of "is this normal": the body, growth, running off to strangers
@small a23
The parent asks whether a bodily change is developmental or needs care. => vomiting, tiredness, teeth, eyes, falls
The parent worries that growth measurements signal a problem. => too small or too big
The parent wants safe independence around strangers. => running off; going up to strangers
Other: The parent wonders how a toddler remembered nursing. => how a 2-year-old remembered how to nurse
@end

Four of these 21 posts about the body and growth are about children under 2.

- "My son is 3 years and 3 months old and he's 3 foot 7." [ar024-0035]

**But:** nothing found against it.

### Most who ask "is this normal" have already practised, changed the care or had it checked
