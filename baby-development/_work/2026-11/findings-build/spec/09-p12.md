
## The child has a known delay or is in therapy
@pattern 12

### About one post in twenty-five is from a parent whose child already has a named delay, a diagnosis or therapy
@count pattern

- "But no words. Not even mama or dada." [sr018-0020]
- "I feel a little lost and am unsure if I’m actually helping." [sr050-0008]

**But:** some report that early intervention helped a lot.

### Parents want the exercises, stretches or helmet to work at home, and to hear that others caught up
@forms
Parents need workable movement and positioning practice at home.
Parents want stories of delayed movement eventually improving.
Parents want clearer instructions and reassurance about torticollis.
Parents question helmet necessity and worry about wearing one.
@end

- "She is in physical therapy but it hasn’t helped." [sr070-0089]
- "Has anyone else’s kid done this and when did they walk?" [sr020-0024]
- "All memories I make with him at this age will include the helmet." [sr049-0012]

**But:** others report improvement with therapy.

### Parents ask what the delay means: autism, a score they do not recognise, an unknown future
@forms
Parents notice additional missing skills during existing care.
Parents wonder whether delays and other behaviours mean autism.
Parents find assessment results hard to reconcile with home life.
Parents face uncertain futures after serious medical news.
@end

- "Is arm flapping always an autism sign?" [sr006-0031]
- "Good outcomes, bad outcomes, somewhere in between — I want to hear all of it." [sr086-0103]

**But:** a professional may see no cause for alarm.

### Parents wait for speech and ask what speech therapy is doing
@forms
Parents wait for delayed speech or understanding to catch up.
Parents want more ways to encourage speech and gestures.
Parents question what speech therapy is accomplishing.
Parents ask whether hearing or oral conditions explain delayed speech.
@end

- "He has completed eight speech therapy sessions (45 minutes each), once a week, but I don't see any progress whatsoever." [sr001-0128]
- "I don’t think she really understands anything we say." [sr037-0074]

**But:** words can come fast. "Now it’s been two months, and he’s saying SO many new words!" [sr093-0056]

### Parents blame themselves, are blamed, and hurt when they see other children
@forms
Parents blame themselves or fear blame for the delay.
Parents find comparisons with other children painful.
Parents want care and friendships to include their child.
@end

- "I am wracked with guilt that I didn’t talk to her “right” or “enough”." [sr074-0088]
- "I just want to make sure he has friends in this life." [sr076-0059]

**But:** the child may be happy in the moment.

### Smaller forms: feeding, getting therapy, and separate medical worries
@small
Parents need solids to become a workable source of nutrition. => solids
Parents need milk feeding to work despite oral difficulties. => milk feeds
Parents struggle to obtain or keep therapy. => getting or keeping therapy
A parent wants their caregiving role respected. => a mother with DID, whose son is the result of sexual assault, overridden by relatives
Other concerns leave parents seeking explanations or next steps. => other medical worries
@end

- "She’s now latching it seems, but drinks no milk." [sr014-0115]
- "We’ve done both osteo and chiro with limited success and are out of $$ for more sessions." [sr115-0150]

**But:** small steps are reported.

### All but two have already tried home practice or professional treatment, often several kinds
@across Parents have already tried home practice and professional treatment.

- "Tongue/lip tie release, amino acid formula, reflux meds, GI doctor, weekly OT and chiropractor, craniosacral therapy…" [sr055-0127]

**But:** some stop a treatment. "I stopped after three sessions because the approach felt rough and my baby cried throughout, which made me uncomfortable." [sr108-0071]

### Specialists give opposite opinions
@across Parents receive conflicting advice and sometimes feel undermined.

- "We’ve seen two specialists and gotten completely different opinions: the neurologist suspects autism, while the neuropsychologist thinks it’s more likely just a developmental delay that he’ll catch up from." [sr044-0016]

**But:** some feel well cared for, as by a hospice team.

### Where money is mentioned it is therapy, a helmet and equipment, and it strains the budget
@across Parents report paying for therapy, equipment and helmets.

- "Even with copay being $35 for a once a week session, it’s really stretching things." [sr026-0066]

**But:** they will pay and distrust the seller. "We’re willing to do it and pay for it if it’s necessary (insurance won’t cover it) but it’s hard to make the best decision when you know they just want money from you." [sr078-0090]

### Parents say they feel terrified, guilty and helpless
@across Parents describe fear, grief and helplessness alongside love.

- "I love my little boy so damn much but it wrecks me because I don’t know how to make this better." [sr026-0063]
- "I’m terrified it’s in my genetics and I never should have had a baby." [sr062-0054]

**But:** hope is said too, and feared.
