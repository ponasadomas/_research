# Reader 01 notes

Read all 150 assigned post files, including their authors’ archived replies, in share order. Nothing remains unread. This is the assigned share of the supervisor’s random sample, not a fresh sample or a measure of parents or buyers. The supplied posts run from 1 April to 8 July 2026; some replies are later.

Marks: **22 yes, 99 near, 29 no**. Extracted **448 quotes from 47 posts**: every yes post and the 25 near posts explicitly mentioning development, skills, milestones or regression. Counts below count posts, never quotes; a post can show several patterns.

Among the 22 yes posts, six show pattern 1 (normality or concern), five each show daily activities, age-appropriate purchases, and delight/pride, and four show hard behaviour. Sleep dominates the borders: 25 near posts are marked sleep, compared with 22 parent_state, 17 age_over_24, 15 logistics, ten feeding, six other and four age_unknown. Exhaustion, uncertainty about doing enough, and attempts that work only briefly recur across different subjects. The 29 no posts include adult health, school-age problems, work, finances and unrelated discussion.

What surprised me was the coexistence of pride and doubt. One mother worries about missing educational toy play while her toddler helps with household tasks and dresses herself. A parent initially describing relentless fussing reports that it stopped after the baby sat up independently. The head-growth thread eventually reports a normal scan and resumed growth. I kept those updates with their own reply dates and links. Payment was distinguished from ownership: a quoted scan price, gifted instruments, or using an app did not establish that the author paid.

The hardest boundaries were medical posts that mention normal milestones or mobility, and sleep posts that explicitly call the problem a regression. I retained those as near when health or sleep remained the main subject, extracting their quotes when the developmental link was explicit. Cataract care is near/other rather than a known developmental delay. “Two years old” without months stays near/age_over_24. Unknown ages were not supplied from subreddit names. For authors who explicitly identify as mothers but give no first-time or previous-child evidence, I used parent_unspecified and noted that limitation where needed. Posts about substantially older children that do not touch infant/toddler development were no; adjacent toddler behaviour or learning was near. Possible promotional framing and reported AI advice are labelled in the ledger.

Validation: exact one-to-one share coverage; unique sequential quote IDs; source-file/post membership; every quote checked verbatim against its raw file; quote totals and required-field/word-limit checks passed. No network, git, delegation, or writes outside the three requested outputs.
