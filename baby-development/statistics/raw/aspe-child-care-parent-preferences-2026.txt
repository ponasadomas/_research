          Most Parents of Young Children Prefer Either
         Parental Care or a Home-Based Setting, Though
                  Preferences Vary by Child Age
                                      Jessica Rendon and Alayna Schreier

Care patterns for young children differ substantially by age: parental and home-based arrangements are most
common among infants and toddlers, while center-based care is most common among preschool-age
children.5 While this mirrors the findings about care preferences at a high level, the share of parents actually in
these types of arrangements varies from their stated preferences.

Our analysis of the 2024 NSECE data found that about 22 percent of households with a child under age five use
only parental care, lower than the 35 percent of parents who report they would most want parental care if
cost and location were not barriers. Other published estimates on children’s participation in child care show
that among children under age three, 53 percent received regular nonparental care in 2024.6 Unpaid care from
an individual other than a parent was the most common type (24 percent), followed by center-based care (18
percent). Among children ages three through five, 69 percent received regular nonparental care. Center-based
care was the most common type (42 percent), followed by unpaid individual care (27 percent).
