# Then-vs-now development facts (verified 2026-11-10)

Facts behind the "catching up" block on the quiz landing page. Pirmi's page builds its problem and agitation on one story: babies develop later than they used to, and the standards were lowered instead of the knowledge raised. These are the primary-source versions of each claim. Use only the numbers below; Pirmi's "5x more time in bouncers" and "6–8 weeks average delay" have no source we could find.

## Back to Sleep

- The US Back to Sleep campaign launched in 1994 (NICHD with the AAP). SIDS fell from 1.17 per 1,000 live births in 1993 to 0.62 in 2000, about 50%.
  Source: NICHD, https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/releases/sleep-tenthann ; https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/resources/spotlight/100312-safe-to-sleep
- Back sleeping is correct and must be followed. Copy must say so whenever it mentions the campaign.

## Back sleeping and motor timing

- Majnemer A, Barr RG. Association between sleep position and early motor development. J Pediatr 2006;149(5):623–629. PMID 17095331.
  Abstract: "Motor delays were documented in 22% of babies sleeping supine" at 6 months (supine n=50, prone n=22). "Prone sleep-positioned infants were more likely to sit and roll." "Daily exposure to awake prone positioning was predictive of motor performance in infants sleeping supine." "At 15 months, sleep position continued to predict motor performance."
  Note: small prone group. Quote as "one study found", not as a population rate.
- Davis BE et al. Effects of sleep position on infant motor development. Pediatrics 1998;101(6):1135–1140. 351 infants. Prone sleepers rolled, sat (tripod), crawled and pulled to stand earlier; no difference in age of walking; all within normal range. (From secondary summaries; abstract not re-fetched.)

## Time in seats

- Callahan CW, Sisler C. Use of seating devices in infants too young to sit. Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med 1997;151(3):233–235. 187 infants; mean 5.7 hours per day in a sitting device (range 0–16).
  Confirmed via Liaw P, Moon RY, Han A, Colvin JD. Infant Deaths in Sitting Devices. Pediatrics 2019;144(1):e20182576, which cites it as reference 3 with that figure.
  Note: 1997, US sample. Say "one study found", not "the average baby today".
- Malachowski L, … Humphreys K. Infant and Child Development 2023, doi 10.1002/icd.2405. 60 infants aged 4–6 months. Infants who spent the most time in seating devices heard nearly 40% fewer adult words per day than those who spent the least. Source: https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2023/03/02/infant-seating-devices-may-reduce-language-exposure/

## CDC 2022 milestone revision

- Zubler JM et al. Evidence-Informed Milestones for Developmental Surveillance Tools. Pediatrics 2022;149(3):e2021052138. Author manuscript: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/122643/cdc_122643_DS1.pdf
  - Old lists used the 50th percentile; new lists place a milestone "at the age most (≥75%) children would be expected to demonstrate" it.
  - 216 milestones across 10 checklists became 159 across 12. One third of the 94 retained milestones moved age; 21 of those 31 moved to an older age.
  - New placements: "Takes a few steps on his own" at 15 months. "Walks without holding onto anyone or anything" at 18 months. "Walks, holding onto furniture" at 12 months.
  - Crawling does not appear on the new checklists.
- Old CDC 1-year checklist (Wayback snapshot 2021-05-13 of cdc.gov/ncbddd/actearly/milestones/milestones-1yr.html): Movement, "May take a few steps without holding on", "May stand alone". Act-early warning sign: "Doesn't crawl".
  So "walking alone" went from the 12-month list to the 18-month list, and crawling went from a warning sign to absent.

  Condition name. Kept here for the FAQ or for a reply if asked, not for headlines or cards.

## Weak or unverified (do not use)

- APTA 2008 press release: two-thirds of 400 therapists reported more early motor delays over six years. Survey of opinion, not measurement.
- "30% of parents meet the tummy time guideline": repeated in summaries of Hewitt 2020 (Pediatrics, doi 10.1542/peds.2019-2168) but the underlying study was not found.
- Pirmi's "5x more time in bouncers than 30 years ago" and "6–8 weeks average motor delay": no source found.

## The one scary figure (added 2026-11-10, used in the Problem line)

- Majnemer A, Barr RG. Influence of supine sleep positioning on early motor milestone acquisition. Dev Med Child Neurol 2005;47:370–376. doi 10.1111/j.1469-8749.2005.tb01156.x.
  Abstract and Table III: "Only 22% of 6-month-olds could sit without arm support versus 50% expected in a normative sample." Normative data are the Alberta Infant Motor Scale norms (Piper and Darrah 1994, collected in Alberta around 1990–1992, before back sleeping became the norm). Same study: 22% of the 6-month-olds had gross motor delay (PDMS GMQ <78); 32.7% of 4-month-olds were never placed on their tummy awake.
  Copy wording: "In the early 1990s, half of all babies could sit up on their own at six months. In a 2005 study, only one in five could." Sample is 50 Montreal infants, so say "a study", never "babies today" as a population rate.

## Old list vs new list, per milestone (for the block visual; verified 2026-11-10)

| Milestone | Old CDC checklist (2004–2021 wording) | 2022 checklist (Zubler Table) |
|---|---|---|
| Rolls | 4 mo: "May be able to roll over from tummy to back". 6 mo: "Rolls over in both directions" | 6 mo: "Rolls from tummy to back" |
| Sits | 6 mo: "Begins to sit without support" | 9 mo: "Sits without support" |
| Crawls | 6 mo: "Rocks back and forth, sometimes crawling backward before moving forward". 1 yr act-early sign: "Doesn't crawl" | not on any checklist |
| Walks alone | 1 yr: "May take a few steps without holding on", "May stand alone" | 15 mo: "Takes a few steps on his own". 18 mo: "Walks without holding onto anyone or anything" |

Old wording from 2020 copies of the CDC 4- and 6-month checklists (RUHealth.org, credited to AAP Caring for Your Baby and Young Child, 2009) and the 2021-05-13 Wayback copy of the CDC 1-year page. Children's Mercy (2022-03) confirms sitting moved 6 → 9 months. The old 9-month page ("Sits without support", "Crawls") could not be re-fetched today; Wayback blocked the request. Verify before quoting the 9-month line itself.

## Long-run outcome studies (added 2026-11-10; use with care, see notes)

- Flensborg-Madsen T, Mortensen EL. Infant developmental milestones and adult intelligence: a 34-year follow-up. Early Hum Dev 2015. PMID 25981493. Copenhagen Perinatal Cohort, 9,125 infants, 1,155 tested at 20–34 (WAIS). Later milestones → lower IQ, mostly Performance IQ. Correlations small (r < 0.10). Held after confounders. Stronger in offspring of lower social status parents.
- Flensborg-Madsen T et al. Early motor developmental milestones and personality traits in midlife: a 50-year follow-up. Children 2023;10(4):718. PMID 37189967. 8,395 infants, 1,307 with NEO-FFI at mean age 50.1. Slower milestones → higher neuroticism, lower conscientiousness. 12 milestones explain 2.4% of variance in neuroticism, 3.2% in conscientiousness. Held after family, perinatal covariates and adult IQ.
- Walle EA, Campos JJ. Infant language development is related to the acquisition of walking. Dev Psychol 2014. PMID 23750505. N=44 longitudinal; walking onset → significant jump in receptive and productive language, independent of age; replicated in study 2.
- Libertus K, Violi DA. Sit to talk. Front Psychol 2016. PMID 27065934. N=29; emergence of sitting correlated with receptive vocabulary at 10 and 14 months.
- Fernald A, Marchman VA, Weisleder A. Dev Sci 2013. PMC3582035. SES gap in vocabulary and processing visible at 18 months; six-month gap by 24 months.
- Adolph KE et al. 2012: 12–19-month-olds average 2,368 steps and 17 falls per hour of free play.

Copy rules for these: say "linked" or "followed … found", never "causes". Effects are small per child; the honest claim is "it does not wash out", not "your baby will be X". Never mention the psychopathology/schizophrenia framing from the 2023 abstract; brief §8.

## Why the CDC moved to the 75th percentile (Zubler 2022, verbatim, added 2026-11-10)

- "The strategy (≥75%) was chosen to support clinical judgment regarding performing additional developmental screenings … This strategy may also prevent a wait-and-see approach because most children of the same age would be expected to achieve the milestone."
- "To reduce confusion about when to be concerned, developmental warning signs were eliminated …"
- "SMEs believed that using 50th percentile milestones for surveillance would not support clinical decision-making for developmental screening because only half of children would be expected to achieve an individual milestone by a given age. Using milestones that 85% or 90% of children would be expected to achieve may limit opportunities for additional screening for too many children at risk …"

What this supports in copy: the list was moved to the point where one baby in four misses a milestone instead of one in two, and the stated aim was clearer decisions about who gets screened and less confusion about when to worry. What it does not say: that babies got slower, or that the aim was fewer visits. "Fewer flags, fewer worried mums in the waiting room" is our reading of the consequence, phrased as consequence, not as their motive.
